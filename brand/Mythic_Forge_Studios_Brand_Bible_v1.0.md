## 1. Brand Philosophy **[LOCKED]**

**Mission and Vision:** Mythic Forge Studios' Mission and Vision Statements are established, Locked facts of the company — Studio OS Section 2 (Mission Statement) and Section 1 (Vision Statement). This Bible does not restate them; it exists so that everything the audience sees *looks* like it was made by the company those sections describe.

**Purpose:** To give every visual and verbal touchpoint of the studio — outside the fiction itself — the same craft, restraint, and internal consistency the studio demands of its stories. A viewer should be able to recognize a Mythic Forge Studios post, thumbnail, or product on sight, the same way they'd recognize a shot from the show.

**Core Values:** Inherited directly from Studio OS Section 3 (Originality, Craft, Consistency, Patience, Transparency, Adaptability) — this Bible does not define a second set of studio values for branding specifically. A branding decision that would violate one of those six values is rejected on that basis alone.

**Creative Principles (brand-specific application of the values above):**
1. **Restraint over spectacle.** The brand earns attention the same way the story earns power — through consistency and craft, never through volume or hype.
2. **One identity, every surface.** A viewer should never be able to tell whether they're looking at the channel banner, a T-shirt, or a Discord icon — the same marks, colors, and voice govern all of them.
3. **The brand echoes the fiction; it never explains it.** Where this Bible's visual choices reference *The Fracture Protocol* (Section 6, Section 7), they do so structurally — through palette and mood — never by depicting or describing story content.

**Brand Personality:** Governed by Studio OS Section 4 in full — this Bible does not define a competing personality table. The one-line summary remains: *cinematic, sincere, and a little mythic — never ironic about its own world* (Studio OS Section 4).

**Brand Promise:** Every piece of official Mythic Forge Studios media — however small, however far from the flagship show — was made with the same care as the show itself. Nothing is a throwaway asset.

**Long-Term Vision:** As the studio grows from one show into a multi-format company (Studio OS Section 28, Phased Roadmap), this brand identity is built to scale to a second flagship IP, a second format, and a second platform without a redesign — see Section 16 (Future Expansion).

## 3. Logo System **[LOCKED]**

**Design concept:** the mark pairs the studio's two operative words visually — a minimalist **anvil silhouette** (forging: original creation, made by hand) bisected by a single **fracture line** (the studio's flagship creative signature) rendered in the brand's accent color. The mark depicts no character, creature, or in-fiction object — it is an abstract studio emblem, not a piece of key art.

| Variant | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Primary Logo** | Icon + wordmark lockup: anvil-and-fracture icon at left, "MYTHIC FORGE" set large in Space Grotesk Bold, "STUDIOS" set smaller and letter-spaced beneath it in Space Grotesk Regular |
| **Secondary Logo** | Wordmark only ("MYTHIC FORGE STUDIOS," single line, Space Grotesk Bold) — for contexts too narrow or too small for the icon to read cleanly |
| **Horizontal Logo** | Icon left, full wordmark right, single baseline — for banners and wide headers |
| **Vertical (Stacked) Logo** | Icon centered above the two-line wordmark — for square or portrait placements (profile pictures, app icons at larger sizes) |
| **Icon Version** | Anvil-and-fracture mark alone, no wordmark — for favicons, app icons, watermarks, and social avatars |
| **Monochrome Version** | Single-color rendering (pure Void Black or pure Authority White, Section 4) for engraving, embossing, or single-color print where the accent color cannot reproduce |
| **Dark Version** | Full-color lockup (Authority White wordmark, Fracture Cyan crack accent) on a Void Black or near-black field — the primary, default usage |
| **Light Version** | Void Black wordmark on an Authority White field, with the Fracture Cyan crack accent retained (the accent is the one color that must always survive, dark or light background) |

**Clear Space Rules:** minimum clear space on every side of any lockup equals the height of the icon's anvil silhouette within that lockup — no other graphic element, text, or edge of frame may enter that space.

**Minimum Size:** Icon alone: 24px height (digital), 0.25in (print). Full primary lockup: 120px width (digital), 0.75in width (print). Below these sizes, use the icon-only version.

**Improper Usage:** never recolor the fracture accent to a non-brand color; never separate the icon from the wordmark in the primary lockup (use the designated Secondary or Icon-only variants instead of manually deconstructing the Primary); never stretch, skew, or rotate any variant; never add drop shadows, bevels, outer glows, or gradients beyond what's specified here; never place a full-color variant on a busy or low-contrast background without a solid-color safe backing plate.

**Logo Consistency Rules:** exactly one Primary Logo file per color mode (dark/light) is ever "current" at a time — a revision is a new version (Section 14), not a second simultaneous variant. Every use of the logo across every surface (Sections 10–13) pulls from the same current asset, never a recreated or eyeballed redraw.

## 4. Color System **[LOCKED]**

| Name | Hex | RGB | HSL | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fracture Cyan** | `#00E5FF` | 0, 229, 255 | 189°, 100%, 50% | Primary accent — CTAs, links, the logo's fracture line, highlights only. Never decorative or used as a large fill; earned the same way the show's own cyan is earned (Art Bible `color-language.md`), reserved for what matters. |
| **Void Black** | `#0A0B0D` | 10, 11, 13 | 220°, 13%, 5% | Primary dark background / primary text on light backgrounds |
| **Authority White** | `#F5F7FA` | 245, 247, 250 | 216°, 33%, 97% | Primary light background / primary text on dark backgrounds |
| **Charcoal Steel** | `#2A2E35` | 42, 46, 53 | 220°, 12%, 19% | Secondary — panels, cards, dividers on dark backgrounds |
| **Signal Blue** | `#1E3A5F` | 30, 58, 95 | 209°, 52%, 25% | Accent — secondary highlight, used sparingly, never in place of Fracture Cyan for primary CTAs |

**Neutral Palette (grayscale ramp, for UI/web use):**

| Step | Hex |
|---|---|
| Steel-50 | `#F5F7FA` (= Authority White) |
| Steel-100 | `#E4E7EB` |
| Steel-300 | `#9AA5B1` |
| Steel-500 | `#66707A` |
| Steel-700 | `#3E4751` |
| Steel-900 | `#0A0B0D` (= Void Black) |

**Background Colors:** Void Black is the default background across all digital surfaces (YouTube, website, Discord). Authority White is the designated light-mode alternative — never a random off-white, always this exact value.

**Text Colors:** Authority White text on Void Black/Charcoal Steel backgrounds; Void Black text on Authority White backgrounds. Fracture Cyan and Signal Blue are never used for body text — accent colors only.

**Accessibility Guidelines:** all body text meets WCAG 2.1 AA contrast (4.5:1 minimum); large text and UI components meet 3:1 minimum. Fracture Cyan on Void Black passes AA at large/bold sizes only — never use Cyan-on-Black for small body copy; use Authority White for body text and reserve Cyan for headlines, links, and buttons where size and weight clear the ratio.

**Usage Examples:** a YouTube CTA button is Fracture Cyan fill with Void Black text (passes AA at button scale); a webpage body paragraph is Authority White or Steel-100 on a Void Black or Charcoal Steel background; a printed T-shirt uses the Monochrome logo (Section 3) rather than reproducing all five brand colors at small scale.

## 5. Typography **[LOCKED]**

| Role | Typeface | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Space Grotesk (Bold/Medium) | Headlines, the wordmark, section headers — geometric, technical, confident without being cold |
| **Secondary** | Inter (Regular/Medium) | Body copy, UI text, long-form writing — chosen for legibility at small sizes across every platform |
| **Display/Accent** | IBM Plex Mono (Regular) | Used sparingly for data-flavored brand moments (stat displays, credits, technical captions) — echoes the show's system/glyph aesthetic (Art Bible `color-language.md`) without depicting it directly |

**Hierarchy:** H1/display sets in Space Grotesk Bold; H2–H3 in Space Grotesk Medium; body and UI text in Inter Regular; captions, timestamps, and technical labels in IBM Plex Mono.

**Spacing:** headline tracking is tight-to-neutral (0 to -1%); the "STUDIOS" line in the wordmark carries wider tracking (+8–10%) to visually subordinate it beneath "MYTHIC FORGE," per the logo lockup (Section 3). Body text uses standard Inter default tracking — never artificially condensed or expanded.

**Weights:** Space Grotesk — Bold (headlines), Medium (subheads); Inter — Regular (body), Medium (UI labels/buttons), never Inter Light for body text at small sizes (accessibility). IBM Plex Mono — Regular only; no bold/italic mono usage.

**Fallback Fonts:** Space Grotesk → `system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI", Arial, sans-serif`. Inter → the same stack. IBM Plex Mono → `"Courier New", monospace`.

**Digital Usage:** all three typefaces are freely licensed (Google Fonts / Open Font License) — no licensing gate on web or app use.

**Print Usage:** minimum 8pt for Inter body copy in print; Space Grotesk headlines print cleanly at any size tested to date; avoid IBM Plex Mono below 9pt in print (stroke weight thins out at small physical sizes).

## 6. Visual Language **[LOCKED]**

**Design Philosophy:** the studio's visual language is deliberately more restrained and geometric than the show's own cinematic register (Art Bible `visual-development-guide.md`) — brand materials should feel like the *frame around* the fiction, not compete with it.

**Shape Language:** angular, precise geometry with one deliberate irregularity — the fracture line — breaking an otherwise clean, orderly shape. This mirrors the show's core visual idea (order, then a crack in it) without borrowing any specific in-fiction imagery.

**Textures:** subtle noise/grain only, never busy or decorative texture — brand surfaces stay clean so the fracture accent remains the only "event" in the frame.

**Patterns:** a single repeatable motif — thin fracture-line fragments at low opacity — may be used as a background texture on large surfaces (banners, packaging interiors), always at low contrast, never competing with foreground content.

**Lighting Style:** for any rendered or photographed brand asset, lighting follows the Art Bible's Wounded/Grounded/Charged mode logic in spirit (`lighting-system.md`) but is simplified to two states for brand use: a **Grounded** (even, confident, neutral) state for most materials, and a **Charged** (higher contrast, single accent-lit edge) state reserved for launch/announcement materials only.

**Graphic Elements, Borders, Panels:** panels and cards use a single hairline border (1px, Steel-500) or a solid Charcoal Steel fill — never both a heavy border and a heavy fill on the same element. Corners are sharp or minimally rounded (2–4px) — never pill-shaped or heavily rounded, to preserve the angular brand language.

**Composition:** brand layouts favor asymmetric, confident negative space over centered, symmetric "safe" layouts — echoing the show's own compositional bias toward negative space for emotional weight (Art Bible `camera-language.md`), adapted to static brand materials.

**Negative Space Rules:** every brand composition reserves at least 20% of its frame as clean, undecorated space — a brand asset that feels crowded has violated this rule regardless of how "on-brand" its individual elements are.

**Brand Motifs:** the fracture line (Section 3) is the studio's one recurring visual signature — it appears in the logo, may appear as a subtle background pattern (above), and may appear as a transition device in motion work (Section 8) — it never appears as literal in-fiction imagery (no Cipher-glyphs, no Fragment effects, no megacity skyline) borrowed from the Art Bible.

## 7. Illustration Style **[LOCKED]**

For any illustrated brand asset (as distinct from in-episode art, which is the Art Bible's exclusive domain):

**Rendering Style:** flat-to-subtle-gradient vector illustration — distinct from the show's semi-realistic cinematic register (Art Bible `visual-development-guide.md`), so brand illustration is never mistaken for a frame of the actual show.

**Contrast:** high contrast between foreground marks and background field, consistent with the studio's overall restrained-but-confident visual language (Section 6).

**Depth:** minimal — brand illustration is intentionally flatter and more graphic than the show's cinematic depth-of-field language, to keep the studio's identity visually distinct from its own flagship IP's look.

**Line Style:** consistent single-weight vector line work where linework is used at all; no sketchy, textured, or hand-drawn linework.

**Color Usage:** brand illustration draws only from the palette in Section 4 — no additional illustration-specific colors are introduced.

**Composition Rules:** follows Section 6's negative-space and asymmetry rules.

**Mood:** confident, clean, quietly serious — never playful-cartoonish, never grimdark; consistent with Studio OS Section 4's brand personality.

**Consistency Standards:** any commissioned or AI-assisted brand illustration passes the same Originality Check (Studio OS Section 5) as any other studio asset before use.

## 8. Motion Identity **[LOCKED]**

**Animation Philosophy:** brand motion should feel like a system *activating*, not a spectacle *performing* — restrained, purposeful movement, mirroring the "cost is visible, power is earned" principle (Franchise Design Manifesto Section 3) translated into a marketing context: motion is used because it means something, not because motion is expected.

**Logo Animation:** the fracture line draws on in a single deliberate stroke, then settles into a steady glow — echoing a calm, stable state rather than a flashy reveal. No spinning, no bouncing, no particle bursts.

**Motion Speed:** deliberate and measured — brand bumpers run 0.6–1.2 seconds for the logo activation itself; nothing in brand motion should feel rushed or frantic.

**Transitions:** hard cuts, or a short glitch/static-resolve wipe (echoing system-interference visual language, Art Bible `system-interference-visual-rules.md`, adapted for brand use) — never generic crossfades, spins, or stock transition-pack effects.

**Camera Language:** where brand video uses camera movement at all, it follows the Art Bible's camera philosophy in spirit (`camera-language.md`) — static or slow push-ins only, reserved for landing a specific moment (a title reveal, a CTA), never used as generic B-roll motion.

**Movement Style:** measured, confident, minimal easing flourish — no bouncy/elastic easing curves, no cartoon squash-and-stretch.

**Intro Identity:** a short (2–3 second) logo-activation bumper opens official studio-branded video content — consistent across every series and format the studio produces.

**Outro Identity:** the logo settles to its steady glow state alongside the tagline and a single clear call-to-action (Section 12) — never a cluttered end-card with multiple competing CTAs.

## 9. Audio Identity **[LOCKED]**

**Studio Audio Logo:** a short (1–2 second) sonic mark — a low tone rising cleanly into a settled tone, with a brief, subtle static/glitch texture that resolves into clarity as it lands. Mirrors the visual logo's "activation, then stability" motif (Section 8) in sound.

**Music Direction (brand/marketing only — distinct from the show's own score):** restrained electronic/orchestral hybrid, moody and confident rather than bombastic — explicitly avoiding generic "epic trailer" stock-music energy, consistent with Studio OS Section 4's rule against over-hyping the world.

**Sound Palette:** soft synth pads, subtle data/static texture accents used sparingly, no library "whoosh" or impact stingers pulled from generic stock packs.

**Transition Sounds:** one signature "static-resolve" sting (paired with the visual transition style, Section 8), reused consistently rather than varied per asset.

**Brand Audio Personality:** quietly confident, a little mysterious — the sonic equivalent of Studio OS Section 4's "inviting, a little mysterious" marketing-voice attribute.

## 12. Brand Voice **[LOCKED]**

**Writing Style and Tone:** governed by Studio OS Section 4's Brand Do/Don't checklist in full. This section adds the marketing-copy-specific execution layer beneath it.

**Headlines:** short, declarative, present-tense; describe what's true, not how excited we want the reader to be. No superlatives that aren't earned by specific, statable content.

**Descriptions:** follow the spoiler-safe synopsis structure already defined for SEO copy (Studio OS Section 23) — a two-line hook, a spoiler-safe synopsis, then links.

**Calls to Action:** invite, never demand. "Watch the next chapter" — not "SMASH THAT SUBSCRIBE BUTTON." One clear CTA per asset, never a stacked list of asks.

**Words We Use:** forge, build, discover, earn, become, world, craft.
**Words We Avoid:** epic, insane, you won't believe, mind-blowing, lore-dump, and any generic hype adjective not tied to something specific the content actually delivers.

**Community Interaction Style:** earnest and responsive; never dismissive of fan theories, even incorrect ones; never confirms or denies an unresolved mystery (World Bible Section 2, Series Bible Section 15) — community replies are held to the same ambiguity-preservation discipline as the story itself.

## 14. Asset Standards **[LOCKED]**

**Logo Exports:** SVG (vector master, primary distribution format for all uses); PNG at 1×/2×/3× for raster contexts; Transparent PNG (no background) for overlay use; Print Files (CMYK-converted PDF/EPS, minimum 300dpi); Favicons (ICO plus 32×32/16×16 PNG, derived from the Icon Version); App Icons (1024×1024 master, Icon Version, cropped per each platform's own icon-shape guidelines).

**Naming Convention:** `mfs-logo-{variant}-{color-mode}_v{X.X}.{ext}` — e.g., `mfs-logo-primary-dark_v1.0.svg`. Extends Production Bible Section 5's naming pattern rather than inventing a separate one.

**Folder Structure:** brand assets live under `/brand/assets/` once real files exist — not scaffolded speculatively (Studio Wiki Section 15's rule against empty planned folders).

**Version Control:** standard studio-wide `v{major}.{minor}` (Production Bible Section 6) — a new logo variant or palette value is a major bump; a corrected export or added size is a minor bump.

## 16. Future Expansion **[LIVING]**

**How new brands are added:** a second flagship IP gets its own Art-Bible-equivalent visual law for its fiction, but is marketed under this same master Brand Bible unless the Founder explicitly establishes a distinct sub-brand — sub-brand creation is a Founder Authority decision (Section 15), not a default.

**How future animated series fit under Mythic Forge Studios:** every future series is marketed under the studio's master identity (Sections 2–5) by default; each new series' key art follows its own Art Bible's illustration rules while still carrying the studio's logo system (Section 3) in marketing placements.

**Sub-brand guidelines:** requires the same bar as introducing any new process document (Studio Governance Manual Section 19, Future Expansion Rules) — a stated scope that doesn't already belong to this Bible, and explicit Founder approval before it's built.

**Studio growth principles:** mirrors Studio OS Section 28's Phased Roadmap — brand investment sequences with actual studio growth (don't build a full merchandise line or a five-platform social presence before there's an audience to serve any of it); growth in brand surface area should never outpace the studio's actual capacity to maintain consistency across all of it (Section 1, Creative Principle 2 — "one identity, every surface").
