## 1. Episode Identity

- **Episode number:** 20
- **Episode title:** Placeholder only, not final — "A Public Voice" (Phase 4A.2, Episode 20 seed entry).
- **Core purpose:** Develop Mira's expanding importance and the Guardian's intervention-timing tension in parallel, immediately ahead of the turning point (Phase 4A.2, Episode 20, Purpose).

## 2. Story Structure

- **Opening hook:** Mira, still primarily known through her relationship to Kael, begins to be recognized for her own voice; the Guardian's disagreement with Kael is present but has not yet come to a head (Phase 4A.2, Episode 20, Beginning State).
- **Setup:** Both threads develop independently — Mira's growing visibility unconnected to any decision by Kael; the Guardian/Kael tension building from Kael's own accumulating urgency since Episode 16.
- **Rising conflict:** Mira's role grows from established relationship into an active voice against the Doctrine's logic, a more public, politically visible role connected to her reconstruction work; simultaneously, the already-Locked intervention-timing disagreement comes to a head — Kael wants more direct action, the Guardian refuses, creating real tension (Phase 4A.2, Episode 20, Central Conflict).
- **Character turning point:** Mira gains narrative weight independent of Kael; the Guardian/Kael relationship reaches open disagreement, unresolved (Phase 4A.2, Episode 20, Character Progression).
- **Major reveal or progression:** None new — both beats are character-scale (Phase 4A.2, Episode 20, World Progression).
- **Climax:** Two distinct high points — Mira's voice landing publicly on her own terms, and the Guardian's refusal landing as a real breach in its partnership with Kael.
- **Closing emotional beat:** Both threads left open, heightening tension immediately before Cassian's offer (Phase 4A.2, Episode 20, Ending Beat).
- **Cliffhanger / Transition:** Into the major turning point (Phase 4A.2, Episode 20, Transition; Episode 21's own seed entry).

## 3. Scene Breakdown

*Five scenes proposed, alternating between the two parallel threads per the seed entry's own structure, sized for Studio OS's Mainline Episode runtime target.*

**Scene 1 — Recognized**
- Purpose: Open the Mira thread — her voice beginning to be recognized beyond her relationship to Kael.
- Location: A civilian reconstruction or public-facing context, consistent with her Locked role.
- Characters present: Mira; unnamed civilians and reconstruction colleagues.
- Emotional state: Mira — steady, unperformed; she isn't seeking attention, but her lived experience carries weight when she speaks.
- Conflict: None external — the scene establishes that her perspective is beginning to reach further than her immediate circle.
- Visual requirements: Reused reconstruction-work environment from Episode 19; no new location required.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should carry her own lived experience directly, not as rehearsed argument — per her own Locked framing, "she simply is the argument."
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 2 — What Kael Wants**
- Purpose: Open the Guardian/Kael thread — his push for direct action.
- Location: Existing Guardian/Kael partnership environment, reused.
- Characters present: Kael; the Guardian.
- Emotional state: Kael — urgent, carrying the accumulated weight of Episodes 16–18; the Guardian — attentive, already anticipating where this leads.
- Conflict: Kael believes that seeing a civilization heading toward destruction creates a responsibility to act before more lives are lost (Character Bible, "The Guardian & Kael") — voiced directly for the first time this arc.
- Visual requirements: Same environment; no new location.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should carry Kael's urgency as sincere, not reckless — a genuine extension of his leadership philosophy under new pressure.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 3 — She Simply Is the Argument**
- Purpose: Deliver Mira's voice landing on her own terms.
- Location: Same as Scene 1.
- Characters present: Mira; unnamed civilians.
- Emotional state: Mira — grounded, speaking from lived experience rather than ideology.
- Conflict: Her role grows into "an active voice against the Doctrine's logic — a more public, politically visible role connected to reconstruction work" (Phase 4A.2, Episode 20, Central Conflict) — not by arguing abstractly, but by being living proof of what protection should mean.
- Visual requirements: Same environment; no new location, no new political iconography or organization invented.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should stay grounded in her own personal history — displacement, rebuilding — never becoming a policy speech.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 4 — The Guardian Refuses**
- Purpose: Bring the intervention-timing disagreement to its head.
- Location: Same as Scene 2.
- Characters present: Kael; the Guardian.
- Emotional state: Kael — frustrated, genuinely caring; the Guardian — firm but not cold, its own principle costing it something.
- Conflict: The Guardian believes civilizations must be allowed to face the consequences of their own choices in order to truly grow (Character Bible, "The Guardian & Kael") — refusing Kael's push for more direct action, creating real, unresolved tension between them.
- Visual requirements: Same environment; no new location.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should keep both positions sincere — per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement, the Guardian's calm must not read as certainty; its refusal should carry visible cost.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 5 — Two Threads, Still Open**
- Purpose: Close the episode with both threads left unresolved, heightening tension into Episode 21.
- Location: Cutting between Mira's and Kael/Guardian's settings, or a shared closing beat if production prefers — a mood note, not a shot list.
- Characters present: Mira in one beat; Kael and the Guardian in the other.
- Emotional state: All three — unresolved, carrying real weight into what comes next.
- Conflict: None new — the scene exists to let both threads' tension stand, unresolved, immediately before the turning point.
- Visual requirements: Same environments as established; no new location.
- Audio requirements: The scene should end without a resolving musical cue on either thread.
- Production needs: No new asset.

## 4. Character Performance Planning

**Kael.**
- Motivations this episode: To act on what he's learned since Episode 16 — a genuine, urgent desire to prevent further harm, distinct from and not a test of his burden-alone flaw (Phase 4A.2, Episode 20, Character Progression).
- Emotional progression: Urgent (Scene 2) → frustrated but caring (Scene 4) → unresolved (Scene 5).
- Dialogue intent, not lines: his push for direct action should read as a sincere extension of his leadership philosophy under pressure, not recklessness; per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement, this disagreement is political/strategic, not a dramatization of his personal flaw.
- Body language: increasingly urgent through Scenes 2 and 4, unresolved and carrying real tension by Scene 5.
- Relationship dynamics: with the Guardian only, this episode — their Season One partnership under genuine strain for the first time.

**Mira.**
- Motivations this episode: To speak from her own lived experience when it's sought, not to seek a platform for its own sake (Character Bible, "Philosophy & Internal Conflict").
- Emotional progression: Steady, unperformed recognition (Scene 1) → grounded, lived-experience voice (Scene 3) → unresolved, carrying her own weight forward (Scene 5).
- Dialogue intent, not lines: her voice should remain personal and specific throughout — per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement, she is never written as a spokesperson or ideologue, only as someone whose own history speaks for itself.
- Body language: grounded and unperformed throughout, never seeking attention, simply present and honest.
- Relationship dynamics: with unnamed civilians/colleagues only this episode — no scene with Kael, consistent with her narrative weight growing independent of him this episode specifically.

**The Guardian.**
- Motivations this episode: To hold its own principle even as it costs something in its partnership with Kael (Character Bible, "The Guardian & Kael").
- Emotional progression: Attentive anticipation (Scene 2) → firm, costly refusal (Scene 4).
- Dialogue intent, not lines: its refusal should be principled and sincere, per its own Locked belief that civilizations must face consequences to truly grow — never dismissive of Kael's urgency.
- Body language: composed but visibly affected in Scene 4 — per Phase 2E's requirement, its calm must not read as certainty.
- Relationship dynamics: with Kael only, this episode.
