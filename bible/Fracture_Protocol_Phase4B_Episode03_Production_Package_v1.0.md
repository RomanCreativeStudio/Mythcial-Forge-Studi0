## 1. Episode Identity

- **Episode number:** 3
- **Episode title:** "What's Left Behind"
- **Core purpose:** Show how the people around Kael respond to what Episode 2 revealed (Phase 4A, Episode 3, Purpose).
- **Narrative objective:** Dramatize Light Order's guardian-ethos culture treating Kael's overextension as expected sacrifice rather than a warning sign — institutional reinforcement of the flaw, not correction of it (Phase 4A, Episode 3, Central Conflict).
- **Emotional objective:** Unease under warmth — every gesture of approval Kael receives should read, to the audience, as one step further from the help he actually needs.
- **Audience experience goal:** The audience should see the danger before Kael does. Per Phase 4A's own Ending note for this episode, this is the first episode where dramatic irony is the entire point — Kael reads institutional approval as validation; the audience should not.

---

## 2. Story Structure

*Two distinct threads this episode, both already fixed by Phase 4A's seed entry: Kael's institutional aftermath (Central Conflict) and the Ascendant Doctrine's background recruiting (World Progression). Neither is new; both are dramatized here for the first time at full scene length.*

- **Opening hook:** Ward Station Seven in the immediate aftermath of Episode 2's crisis — the institution processing what happened, not Kael alone.
- **Setup:** Kael recovering, receiving attention and praise from Light Order-affiliated colleagues and superiors for the crisis's outcome.
- **Rising conflict:** The praise never once questions *how* the outcome was achieved — Light Order's guardian-ethos culture treats his overextension as expected sacrifice, not a warning sign (Phase 4A, Episode 3, Central Conflict).
- **Character turning point:** None this episode — Kael's own turning points remain Episode 4 (the Guardian's outreach) and Episode 7 (rank advancement); this episode is entirely about what happens *around* him, not a choice he makes.
- **Major reveal or progression:** Among the displaced survivors from Episode 2's crisis, an unnamed Ascendant Doctrine opportunist is shown quietly recruiting — the season's first sustained look at the Doctrine's method, still fully unpersonified (Phase 4A, Episode 3, World Progression; Phase 2A.2, Section 1).
- **Climax:** Not a confrontation — the accumulation of institutional approval reaches its clearest, most uncomfortable point: Kael is being rewarded for the exact behavior that should concern everyone around him.
- **Closing emotional beat:** Kael, genuinely warmed by the institutional approval, reads it as validation — the audience is left holding the danger he cannot yet see (Phase 4A, Episode 3, Ending).
- **Cliffhanger / Transition:** Directly into the Guardian's first direct outreach (Phase 4A, Episode 3, Transition) — no plot event closes the episode; the transition is tonal, into Episode 4.

## 3. Scene Breakdown

*Five scenes proposed, sized for Studio OS's Mainline Episode runtime target. The Ascendant Doctrine thread and the Light Order thread are kept visually and narratively separate — they do not intersect this episode, consistent with the Doctrine still being background-only.*

**Scene 1 — Aftermath**
- Purpose: Open on Ward Station Seven processing the prior episode's crisis.
- Location: Ward Station Seven.
- Characters present: Kael, recovering; unnamed Ward Division/Light 98th personnel.
- Emotional state: Quiet, still recovering physically from Episode 2.
- Conflict: None yet direct.
- Visual requirements: Ward Station Seven interior; Kael's strain-state variant from Episode 2, easing but not fully resolved.
- Audio requirements: Low-key, recuperative — no urgency.
- Production needs: Reuses Ward Station Seven asset and Kael's Episode 2 strain-state variant.

**Scene 2 — Praise**
- Purpose: Dramatize the institutional approval Kael receives.
- Location: Ward Station Seven.
- Characters present: Kael; unnamed Light Order-affiliated superiors/colleagues.
- Emotional state: Warm, validating — genuinely well-intentioned on every side.
- Conflict: Dramatic irony — the praise never questions the method, only the outcome (Phase 4A, Episode 3, Central Conflict).
- Visual requirements: Ward Station Seven interior; Light Order cultural markers per Faction Bible, cited not restated.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue carrying warmth and approval — intent only, no lines written here.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 3 — Among the Displaced**
- Purpose: Open the episode's second thread — the Ascendant Doctrine's recruiting method.
- Location: Lower Sector, among displaced survivors from Episode 2's crisis.
- Characters present: Unnamed displaced civilians; an unnamed Ascendant Doctrine opportunist. Kael does not appear in this scene.
- Emotional state: Vulnerable, uncertain — civilians processing loss and displacement.
- Conflict: The opportunist's quiet, patient recruiting — offering certainty to people who currently have none (Phase 2D, Section 1, "Doctrine sympathy concentrates among the displaced and disaffected").
- Visual requirements: Lower Sector displaced-civilian environment, a new dressing of the existing asset; no new character is designed for the opportunist beyond a generic, unnamed figure.
- Audio requirements: Quiet, measured dialogue — intent only; the opportunist's appeal should sound reasonable, not villainous, consistent with the Doctrine's Locked characterization (Faction Bible, "Why People Follow It").
- Production needs: New displaced-civilian dressing of the Lower Sector asset; checked against the Asset Registry before build.

**Scene 4 — More Praise**
- Purpose: Deepen the institutional-approval thread to its most uncomfortable point.
- Location: Ward Station Seven.
- Characters present: Kael; unnamed Light Order-affiliated figures, now including someone more senior than Scene 2's colleagues.
- Emotional state: Kael visibly warmed and reassured; the audience should feel the opposite.
- Conflict: The approval escalates — Kael's overextension is now being held up as an example, not merely excused.
- Visual requirements: Same Ward Station Seven asset.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should make the institutional reinforcement explicit without ever having a character state the theme aloud.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 5 — What Kael Doesn't See**
- Purpose: Close the episode on unresolved dramatic irony.
- Location: Ward Station Seven, a quiet moment alone.
- Characters present: Kael only.
- Emotional state: Genuine, uncomplicated relief and pride — no doubt, no hesitation.
- Conflict: None external — the entire conflict is the gap between what Kael feels and what the audience now understands.
- Visual requirements: Same Ward Station Seven asset; a quieter, more intimate framing than Scenes 2 and 4.
- Audio requirements: Minimal or no dialogue; the scene should be allowed to sit in silence.
- Production needs: No new asset.

## 4. Character Performance Planning

**Kael.**
- Motivations this episode: To be worthy of the trust and care being shown to him — not pride for its own sake, but a genuine, uncomplicated wish to have done right by the people who raised him (Character Bible, "Leadership Philosophy").
- Emotional progression: Quiet recovery (Scene 1) → warmth and validation (Scene 2) → deepening reassurance (Scene 4) → genuine, unclouded relief (Scene 5). No doubt or hesitation appears anywhere this episode — that is the point, not an oversight.
- Dialogue intent, not lines: in Scenes 2 and 4, Kael's responses to praise should carry gratitude and quiet pride, never self-questioning. Per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement, this episode must not show him "sharing responsibility effortlessly, without cost or hesitation" in a way that resolves the flaw — the distinction here is that he isn't sharing responsibility at all; he's being told there was nothing to share. The flaw remains fully intact and, if anything, is being reinforced, not softened.
- Body language: physically still recovering in Scene 1 (visible ease from Episode 2's strain variant); increasingly relaxed and open across Scenes 2–5, ending in Scene 5 on genuine, unguarded calm — a calm the audience should read as precarious, not earned.
- Relationship dynamics: institutional relationships (unnamed Light Order-affiliated superiors and colleagues) are this episode's primary relational focus; Mira does not appear, consistent with Phase 4A's own Character Progression note for this episode ("Kael reads institutional approval as validation, not correction").

**Ascendant Doctrine opportunist (unnamed, background figure only).**
- Motivations this episode: Genuine, patient recruitment — offering certainty and belonging to people who currently have neither (Faction Bible, "Why People Follow It"). Not villainous performance; the Doctrine's own Locked characterization requires this figure to sound reasonable, even sympathetic, in intent.
- Emotional progression: none — a single scene, single register (calm, patient).
- Dialogue intent, not lines: should never invoke Cassian, the Doctrine's leadership, or any named canon fact beyond what's already Locked (Faction Bible, "Belief, Strengths, Weakness"). No new ideology or leadership detail is introduced.
- Body language: calm, unhurried, non-threatening — the opposite of a villain's body language, consistent with the franchise's own rule that the Doctrine is dangerous through what protection becomes without humanity, not through overt menace.
- Relationship dynamics: none — this figure does not interact with Kael or any named character this episode.

## 5. Visual Production Planning

- **Required environments:** Ward Station Seven (interior, Scenes 1, 2, 4, 5 — reused) and a new displaced-civilian dressing of the existing Lower Sector environment asset (Scene 3) — no new location introduced.
- **Character appearances:** Kael, per his existing Art Bible reference sheet, using Episode 2's strain-state variant in an easing state for Scene 1. The Ascendant Doctrine opportunist uses a generic, unnamed figure design — no distinguishing feature that could read as a named character is introduced.
- **Props:** None beyond what's already implied by Locked world-building.
- **Effects:** None this episode — no Breach-adjacent or disturbance effect is required.
- **Technology:** Governed by Phase 1B.3's existing framework; nothing new required.
- **Lighting:** Per Art Bible's `lighting-system.md` — Ward Station Seven's institutional scenes should read warmer and more settled than Episode 2's crisis lighting; the Lower Sector displaced-civilian scene should read more exposed and uncertain, within existing zone-identity rules, not a new lighting rule.
- **Cinematic requirements:** Per Art Bible's `camera-language.md` and `cinematic-mood-guide.md` — this episode's tone (warm institutional scenes undercut by dramatic irony; a quieter, more vulnerable civilian scene) is a mood note, not a shot list.

---

## 6. Audio Planning

- **Narration needs:** None.
- **Dialogue priorities:** Scenes 2 and 4 (institutional praise) and Scene 3 (the opportunist's recruiting) carry this episode's dialogue weight; Scene 5 should use minimal or no dialogue.
- **Sound design requirements:** Warm, settled ambience for Ward Station Seven scenes; a more exposed, uncertain ambience for the Lower Sector displaced-civilian scene — per Phase 3A's routing to Production OS's Music Production SOP and Studio OS's Music & Audio Standards.
- **Music tone:** Deliberately warm and reassuring under Scenes 2 and 4 — the score should sound like it agrees with the institution, so the dissonance is entirely the audience's own recognition, not a musical cue telling them something is wrong.
- **Emotional timing:** No musical or sound cue should signal danger anywhere in this episode — the entire point of "What's Left Behind" is that nothing here sounds or looks wrong from the inside. Any score choice that undercuts the institutional warmth would blunt the dramatic irony Phase 4A's seed entry is built on.

- **Props:** None required.
- **Effects:** None required.
- **Reusable assets:** This episode's new items (the opportunist figure, the displaced-civilian dressing) are both explicitly designed for reuse in later episodes where the Doctrine's background presence continues, rather than single-use builds.
