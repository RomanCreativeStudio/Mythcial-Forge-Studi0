The Second Generation Review found three specific composition elements driving the derivative-design concern: **(a)** both hands gripping the jacket collar, **(b)** the hairstyle treatment, **(c)** the distressed jacket silhouette (plus the general portrait composition).

| (c) Distressed jacket silhouette / general composition | **NOT RESOLVED — and one new detail makes it more specific, not less.** The weathered, mottled-brown canvas/leather jacket is unchanged in material and construction. It is now worn over a **bib-style overall layer with a visible metal shoulder-strap buckle** — a garment detail that was not present in V1 or V2. This specific combination (worn jacket + bib overalls + a metal buckle on an adjustable shoulder strap, at this exact collar/chest position) is itself a highly recognizable, specific design element strongly associated with the same existing published character this review has flagged twice already. Removing the collar-grip gesture addressed the *pose*, but the *garment construction* — which is at least as strong a source of the resemblance, arguably stronger now — was not addressed, and the newly added buckle-strap detail moves it closer to that reference, not further away. |

**Determination on the core concern: still not resolved.** The literal instruction that was given ("Do NOT use a jacket collar gripping pose") was followed precisely — this is real, credited progress. But the Second Generation Review's finding was broader than the pose alone: it named the jacket silhouette and general styling as independently contributing to the resemblance, and that part of the concern remains, now expressed through a different specific detail (the buckle-strap overall construction) rather than through the pose.

## Step 4 — Determination

**REVISION REQUIRED.** The specific pose flagged in the Second Generation Review is corrected. The broader concern that review also named — the garment silhouette contributing to resemblance with an existing published character — is not resolved, and the new buckle-strap overall detail is, if anything, a more specific match to that reference than the previous version's plain jacket.

**Concrete, actionable fix, grounded in what is and isn't Locked:** nothing in Mira's Costume Canon (Phase 6B.3 §3) requires bib overalls or a shoulder-strap buckle — her Locked Layering Philosophy is simply "a protective outer layer over simple base wear," which the jacket-over-a-simple-base-layer concept already satisfies without any overall/buckle construction. Removing the bib-overall-with-metal-buckle layer entirely (returning to a jacket over a simple shirt/base layer, per Phase 6B.3's own language, with utility pockets on the jacket itself if tool-carry needs representing) would address the remaining concern without losing any Locked requirement — it was an unforced addition, not something the canon calls for.
