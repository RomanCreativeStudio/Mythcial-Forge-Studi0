## 1. Episode Identity

- **Episode number:** 1
- **Episode title:** "Status Quo"
- **Core purpose:** Establish Ward Station Seven, Kael's dual Cadet/Light 98th status, and the early-stage Mira relationship (Phase 4A, Episode 1, Purpose).
- **Narrative objective:** Ground the audience in Kael's ordinary world before Movement I's crisis begins (Phase 4A, Section 2, Movement I) — establish the baseline the rest of the season will test, not narrate an event.
- **Emotional objective:** Warmth, stability, and quiet competence — a world worth protecting, seen before anything costs Kael to protect it.
- **Audience experience goal:** The episode should feel grounded and lived-in, not tense. Per Phase 4A Section 1's "Audience Experience," Season One asks the audience to watch trust get built at the same time it watches a flaw get proven — Episode 1 is pure trust-building, with the flaw not yet visible even to the audience.

---

## 2. Story Structure

*Per Phase 4A's own Locked characterization of this episode — "no conflict yet, by design" (Phase 4A, Episode 1, Central Conflict) — several of the eight fields below are intentionally minimal. Forcing invented conflict into them to fill the template would contradict the Locked seed entry; that tension is resolved here by leaving them honestly minimal rather than fabricating content.*

- **Opening hook:** Kael in the middle of an ordinary Ward Division duty shift at Ward Station Seven, Lower Sector — competence shown before anything is explained.
- **Setup:** Ward Station Seven's environment and Light 98th culture established around him; his dual Cadet/Light 98th-affiliated status made visible through ordinary interactions, not exposition.
- **Rising conflict:** None external, per the Locked seed entry. The only tension is dramatic irony — the audience senses, before any character does, that this stability is what the rest of the season will test.
- **Character turning point:** None this episode. Phase 4A places the season's first turning point at Episode 4 (the Guardian's outreach) and its proposed mid-season turning point at Episode 7 — Episode 1 precedes both by design.
- **Major reveal or progression:** No reveal. The early-stage Mira relationship is shown already in progress, establishing baseline rather than introducing anything new (Phase 4A, Episode 1, Character Progression).
- **Climax:** None in the traditional sense — consistent with "no conflict yet, by design." The episode's dramatic peak is its ending beat, not a confrontation.
- **Closing emotional beat:** Quiet stability, immediately preceding the first sign of disruption.
- **Cliffhanger:** First signs of unusual Breach-adjacent activity in the Lower Sector (Phase 4A, Episode 1, Ending — Locked seed content, not new here).

## 3. Scene Breakdown

*Five scenes proposed, sized for Studio OS's Mainline Episode runtime target (8–14 minutes, Section 9) and its standard template (cold open → title card → Act structure → end card). Scene count and shape are new production-level synthesis; no scene introduces content beyond the seed entry.*

**Scene 1 — Cold Open: Duty Shift**
- Purpose: Establish Kael's competence and ordinary routine before any names or context are given.
- Location: Ward Station Seven, Lower Sector (exterior/patrol context).
- Characters present: Kael.
- Emotional state: Focused, unremarkable calm.
- Conflict: None.
- Visual requirements: Lower Sector environment per Phase 2D Section 2 and Art Bible environment-design-rules.md; no new location invented.
- Audio requirements: Ambient Lower Sector soundscape; no dialogue required to carry this scene.
- Production needs: Reuses the season's core Lower Sector environment asset (Phase 4A, Section 6).

**Scene 2 — Ward Station Seven: Dual Status**
- Purpose: Make Kael's simultaneous Cadet/Light 98th-affiliated status visible through ordinary interaction, not exposition.
- Location: Ward Station Seven interior.
- Characters present: Kael; unnamed Light 98th and Ward Division colleagues (no new named character introduced).
- Emotional state: Familiar, at ease among people who know him.
- Conflict: None.
- Visual requirements: Ward Station Seven interior set; Light 98th culture markers per Faction Bible, cited not restated.
- Audio requirements: Light dialogue (intent only, see Section 4) establishing routine, not plot.
- Production needs: Reuses Ward Station Seven interior asset (Phase 4A, Section 6).

**Scene 3 — Kael and Mira**
- Purpose: Show the earned-trust relationship already in progress.
- Location: Ward Station Seven or adjacent Lower Sector civilian space.
- Characters present: Kael, Mira.
- Emotional state: Warm, unguarded, comfortable silence as much as conversation.
- Conflict: None.
- Visual requirements: Kael and Mira reference sheets (Phase 4A, Section 6, top asset priority).
- Audio requirements: Dialogue establishing the relationship's current stage — trust before friendship before love, not yet spoken aloud (Phase 2C, Kael §4; Mira §4).
- Production needs: No new asset; both characters already the season's top priority build.

**Scene 4 — Baseline Belief**
- Purpose: Establish, through action rather than statement, what Kael already believes — power as responsibility, not weapon (Phase 2A.2, Section 1).
- Location: Ward Station Seven.
- Characters present: Kael; unnamed colleagues or civilians.
- Emotional state: Quiet conviction.
- Conflict: None.
- Visual requirements: Same Ward Station Seven asset set as Scene 2.
- Audio requirements: Minimal dialogue; the beat should read visually wherever possible, consistent with "show, don't state."
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 5 — The First Sign**
- Purpose: Deliver the episode's cliffhanger.
- Location: Lower Sector.
- Characters present: Kael (Light 98th/Ward Division personnel may register the disturbance around him; no new named character).
- Emotional state: Calm breaking into alertness — the episode's only shift in tone.
- Conflict: The first, minimal external disturbance — unusual Breach-adjacent activity, not yet a crisis (Phase 4A, Episode 1, Ending).
- Visual requirements: A subtle environmental/Signal-adjacent disturbance cue, governed by Art Bible's `system-interference-visual-rules.md` — cited, not redefined.
- Audio requirements: A single sound-design cue marking the shift; no music sting that overplays the moment (see Section 6, Emotional Timing).
- Production needs: Reuses the Lower Sector environment asset; the disturbance effect itself follows Art Bible rules, no new effect designed here.

## 4. Character Performance Planning

**Kael.**
- Motivations this episode: To do his duty well, quietly, without needing acknowledgment — consistent with his Locked belief that power is a responsibility, not a weapon (Character Bible, "Leadership Philosophy"; Phase 2C, Kael §1).
- Emotional progression: Calm competence (Scenes 1–2) → warmth and ease (Scene 3) → quiet conviction (Scene 4) → alertness (Scene 5) — the episode's only emotional shift is its final beat.
- Dialogue intent, not lines: in Scene 2, dialogue should establish familiarity and routine, never backstory dumped aloud. In Scene 3, dialogue should imply trust already built, not narrate how it was built. In Scene 5, dialogue (if any) should be minimal — reaction, not explanation.
- Body language: unhurried, economical movement in Scenes 1–2 and 4; visibly more open and relaxed in Scene 3; a sudden, small shift in posture (not panic) in Scene 5 — per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement, no scene may show him effortlessly carrying responsibility without any hint of the cost that will surface later; Episode 1 should not oversell his ease to the point of contradicting that requirement.
- Relationship dynamics: with Mira, earned trust already established, not being built for the first time on screen (Phase 2C, Kael §4). No other core-cast relationship is active this episode (the Guardian, Cassian, and Titan's legacy are not yet introduced, per Phase 4A's Character Progression Matrix).

**Mira.**
- Motivations this episode: Presence and ease with Kael, consistent with a relationship already in progress (Phase 2C, Mira §1, §4).
- Emotional progression: Comfortable, present, unguarded throughout Scene 3 — no arc movement this episode; her own backstory surfaces later (Episode 5, per Phase 4A).
- Dialogue intent, not lines: should imply a relationship with history, without stating any specific past event — her displacement backstory is explicitly not surfaced yet (Phase 4A, Section 4, Mira row).
- Body language: at ease, unguarded — per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement for Mira, this scene must not define her by rescue or victimhood in any way; here that means her presence reads as an equal, established relationship, not a rescued or fragile figure.
- Relationship dynamics: with Kael only, this episode.

## 6. Audio Planning

- **Narration needs:** None — the episode is designed to establish its world through action and ordinary interaction, not narration.
- **Dialogue priorities:** Scene 3 (Kael and Mira) carries the episode's only dialogue with real weight; Scenes 1, 2, and 4 use dialogue sparingly, in service of routine and characterization, not plot delivery.
- **Sound design requirements:** Ambient Lower Sector and Ward Station Seven soundscapes throughout; a single, clearly distinct sound cue for Scene 5's disturbance, per Phase 3A's routing to Production OS's Music Production SOP and Studio OS's Music & Audio Standards.
- **Music tone:** Quiet, grounded, warm — consistent with Phase 2A.2 Section 7's own pacing note that quiet character moments fall early in Movement I.
- **Emotional timing:** Music should not telegraph the ending disturbance early — the shift from calm to alert in Scene 5 should land as a genuine change in register, not a moment the score has already been signaling.
