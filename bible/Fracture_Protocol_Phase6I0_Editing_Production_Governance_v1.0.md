# THE FRACTURE PROTOCOL — PHASE 6I.0: EDITING PRODUCTION GOVERNANCE

**Classification:** Internal — Governance Document (**Compiled — Pending Founder Review**)
**Status:** Compiled 2026-07-18 per "Founder Directive — Phase 6I.0 Editing Production Governance (v1.0)." Continues directly from Phase 6H.2. This document creates no edit, modifies no canon, and rewrites no screenplay. It establishes only the new governance Phase 6I (Editing Production) requires — it does not restate the Environment (Phase 6C), Prop & Technology (Phase 6D.0), Storyboard (Phase 6E.0), Animation (Phase 6F.0), Audio (Phase 6G.0), or Visual Effects (Phase 6H.0) governance already Locked; those remain in force, unmodified, and are cited by reference only.
**Absolute authority:** subordinate to Master System Prompt → Locked Canon (World Bible, Character Bible, Faction Bible) → Production Bible → Approved Screenplays → Art Bible (`system-interference-visual-rules.md`, `color-language.md`, `lighting-system.md`, `global-consistency-rules.md`) → Phase 6B Character Production line → Phase 6C Environment Production Canon and its full reconciliation lineage → Phase 6D.0/6D.1/6D.1.5 Prop & Technology Production → Phase 6E.0/6E.1/6E.2 Storyboard Production → Phase 6F.0/6F.1/6F.2 Animation Production → Phase 6G.0/6G.1/6G.2 Audio Production → Phase 6H.0/6H.1/6H.2 Visual Effects Production → **Phase 6I.0 Editing Production Governance**.

---

## 1. Purpose

Phase 6I exists to govern the **assembly** of already-Approved assets into edited sequences — it does not itself produce a single new asset of any kind. Editing is strictly a downstream discipline: it may only combine, order, and time material that Storyboard (Phase 6E), Animation (Phase 6F), Audio (Phase 6G), and Visual Effects (Phase 6H) have already approved, exactly as each of those disciplines' own governing packages define it. Phase 6I.0 establishes only the rules that govern this assembly work. It authorizes no edit, cut, or sequence itself.

---

## 2. Editing Authority

An edit is authorized only by:
1. **Approved Screenplays** — each scene's own Editing Direction / Camera Notes field, where present, is the primary source governing how that scene is to be cut (e.g., the confirmed "hard cut, not fading" and "no fade or gradual build" instructions already logged as FPP-AUD-014).
2. **Approved Storyboard Production Packages** (`Fracture_Protocol_Phase6E2_Storyboard_Production_Packages_v1.0.md`) — each package's own Transition Rules and Continuity Requirements fields govern how its sequence group may be cut into and out of.
3. **Approved Animation Production Packages** (`Fracture_Protocol_Phase6F2_Animation_Production_Packages_v1.0.md`) — each package's own Timing field bounds how long a given motion, expression, or cycle may be held on-screen before a cut.
4. **Approved Audio Production Packages** (`Fracture_Protocol_Phase6G2_Audio_Production_Packages_v1.0.md`) — the Dialogue Authority rule (exact-text lock on all recorded dialogue, Phase 6G.0 §2) directly constrains cut placement: no edit may truncate, overlap, or reorder a locked line of dialogue.
5. **Approved Visual Effects Production Packages** (`Fracture_Protocol_Phase6H2_VFX_Production_Packages_v1.0.md`) — where an edit crosses a VFX-bearing shot (Kael's eye-glow, the Breach-adjacent disturbance lineage), the effect's own Continuity field governs how it may carry, or must not carry, across the cut.

An edit with no citable source in one of these five categories does not exist for production purposes — it must be flagged as missing, never invented. Editing assembles existing Approved assets only; it is never itself a source of new visual, motion, dialogue, or effects content.

---

## 3. Assembly Authority

Assembly (the ordering of shots, scenes, and sequences into a continuous cut) is governed by:
- Each screenplay's own Scene Count, Scene List, and scene order — already the basis of Phase 6E.1's Master Storyboard Inventory (175 scenes across 36 episodes) — which fixes narrative sequence; assembly may not reorder scenes beyond what an Approved Screenplay itself specifies.
- Each Storyboard Production Package's own Governing Screenplay Scenes and Reuse Opportunities fields, which determine which shots may be assembled from a shared reusable template (e.g., FPP-SB-002's confirmed camera-echo structure across Episodes 1, 2, and 9–11) versus which require a scene-specific build.
- Each Animation and Audio Production Package's own Reuse Rules field, governing which motion cycles, dialogue lines, music cues, or ambient beds may be reused across multiple assembled scenes without re-approval, and which may not.

Assembly may combine only assets that are individually Approved; it may not assemble a placeholder, a flagged-missing item, or an inventory entry that has not yet reached Package Complete status in its own discipline.

---

## 4. Continuity Authority

Every assembled edit must remain consistent with the continuity rules already Locked in each contributing discipline, cited by reference and never restated or re-adjudicated here:
- Storyboard Continuity Requirements (Phase 6E.0 §6; each Phase 6E.2 package's own Continuity Requirements field).
- Animation Continuity Rule (Phase 6F.0; each Phase 6F.2 package's own Continuity field — e.g., Cassian's five-stage unraveling schedule, FPP-ANIM-003, must remain distinguishable and escalating across every edit that includes it, never collapsed or reordered).
- Audio Continuity Rule (Phase 6G.0 §7; each Phase 6G.2 package's own Continuity field — e.g., the series-wide restraint music baseline, FPP-AUD-006, must not be violated by an edit that introduces a swell or resolving cue a scene's own Music Direction did not call for).
- Visual Effects Continuity Rule (Phase 6H.0 §5; each Phase 6H.2 package's own Continuity field — e.g., Kael's eye-glow must render identically across every episode it appears in, and the Breach-adjacent lineage's escalating-but-consistent character must not be broken by re-ordering or intercutting its three confirmed appearances).

An edit that would violate any one of these already-Locked continuity rules is prohibited, regardless of any perceived editorial or pacing benefit.

---

## 5. Transition Authority

Per Phase 6H.1's own confirmed, exhaustive finding, **no rendered transition effect (dissolve, wipe, cross-dissolve, match cut) exists anywhere in the 36 Approved screenplays**, and two episodes explicitly reject fades in favor of hard cuts. Transition Authority is therefore governed as follows:
1. The default, Locked transition standard for this production is the **hard-cut tonal-break device** (already logged as FPP-AUD-014) — a direct cut with no rendered transition effect.
2. A transition other than a hard cut may be used only where a specific screenplay scene's own Editing Direction explicitly calls for it. No such instance is currently confirmed anywhere in the 36 Approved screenplays.
3. No transition effect (dissolve, wipe, fade, match cut, or any other rendered device) may be invented or introduced on editorial judgment alone. Per the Flag-Don't-Invent Rule already Locked at Phase 6H.0 (Amendment), any perceived creative need for a transition beyond the confirmed hard-cut standard must be flagged for Founder decision, never self-authorized.

---

## 6. Color Consistency Rule

Color across every assembled cut must remain consistent with:
- The Locked permanent three-color system (`color-language.md`, `global-consistency-rules.md`): cyan/icy-blue remains reserved exclusively for Fracture-anomaly phenomena, Kael's eye-glow, Cipher-work, and Breach residue across every cut it appears in — an edit may never introduce, extend, or grade this reserved family onto any other asset.
- Each governing Environment Production Package's own Color Philosophy and Lighting Rules/Lighting Philosophy fields (e.g., Night Haven's Phase 6C.7 Permanent Lighting Canon, Cassian's Private Interior's Phase 6C.6 Permanent Production Rule) — an edit that cuts between two environments must not introduce a color-grading choice that contradicts either environment's own confirmed register.
- `system-interference-visual-rules.md`'s physical-integration rule, carried forward from Phase 6H.0 §3–4: no edit may alter a composited VFX asset's own falloff/occlusion/shadow-casting behavior via color correction in a way that produces a flat, decorative glow.

No new color grading standard, LUT, or palette may be introduced at the editing stage. Color consistency is enforced, not created, at Phase 6I.

---

## 7. Final Timing Rule

Final cut timing/duration is governed by:
- The Audio Dialogue Authority's exact-text lock (Phase 6G.0 §2) — a scene's assembled duration must accommodate its Locked dialogue in full; no edit may compress, speed up, or truncate a locked line to fit a shorter timing.
- Each Animation Production Package's own Timing field — a motion, expression, or cycle may not be held for a duration its own governing package does not support, nor cut before its confirmed completion where continuity requires it (e.g., Kael's eye-glow state transitions, FPP-VFX-001, must be allowed to complete within a scene, not truncated mid-transition).
- Each screenplay's own Scene Count and scene-level pacing evidence — no scene-length or episode-length standard is invented at this stage; where a screenplay does not specify a scene's duration, that information is flagged as missing, not assumed.

No general episode-length or act-structure timing standard is established by this document. If one is required for production, it must be sourced from Locked Canon or flagged as missing — never invented at the editing stage.

---

## 8. Editing Revision Rule

Any revision to an Approved edit must:
- Reopen the existing edit entry, never overwrite it silently — preserving all prior Founder Approval as historical record, per the reopen-and-append method already established throughout this production.
- Cite the specific new source that justifies the revision (a corrected screenplay, an updated Storyboard/Animation/Audio/VFX Production Package, or a new Founder Directive).
- Carry its own new Founder Review, Alignment Audit, and Founder Approval before superseding the prior version.
- Never delete a prior version's record — mark it Superseded, consistent with the standing "nothing disappears without a record" practice.

---

## Version Control

Every Editing production document follows the same versioning discipline standing across this entire production: `v1.0` on first compilation, incremented major versions on Founder-directed content changes, changelog entries preserved in full at every version, and git commit/push performed separately for content-compilation versus Founder-approval steps wherever both occur in the same session.

---

## 9. Production Conflict Rule

If any Editing work finds:
- A contradiction between two Locked Canon sources,
- A contradiction between a screenplay's own Editing Direction and its governing Storyboard, Animation, Audio, or Visual Effects Production Package,
- An edit, transition, timing choice, or color treatment with no citable source anywhere in Editing, Assembly, Continuity, Transition, Color Consistency, or Final Timing Authority (Sections 2–7), or
- Any indication of repository corruption or a missing Locked authority,

production on that specific edit stops, and the conflict is flagged for Founder decision — exactly as the Lighting Canon rule and every prior Phase 6 Production Conflict Rule already require in their own domains. No conflict of this kind may be silently resolved by Editing-stage inference, convenience, or pacing preference. This includes, without exception, any apparent creative need to add a transition, timing choice, or color treatment the governing sources do not confirm — such a need is always flagged, never self-authorized.

## Amendment — Explicit Editing Restriction List

*(Appended 2026-07-18 per the reissued "Founder Directive — Phase 6I.0 Editing Production Governance (v1.0)," which explicitly enumerates seven categories editing may never alter and explicitly requires flagging both missing information and production conflicts. Everything above this line is preserved unmodified, per the Editing Revision Rule's own reopen-and-append method, Section 8.)*

### Finding

The original document's nine sections already ground Dialogue (§2.4, §7), Scene Order (§3), Canon (header, Purpose), and Approved Timing (§7) as protected against alteration, each tied to a specific cited authority. Three items the reissued directive names explicitly — **Story**, **Character Intent**, and **Approved Pacing** — were not previously named as their own standalone restriction anywhere in this document, even though they are consistent with (and do not extend beyond) the editing-is-assembly-only principle already established in Section 1 (Purpose) and Section 2 (Editing Authority). This amendment closes that naming gap; it authorizes no new editing behavior and narrows nothing already Locked.

### Editing Restriction List (new, standalone; supplements Sections 2–7, does not replace any of them)

Editing may never alter any of the following. Each restriction is enforcement-only, citing an authority already established above or in prior Locked Canon:

1. **Story** — the narrative content and events of each Approved Screenplay are fixed by Locked Canon and the Production Bible; editing may resequence *presentation* only where Section 3 (Assembly Authority) already permits, and may never add, remove, or alter a story event itself. This is the same "never rewrite screenplays" limit already stated in this document's Status line, restated here as its own standing restriction.
2. **Canon** — no assembled edit may contradict or modify Locked Canon (World Bible, Character Bible, Faction Bible), consistent with this document's Absolute Authority chain.
3. **Dialogue** — governed by §2.4 and §7: the Audio Dialogue Authority's exact-text lock; no edit may truncate, overlap, reorder, or paraphrase a Locked line.
4. **Scene order** — governed by §3: assembly may not reorder scenes beyond what an Approved Screenplay itself specifies.
5. **Character intent** — each character's Locked characterization (Character Bible; each Character Production Package's own Personality/Internal Conflict fields) governs the intent behind every line and action; editing may not recut footage in a way that reverses, undercuts, or reassigns a character's confirmed intent in a scene (e.g., assembling reaction shots out of their governing Storyboard/Animation Package's own sequence in a way that changes what a character is understood to mean or want). Where an edit's effect on character intent is ambiguous, it is flagged under the Production Conflict Rule (§9), never resolved by editorial judgment.
6. **Approved timing** — governed by §7 (Final Timing Rule): no edit may compress, extend, or truncate timing beyond what the governing Animation, Audio, or screenplay source supports.
7. **Approved pacing** — the relative rhythm and sequencing established collectively by each scene's Approved Storyboard Package (Camera Language, Composition Rules), Animation Package (Timing), and Audio Package (Continuity, music/dialogue placement) is fixed; editing may not re-pace an assembled sequence (e.g., by shortening held beats, accelerating cross-cut rhythm, or altering the confirmed escalation structure such as FPP-SB-002's Episode 9–11 structural echo) beyond what those governing packages already define.

### Flagging Restated (explicit, standalone)

Per the reissued directive's explicit two-part instruction, this document already requires both, and both are restated here as standing, co-equal obligations, not one subsuming the other:
- **Flag missing information** — wherever Editing, Assembly, Continuity, Transition, Color Consistency, or Final Timing Authority (Sections 2–7) is silent or incomplete for a given edit, that gap is flagged and production on that edit stops, per the flag-don't-invent discipline already Locked at Phase 6H.0 and carried forward throughout this document.
- **Flag production conflicts** — governed in full by Section 9 (Production Conflict Rule); restated here as its own explicit obligation per the reissued directive's own phrasing, not merely folded into the missing-information flag.
