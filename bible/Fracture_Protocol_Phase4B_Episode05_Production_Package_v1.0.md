## 1. Episode Identity

- **Episode number:** 5
- **Episode title:** "What Mira Carries"
- **Core purpose:** Surface Mira's backstory (Phase 4A, Episode 5, Purpose).
- **Narrative objective:** Deepen the Kael/Mira relationship and the Kael/Titan's-legacy relationship at once, through the same revelation (Phase 4A, Episode 5, Character Progression).
- **Emotional objective:** Trust made costly — Mira chooses to share something that changes how Kael sees both her and Titan, and the audience should feel the weight of that choice as hers, not something extracted from her.
- **Audience experience goal:** This episode should feel like an act of trust, not an act of exposure. Mira controls the telling throughout; nothing about her history is dramatized without her framing it herself.

---

## 2. Story Structure

- **Opening hook:** A moment of ordinary trust between Kael and Mira deepening (Phase 4A, Episode 5, Beginning).
- **Setup:** Something in the quiet moment — not a crisis, not a prompt from Kael — creates the space for Mira to choose to share more of herself than she has before.
- **Rising conflict:** None external. The tension is entirely Mira's own choice to speak, and Kael's growing awareness that what he's about to learn will change something in how he understands both her and Titan.
- **Character turning point:** Mira's decision to share her displacement backstory — an active choice, framed as hers to make, not a secret pulled from her (Phase 2E, Mira's Emotional Continuity requirement).
- **Major reveal or progression:** Mira's displacement and Titan's posthumous connection to it become known to Kael (Phase 4A, Episode 5, Central Conflict; Character Bible, "Her Defining Tragedy," "Relationship with Titan's Legacy"). The Light 98th's institutional history — including its one failure — becomes visible for the first time this season (Phase 4A, Episode 5, World Progression).
- **Climax:** Not a confrontation — the moment Kael learns Titan himself was personally part of the response that arrived too late for her.
- **Closing emotional beat:** Kael, for the first time, questions what "protection" actually means to the person he loves (Phase 4A, Episode 5, Ending) — not resolved, carried forward.
- **Cliffhanger / Transition:** Directly into Titan's legacy (Phase 4A, Episode 5, Transition) — tonal, not a plot cliffhanger.

## 3. Scene Breakdown

*Five scenes proposed, sized for Studio OS's Mainline Episode runtime target. Mira's history is conveyed entirely through her own telling — no scene dramatizes the historical occupation directly or invents specifics beyond what Character Bible already establishes (Lunar-born; civilian reconstruction specialist; Mid Zone resident; displaced when an Ascendant Doctrine occupation offered her home protection only in exchange for submission; the Light 98th, including Titan personally, arrived too late).*

**Scene 1 — An Ordinary Evening**
- Purpose: Establish the quiet, trust-deepening moment the episode opens on.
- Location: Mid Zone or Ward Station Seven — a shared, informal space.
- Characters present: Kael, Mira.
- Emotional state: Warm, unguarded — a continuation of their established relationship, not a new beginning.
- Conflict: None.
- Visual requirements: Existing Kael and Mira reference sheets; existing Mid Zone/Ward Station Seven environment assets.
- Audio requirements: Easy, low-key dialogue — intent only.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 2 — The Choice to Speak**
- Purpose: Mira decides to share more of herself than she has before.
- Location: Same as Scene 1.
- Characters present: Kael, Mira.
- Emotional state: Mira — deliberate, a little vulnerable, entirely in control of the moment; Kael — attentive, not pressing.
- Conflict: None external — the "conflict," such as it is, belongs entirely to Mira's own decision.
- Visual requirements: Same assets; closer, quieter framing per Art Bible `cinematic-mood-guide.md`.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should show Mira initiating, not Kael asking — she leads this scene.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 3 — What Happened**
- Purpose: Mira tells Kael about her displacement.
- Location: Same as Scenes 1–2.
- Characters present: Kael, Mira (as the scene's only speakers; the historical event itself is described, not dramatized).
- Emotional state: Mira — steady, telling her own story on her own terms, not reliving it as a passive victim; grief present but not defining. Kael — listening fully, without interrupting to comfort or fix.
- Conflict: The material itself is heavy (an Ascendant Doctrine occupation offering protection only in exchange for submission), but the scene's own conflict is Mira's act of telling it, not a reenactment of the event.
- Visual requirements: No flashback dramatization is proposed here — Mira's telling carries the scene visually through performance, not through a reenacted historical sequence, keeping the emphasis on her agency in the present rather than her past as spectacle. If a future production pass wants a restrained visual echo, it must not invent any detail beyond what's already Locked, and must not center her as a passive figure within it.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent only; her account should include her own reconstruction and survival, not only the loss — consistent with "someone who rebuilt, not someone who was rescued."
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 4 — Titan's Name**
- Purpose: Land the episode's specific revelation — Titan's personal connection.
- Location: Same as Scenes 1–3.
- Characters present: Kael, Mira.
- Emotional state: Mira — matter-of-fact, not delivering this as a twist but as a fact she has long since made peace with; Kael — visibly recontextualizing what he thought he knew about Titan.
- Conflict: Kael learns the Light 98th, including Titan personally, arrived too late for her (Character Bible, "Relationship with Titan's Legacy") — his own hero-worship of Titan's legend meets a real, human cost for the first time.
- Visual requirements: Same assets; Kael's reaction should be readable but restrained — this is not the episode where he processes it fully (that begins Episode 6).
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should keep Mira in control of this disclosure too — she names the fact plainly, without performing forgiveness or anger for Kael's benefit.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 5 — What Protection Means**
- Purpose: Close the episode on Kael's unresolved question.
- Location: Same as Scenes 1–4, or a brief solitary beat for Kael alone.
- Characters present: Kael (Mira may remain present or the scene may isolate Kael briefly — either serves the beat).
- Emotional state: Kael — quiet, unsettled, turning the same idea over without resolving it.
- Conflict: None external — entirely internal, per the seed entry's own Ending note.
- Visual requirements: Same assets; a quieter close than the episode's earlier scenes.
- Audio requirements: Minimal dialogue or none — the episode should end on Kael's own unresolved thought, not a spoken conclusion.
- Production needs: No new asset.

## 4. Character Performance Planning

**Mira.**
- Motivations this episode: To be fully known by someone she trusts, on her own terms and in her own time — not compelled, not extracted (Character Bible, "Canon Alignment Principle," "exists on her own terms"; Phase 2C, Mira §2).
- Emotional progression: Warm ease (Scene 1) → deliberate vulnerability, self-directed (Scene 2) → steady, agency-centered telling (Scene 3) → matter-of-fact disclosure about Titan (Scene 4) → present throughout Scene 5, not the scene's focus.
- Dialogue intent, not lines: her account in Scene 3 should include what she rebuilt, not only what she lost — the telling itself should demonstrate the reconstruction-specialist identity that defines her, not just narrate a tragedy. In Scene 4, her disclosure about Titan should carry acceptance, not raw grief re-triggered — this is a fact she has lived with, not a wound freshly opened.
- Body language: grounded and present throughout, even in the hardest material — per Phase 2E's explicit requirement, nothing in her posture, framing, or blocking may present her as fragile or passive at any point in this episode. She should occupy the emotional center of Scenes 2–4 as the one in control of the room, not a subject being comforted.
- Relationship dynamics: with Kael, trust deepening toward friendship, not yet stated as love this season (Phase 2C, Mira §4); with Titan's legacy, her own connection is reaffirmed as predating and independent of her relationship with Kael — she is not telling this story only to inform Kael's arc.

**Kael.**
- Motivations this episode: To be trusted with something real, and to actually listen rather than react (Character Bible, "Relationship with Kael"; Phase 2C, Kael §4).
- Emotional progression: Warm and unguarded (Scene 1) → attentive (Scene 2) → fully present, not interrupting (Scene 3) → visibly recontextualizing his understanding of Titan (Scene 4) → quiet and unsettled, carrying an unanswered question (Scene 5).
- Dialogue intent, not lines: in Scene 3, his instinct to comfort or "fix" should be present but held back — per Mira's own Emotional Continuity requirement, the scene is not his to rescue. In Scene 4, his reaction should register as personal recontextualization of Titan's myth, not shock or blame directed at Mira.
- Body language: increasingly still and focused through Scenes 2–4, ending in Scene 5 on a quality of unresolved turning-over, not distress — per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement for Kael, his burden-alone flaw is not this episode's subject, but nothing here should show him resolving any part of it either.
- Relationship dynamics: with Mira, this episode is a direct deepening of trust; with Titan's legacy, this is the first time this season Titan becomes personally, specifically connected to someone Kael loves, setting up Episode 6's direct engagement with his legacy.

## 6. Audio Planning

- **Narration needs:** None.
- **Dialogue priorities:** Scenes 2 through 4 carry this episode's entire dialogue weight; Scene 1 is warm and low-key, Scene 5 minimal or silent.
- **Sound design requirements:** Quiet, intimate ambience throughout — per Phase 3A's routing to Production OS's Music Production SOP and Studio OS's Music & Audio Standards.
- **Music tone:** Restrained and warm, never swelling into melodrama during Mira's account — the material should be allowed to carry its own weight without the score underlining it.
- **Emotional timing:** No musical cue should resolve Kael's Scene 5 question, and none should recast Mira's account as tragedy-for-effect — the score should track her steadiness, not her loss.
