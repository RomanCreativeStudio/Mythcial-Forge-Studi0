# THE FRACTURE PROTOCOL — PHASE 6E.0: STORYBOARD PRODUCTION GOVERNANCE

**Classification:** Internal — Governance Document (**Approved**)
**Status:** Compiled 2026-07-15 per "Founder Directive — Phase 6E.0 Storyboard Production Governance (v1.0)." Continues directly from Phase 6D.1.5. This document creates no story content, modifies no canon, and rewrites no screenplay. It establishes only the new governance Phase 6E (Storyboard Production) requires — it does not restate the Environment (Phase 6C), Prop & Technology (Phase 6D.0), or any prior governance already Locked; those remain in force, unmodified, and are cited by reference only.
**Absolute authority:** subordinate to Master System Prompt → Locked Canon (World Bible, Character Bible, Faction Bible) → Production Bible → Approved Screenplays → Art Bible (`camera-language.md`, `cinematic-mood-guide.md`, `lighting-system.md`, `color-language.md`) → Phase 6B Character Production line → Phase 6C Environment Production Canon and its full reconciliation lineage → Phase 6D.0/6D.1/6D.1.5 Prop & Technology Production → **Phase 6E.0 Storyboard Production Governance**.

---

## 1. Purpose

Phase 6E exists to translate each Approved screenplay's own scene-level Camera Notes, Camera Direction, and Performance Notes into storyboard panels — the first visual-sequencing step, distinct from and prerequisite to animation production. Phase 6E.0 establishes only the rules that govern this translation. It authorizes no storyboard itself.

---

## 2. Storyboard Authority

A storyboard panel's content is authorized only by:
1. The specific screenplay scene it depicts — its own Scene Number, Location, Action, Dialogue, Camera Notes, and Lighting Notes fields, cited directly.
2. That scene's governing Environment Production Package (Section 6's Camera Language and Lighting Philosophy fields) and, where the scene involves a prop or technology asset, its Phase 6D entry.
3. Each present character's own Approved Character Production Package (Phase 6B) — physical description, silhouette, expression/pose library, and costume.

A storyboard panel depicting anything not present in one of these three sources — a new character action, a new prop, a new piece of blocking, a new emotional beat — is not a storyboard of the screenplay; it is new story content, and is prohibited under Section 9, below. Where a screenplay's own Camera Notes are genuinely silent on a shot (several Environment Packages already document this — e.g., "no environment-specific camera instruction found," Night Haven, Doctrine Institutional Environment), the storyboard artist may apply the Art Bible's own general Lens Philosophy defaults (`camera-language.md`) as reasonable interpretation, exactly as those Environment Packages' own Camera Language fields already do — this is applying an existing Locked default, not inventing one.

---

## 3. Shot Authority

Every storyboard panel is one shot. A shot's authority is the scene it belongs to, not an independent creative unit:
- Shot count and sequence within a scene must be traceable to that scene's own Camera Notes (e.g., Episode 1's confirmed progression: "wide, unhurried framing (Scene 1) → handheld-adjacent, populated-space framing (Scene 2) → the episode's first close-up coverage (Scene 3)...").
- Where a scene's Camera Notes describe a progression across sub-beats within a single scene (as in the example above), each described beat may become its own panel or panel sequence — this is decomposition of an already-Approved camera progression, not invention of new shots.
- No shot may be added that depicts action, dialogue, or a beat not present in the screenplay's own text.

---

## 4. Camera Authority

All lens, framing, and movement choices in a storyboard panel must trace to one of:
- The scene's own explicit Camera Notes / Camera Direction text.
- The Art Bible's `camera-language.md` general Lens Philosophy and use-case defaults (e.g., the Locked "landing an emotional beat" push-in use case, already cited in Environment Production Packages such as Cassian's Private Interior and Kael's Transitional/Institutional Space).
- An Environment Production Package's own confirmed Camera Language field, where the scene's environment has one.

Where these sources conflict, the more specific source governs: a scene's own explicit Camera Notes outrank its Environment Package's general Camera Language field, which in turn outranks the Art Bible's general defaults. This mirrors the same specificity-first precedence already applied throughout the Lighting Canon determinations in the Phase 6C.5–6C.7 reconciliation lineage.

---

## 5. Composition Rule

Panel composition (framing, headroom, negative space, character placement within frame) must be consistent with:
- The scene's own confirmed Camera Notes (e.g., "no visual coding tilts either man as dominant," the Formal Meeting Environment's own Locked neutrality mandate).
- Each present character's Approved silhouette and proportions (Phase 6B), so that a character's on-panel scale and posture never contradicts their own Character Production Package.
- The Locked permanent three-color system and the Art Bible's Environmental Storytelling rules for the scene's environment, where a layer is confirmed.

Composition choices are never used to introduce a visual meaning the screenplay's own Performance Notes do not support — e.g., a composition may not code a character as dominant, villainous, or diminished where the governing screenplay explicitly forbids it (the Formal Meeting Environment and Neutral Private Meeting Environment's own Locked neutrality mandates being the clearest existing examples).

---

## 6. Character Blocking Rule

Character blocking (position, movement, and spatial relationship within a scene) must derive from:
- The scene's own Action text and Performance Notes.
- Each character's own confirmed Population Density and Asset Dependencies fields in the scene's governing Environment Production Package (e.g., Cassian's Private Interior's confirmed "zero co-occupancy... every scene is solo Cassian").
- Any explicit blocking instruction already on record (e.g., the Formal Meeting Environment's "eye-level framing... no visual coding tilts either man as dominant," or the Guardian's own Locked "held two-shot" camera default from Phase 6B.1).

No storyboard may block a character into a scene they are not confirmed present in, or remove a character confirmed present, per the scene's own Characters Present field. No storyboard may invent a new physical action (a gesture, a movement, contact between characters) beyond what the screenplay's own Action text and Performance Notes describe or reasonably imply through ordinary staging.

---

## 7. Continuity Rule

Every storyboard must remain consistent with:
- The character's own established silhouette, costume, and any confirmed Signal/schedule-based physical state (e.g., Cassian's exact five-stage unraveling schedule, Episodes 28/29/30/31/35; Kael's Locked eye-glow).
- The environment's own confirmed architecture, materials, lighting register, and any Permanent Production Rule or Permanent Lighting Canon governing it (Cassian's Private Interior's Permanent Production Rule; Night Haven's Permanent Lighting Canon).
- The scene's own transition instructions to and from adjacent scenes, so that a storyboard sequence reads as continuous with the scenes immediately before and after it, per each screenplay's own Transition field.

Any apparent inconsistency between two Approved sources (a screenplay and its own Environment Package, or two adjacent scenes) must be flagged for Founder decision under the Production Conflict Rule (Section 11, below), never silently resolved by the storyboard artist's own judgment.

---

## 8. Cinematic Consistency Rule

Across a full episode, and across episodes within the same movement or arc, storyboards must maintain:
- A consistent visual register for each recurring environment, per that environment's own confirmed Lighting Philosophy/Lighting Rules and any Lighting Canon Determination already on record (A — intentional, with its documented rule; or B — flagged contradiction, not adjudicated by the storyboard artist).
- A consistent application of the Art Bible's three Emotional Lighting Modes (Grounded/Charged/Wounded, `lighting-system.md`) — chosen by story beat, per that Locked rule, never by storyboard convenience.
- Visual continuity for each recurring character consistent with their own Approved Character Production Package across every episode they appear in.

This rule exists specifically to prevent the kind of unexamined drift the Phase 6C.5–6C.7 reconciliation lineage found and had to correct after the fact (e.g., Night Haven's original uncaught lighting contradiction) — storyboard production is the first stage where such drift is visually checkable, and this rule requires it be checked at that stage, not deferred.

---

## 9. Storyboard Revision Rule

Any revision to an Approved storyboard sequence must:
- Reopen the existing storyboard entry, never overwrite it silently — preserving all prior Founder Approval as historical record, per the reopen-and-append method already established throughout Phase 6C.5 onward.
- Cite the specific new source that justifies the revision (a corrected screenplay, an updated Environment or Character Package, or a new Founder Directive).
- Carry its own new Founder Review, Alignment Audit, and Founder Approval before superseding the prior version.
- Never delete a prior version's record — mark it Superseded, consistent with the standing "nothing disappears without a record" practice.

---

## 10. Version Control

Every storyboard production document follows the same versioning discipline standing across this entire production: `v1.0` on first compilation, incremented major versions on Founder-directed content changes, changelog entries preserved in full at every version, and git commit/push performed separately for content-compilation versus Founder-approval steps wherever both occur in the same session.

---

## 11. Production Conflict Rule

If any storyboard work finds:
- A contradiction between two Locked Canon sources,
- A contradiction between a screenplay's own Camera/Lighting Notes and its governing Environment Production Package,
- A shot, blocking choice, or composition with no citable source anywhere in Storyboard Authority (Section 2), or
- Any indication of repository corruption or a missing Locked authority,

production on that specific panel or sequence stops, and the conflict is flagged for Founder decision — exactly as the Lighting Canon rule and the Phase 6D.0 Production Conflict Rule already require in their own domains. No conflict of this kind may be silently resolved by storyboard-stage inference, convenience, or default.
