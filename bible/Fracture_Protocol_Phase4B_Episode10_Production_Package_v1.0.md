## Production Notes and Scope Flags (read before the sections below)

- **This episode does not deliver the loss.** Per Phase 4A's own seed entry, the outcome is left uncertain going into the finale block — the actual cost is Episode 11's scope exclusively. Nothing below resolves whether the at-risk civilian survives.
- **No new named character is introduced.** The person put at direct risk this episode is deliberately unnamed, consistent with the pattern established since Episode 2 (the Episode 11 loss is Locked as "not a core-cast character").
- **Mira's role this episode is witness, not victim, and not rescuer.** She is present in her established civilian-reconstruction capacity — a reasonable, already-Locked-consistent reason for her to be near an active operation site — not repositioned as a combatant or a passive bystander. Per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement, nothing below may define her by rescue or victimhood; she remains an active, capable professional throughout, even while witnessing something difficult.
- **The Guardian, Cassian, and Night Haven do not appear.** None are named in Phase 4A's Episode 10 seed entry; their absence here is scope-correct, not an omission.
- **Signal/Cipher-work/Fragment-Sync remain untouched.** The escalated operation is written entirely in institutional/tactical terms, preserving the still-open Signal Profile Deferral Lock.
- **"Light 98th and Ward Division command begin taking notice"** is staged as institutional awareness rising, not a confrontation or a formal reprimand — that escalation belongs to Episode 11 and 12.

## 1. Episode Identity

- **Episode number:** 10
- **Episode title:** "The Second Operation, Part Two"
- **Core purpose:** Escalate the operation toward its cost (Phase 4A, Episode 10, Purpose).
- **Narrative objective:** Compound the danger established in Episode 9 to the point that a specific, unnamed person under Kael's protection is placed at direct risk by his continued refusal to ask for help (Phase 4A, Episode 10, Central Conflict).
- **Emotional objective:** Mounting dread that never resolves — the audience should feel the operation tipping toward disaster without the episode confirming it.
- **Audience experience goal:** This episode should feel like the second half of a held breath — everything Episode 9 set in motion now visibly compounding, ending on real uncertainty rather than a cliffhanger trick.

## 2. Story Structure

- **Opening hook:** Direct continuation from Episode 9 — the operation's danger compounds (Phase 4A, Episode 10, Beginning).
- **Setup:** The operation's growing scale draws Mira into its edges through her own reconstruction/civilian-support work, positioning her to witness what follows.
- **Rising conflict:** A specific, unnamed civilian under Kael's protection is placed at direct risk, a direct consequence of his continued refusal to call for backup (Phase 4A, Episode 10, Central Conflict).
- **Character turning point:** Mira witnesses the pattern building toward its worst outcome for the first time this season — not the loss itself, but its shape becoming visible to her (Phase 4A, Episode 10, Character Progression).
- **Major reveal or progression:** None new — this episode compounds already-established stakes rather than introducing new lore.
- **World Progression beat:** Light 98th and Ward Division command begin taking notice of the operation's scale — institutional awareness rising, not yet formal action (Phase 4A, Episode 10, World Progression).
- **Climax:** Not resolved — the operation's danger peaks within the episode, but its outcome is deliberately left open.
- **Closing emotional beat:** Uncertainty, held rather than resolved (Phase 4A, Episode 10, Ending).
- **Cliffhanger / Transition:** Into the loss itself — Episode 11's scope exclusively (Phase 4A, Episode 10, Transition).

## 3. Scene Breakdown

*Five scenes proposed, sized for Studio OS's Mainline Episode runtime target. The episode is built to compound tension without resolving it — no scene may confirm the at-risk civilian's fate.*

**Scene 1 — Compounding**
- Purpose: Continue directly from Episode 9's ending.
- Location: Operation site (established in Episode 9).
- Characters present: Kael; response personnel.
- Emotional state: Sustained urgency, escalating from Episode 9's resolve.
- Conflict: The operation's danger grows in scale, consistent with Episode 9's own World Progression note.
- Visual requirements: Same operation-site asset as Episode 9, escalated intensity.
- Audio requirements: Continuous, rising sound design — intent only.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 2 — Mira, at the Edge**
- Purpose: Bring Mira into the episode through her established professional role.
- Location: The operation site's civilian/displacement perimeter.
- Characters present: Mira; unnamed displaced or at-risk civilians she is assisting.
- Emotional state: Focused, competent — Mira in her element, doing reconstruction/support work under pressure.
- Conflict: The operation's scale is affecting the civilians she's there to help.
- Visual requirements: A civilian-support perimeter dressing of the existing operation-site asset — no new location category.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should show her working, not waiting — an active professional, not a bystander.
- Production needs: New perimeter/civilian-support dressing; checked against the Asset Registry before build.

**Scene 3 — Someone in Danger**
- Purpose: Establish the specific person placed at direct risk.
- Location: Operation site.
- Characters present: Kael; an unnamed civilian under his protection, now in direct danger.
- Emotional state: Kael — focused, still confident; the audience should read the danger before he fully registers it.
- Conflict: The civilian's risk is a direct consequence of Kael's continued refusal to call for backup (Phase 4A, Episode 10, Central Conflict).
- Visual requirements: Same operation-site asset.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should stay procedural — no melodrama, consistent with how the season has handled danger throughout.
- Production needs: No new named-character asset; the at-risk civilian remains generic and unnamed.

**Scene 4 — Witnessed**
- Purpose: Land the episode's central beat — Mira sees the pattern.
- Location: A vantage point at the edge of the operation, where Mira's civilian-support position allows her to observe Kael's choices directly.
- Characters present: Mira (observing); Kael (at a distance, not directly interacting with her this scene).
- Emotional state: Mira — growing recognition, not panic; she is processing what she's seeing with the same competence she brings to everything else.
- Conflict: None direct between them — the conflict is entirely what she's witnessing.
- Visual requirements: Staging should make clear she sees the specific choice-point (Kael continuing without support) — a visual echo of the pattern the audience has already tracked since Episode 2.
- Audio requirements: Minimal dialogue; this scene should largely carry through Mira's own performance, per Phase 2E's requirement that she remain an active observer, not a passive one.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 5 — Notice, and No Answer**
- Purpose: Close the episode on institutional awareness and unresolved outcome.
- Location: Split between the operation site and a Ward Station Seven communications/command context.
- Characters present: Unnamed Light 98th/Ward Division command personnel registering the operation's scale; Kael and the at-risk civilian remain in the operation site, outcome unresolved.
- Emotional state: Rising institutional concern, cut against continued operational tension.
- Conflict: Command begins taking notice — an institutional beat, not a confrontation.
- Visual requirements: A brief Ward Station Seven communications-context dressing, alongside the continuing operation-site footage.
- Audio requirements: The episode should end without a resolving cue — no confirmation, in either direction, of the civilian's fate.
- Production needs: Minor Ward Station Seven communications-context dressing; checked against the Asset Registry before build.

## 4. Character Performance Planning

**Kael.**
- Motivations this episode: The same motivation as Episodes 2 and 9 — protect the people in front of him — now compounding under mounting pressure (Character Bible, "Leadership Philosophy").
- Emotional progression: Sustained urgency (Scene 1) → continued confidence (Scene 3) → unaware of Mira's specific vantage (Scene 4) → operation tension held, unresolved (Scene 5).
- Dialogue intent, not lines: his choices in Scene 3 should read as consistent with Episodes 2 and 9 — not a new decision, the same one made again under worse conditions. Per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement, nothing here may show him hesitating in a way that resolves the flaw before Episode 11's actual cost.
- Body language: focused, still outwardly capable — the audience's dread should come from familiarity with the pattern, not from any visible crack in his composure this episode.
- Relationship dynamics: he does not interact directly with Mira this episode — her witnessing happens at a distance, which is itself part of the beat (she sees him without his knowledge, a different register than their established direct relationship).

**Mira.**
- Motivations this episode: To do her own work — supporting displaced/at-risk civilians — which places her, by her own professional presence, in a position to witness Kael's choices (Character Bible, "Who She Is," civilian reconstruction specialist).
- Emotional progression: Focused competence (Scene 2) → growing, processed recognition (Scene 4).
- Dialogue intent, not lines: her reaction to what she witnesses should be internal and controlled, not expository — she does not narrate her own realization aloud.
- Body language: per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement, she must read as active and capable throughout, including in Scene 4 — witnessing something difficult does not make her passive; she remains a professional doing her job while something personally significant registers alongside it.
- Relationship dynamics: this is the first time this season she observes Kael's flaw directly rather than hearing about it or experiencing its effects on their relationship — a distinct beat from her Episode 5 disclosure or her Episode 7–8 warmth with him.
