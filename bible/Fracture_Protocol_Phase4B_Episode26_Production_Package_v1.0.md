## Production Notes and Scope Flags (read before the sections below)

- **This is the reciprocal mechanism, not a Titan biography episode.** Titan does not appear as a character; this is Kael and Mira processing his legacy together, using only what each of them already, separately, Locked-knows about him.
- **The exchange must run both directions.** Mira gives Kael grounded, human evidence of the man; Kael's presence and his own reckoning with Titan's flaw gives Mira room to confront her own unresolved feelings. Neither is a passive audience for the other's revelation.
- **No new fact about Titan is introduced.** Every detail used below is already Locked in the Character Bible or Phase 2C; nothing here expands his biography, his death, or his relationship history beyond what already exists.
- **This does not resolve Kael's burden-alone flaw.** The "absorbing" of the gap between man and myth is realized here ahead of its full fruition in the finale (Episode 35) — it is not the flaw's resolution itself.
- **No other character or thread advances.** Cassian, the Guardian, and Mira's institutional/reconstruction work are not part of this seed entry and do not appear.

## 1. Episode Identity

- **Episode number:** 26
- **Episode title:** Placeholder only, not final — "Both of Them at Once" (Phase 4A.3, Episode 26 seed entry).
- **Core purpose:** Deliver the reciprocal Kael/Mira mechanism resolving the myth-vs-man conflict around Titan's legacy (Phase 4A.3, Episode 26, Purpose).
- **Narrative objective:** Show Kael and Mira processing Titan's legacy together, not one-directionally, each giving the other something the other could not reach alone (Phase 2C, Titan §4).
- **Emotional objective:** Mutual, simultaneous vulnerability — two people confronting different sides of the same person's legacy at once, neither performing revelation for the other.
- **Audience experience goal:** The audience should feel the gap between the legend and the man narrow for Kael, and feel Mira's own long-carried, separate grief given room for the first time this saga.

## 2. Story Structure

- **Opening hook:** Kael still carrying Titan's legacy as an unresolved gap between the man and the myth; Mira still carrying her own unresolved feelings toward that legacy, separate from her feelings for Kael (Phase 4A.3, Episode 26, Beginning State).
- **Setup:** A private moment between Kael and Mira, following directly from Episode 25's settled quiet, in which Titan's legacy surfaces naturally between them.
- **Rising conflict:** Mira shares her own grounded, human memory of the man — not a heroic anecdote, but an ordinary, imperfect one — giving Kael external, lived evidence of the person underneath the legend he's been carrying (Phase 2C, Titan §4).
- **Character turning point:** At the same moment, Kael's presence and his own reckoning with Titan's flaw (carrying every burden alone) gives Mira room to confront her own unresolved feelings toward Titan's legacy — knowing him as a person, and separately carrying the consequences of the institutional failure (the Light 98th arriving too late) that shaped her own life (Phase 2C, Titan §4).
- **Major reveal or progression:** No new fact about Titan is revealed — the progression is emotional, not informational; both characters leave the moment changed, neither a device for the other's arc (Phase 2C, Titan §4).
- **Climax:** The mutual, simultaneous nature of the exchange — both giving and receiving at once, not sequentially.
- **Closing emotional beat:** The gap between the man and the myth stops being something Kael must resolve and becomes something he has absorbed — realized here, ahead of its full fruition in the finale (Phase 4A.3, Episode 26, Ending Beat; Phase 2C, Titan §6).
- **Cliffhanger / Transition:** Into the three-faction rivalry's tension reaching its peak (Phase 4A.3, Episode 26, Transition) — not developed here, named only as the structural handoff to Episode 27.

## 3. Scene Breakdown

*Four scenes proposed, matching this season's typical scale for a character-focused, non-institutional episode — comparable to Episode 19's own four-scene structure. Sized for Studio OS's Mainline Episode runtime target.*

**Scene 1 — What's Still Unsettled**
- Purpose: Establish that both Kael and Mira are still separately carrying unresolved feelings about Titan, without yet naming them to each other.
- Location: A private, informal environment consistent with Kael and Mira's established relationship spaces (from Episode 19's quiet-beat environment).
- Characters present: Kael; Mira.
- Emotional state: Comfortable but carrying something unspoken — a quiet lead-in, not tension.
- Conflict: None yet external — the scene establishes the gap each of them still carries privately.
- Visual requirements: Reused environment from Episode 19; no new location.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should carry ease between them, with an undercurrent neither has voiced yet.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 2 — What Mira Remembers**
- Purpose: Deliver Mira's grounded, human memory of Titan.
- Location: Same environment as Scene 1, continuous.
- Characters present: Kael; Mira.
- Emotional state: Mira — vulnerable but grounded, sharing something ordinary rather than heroic.
- Conflict: Mira shares her own lived memory of Titan — not a heroic anecdote, but an ordinary, imperfect one — giving Kael external, lived evidence of the person underneath the legend (Phase 2C, Titan §4). No new biographical fact about Titan is introduced; only Mira's own already-Locked connection to him (the Light 98th response) is drawn on.
- Visual requirements: Same environment; no new location, no flashback imagery of Titan invented beyond what existing reference material already establishes.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should carry specificity and warmth, not eulogy.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 3 — What Kael Gives Back**
- Purpose: Deliver the reciprocal half of the exchange — Kael's presence and his own reckoning with Titan's flaw giving Mira room to confront her own unresolved feelings.
- Location: Same environment, continuous.
- Characters present: Kael; Mira.
- Emotional state: Kael — reflective, offering his own reckoning rather than comforting from a distance; Mira — open, confronting feelings she has carried separately from her feelings for Kael.
- Conflict: Kael's own recognition of Titan's defining flaw (carrying every burden alone) becomes the thing that lets Mira name her own unresolved feelings — about Titan as a person, and separately, about the institutional failure that shaped her own life (Phase 2C, Titan §4). Neither character is a device for the other's arc.
- Visual requirements: Same environment; no new location.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should keep both directions of the exchange legible — this is not Kael comforting Mira, nor Mira resolving Kael; both are changed simultaneously.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 4 — Absorbed, Not Answered**
- Purpose: Close the episode on the emotional landing point, without resolving Kael's overall flaw or advancing any other thread.
- Location: Same environment, continuous or a natural transition within it.
- Characters present: Kael; Mira.
- Emotional state: Settled, quietly changed — not resolved, not triumphant.
- Conflict: None new — the gap between the man and the myth becomes something Kael has absorbed rather than something still unresolved, realized here ahead of its full fruition in Episode 35 (Phase 2C, Titan §6). This is explicitly not Kael's burden-alone flaw resolving.
- Visual requirements: Same environment; no new location.
- Audio requirements: The episode should close on a quiet, settled register — no triumphant or fully-resolving musical cue.
- Production needs: No new asset.
