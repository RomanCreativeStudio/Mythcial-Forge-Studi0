### SCENE 2 — PRAISE

- **Scene Number:** 2
- **Scene Title:** Praise
- **INT./EXT.:** INT.
- **Location:** Ward Station Seven
- **Time of Day:** Later, same day
- **Story Purpose:** Dramatize the institutional approval Kael receives.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Warm, validating.
- **Characters Present:** Kael; unnamed Light Order-affiliated superiors/colleagues.
- **Required Assets:** Kael; Ward Station Seven.
- **Required Audio:** Dialogue carrying warmth and approval.
- **Required Visual Effects:** None.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Same day as Scene 1.

**ACTION**

A small gathering — not ceremonial, just people stopping by. Praise arrives naturally, in passing, never as a formal commendation. Every word is genuine. Not one word asks how the outcome was reached.

**DIALOGUE**

SUPERIOR
Twelve civilians clear because you didn't wait for backup that would've cost you six more minutes. That's the job, Kael.

KAEL
*(quiet pride, no hesitation)*
Anyone would've done the same.

SUPERIOR
*(warm, meaning it as praise)*
Not everyone would've pushed that far. That's the difference.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

Play this entirely sincerely — the superior believes every word, and by the surface logic of the scene, is right. The dissonance belongs to the audience alone; no character carries it.

**CAMERA NOTES**

Warm, close coverage — the scene should feel good to watch, which is the point.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Ward Station Seven's warmest register this episode.

**SOUND NOTES**

No danger cue of any kind, per Section 6's explicit instruction.

**TRANSITION**

Cut away from Ward Station Seven to the Lower Sector.

### SCENE 3 — AMONG THE DISPLACED

- **Scene Number:** 3
- **Scene Title:** Among the Displaced
- **INT./EXT.:** EXT.
- **Location:** Lower Sector, among displaced survivors from Episode 2's crisis
- **Time of Day:** Same day
- **Story Purpose:** Open the episode's second thread — the Ascendant Doctrine's recruiting method.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Vulnerable, uncertain.
- **Characters Present:** Unnamed displaced civilians; the unnamed Ascendant Doctrine opportunist. Kael does not appear.
- **Required Assets:** Doctrine opportunist; Lower Sector (displaced-civilian dressing).
- **Required Audio:** Quiet, measured dialogue — reasonable, not villainous.
- **Required Visual Effects:** None.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Same displaced population from Episode 2's crisis; no character overlap with Scenes 1–2, 4–5.

**ACTION**

A temporary shelter space, still disordered from the prior day's crisis. Civilians wait, uncertain, some visibly shaken. The OPPORTUNIST moves among them without urgency — offering water, a seat, small practical help before ever speaking to the point.

**DIALOGUE**

OPPORTUNIST
*(quiet, to a civilian, handing over water)*
You don't have to have an answer for what happens next. Most people don't, right after.

DISPLACED CIVILIAN
*(hollow)*
It could happen again tomorrow. It could happen again in an hour.

OPPORTUNIST
*(not dismissive, genuinely sympathetic)*
It could. That's the trouble with leaving it to chance, isn't it. One person, however good, deciding who gets there in time and who doesn't.
*(beat)*
There are people working on a version of this where nobody has to depend on chance. I'm not going to pretend I can explain all of it standing here. But if you ever want to hear more, we're not hard to find.

The civilian doesn't answer. Doesn't refuse, either. The opportunist doesn't push — moves on to the next person.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

The opportunist must read as calm, patient, and genuinely kind in manner — the danger is entirely in what the appeal offers, never in how it's delivered. No menace, no urgency, no pressure. Per the Faction Bible's own characterization, this figure isn't wrong that the crisis was frightening and unpredictable — the audience should understand exactly why this appeal could land.

**CAMERA NOTES**

More exposed, handheld-adjacent framing than the Ward Station Seven scenes — a rawer visual register.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

More exposed and uncertain than Ward Station Seven's warmth, within existing Lower Sector zone-identity rules.

**SOUND NOTES**

Quiet, no score — let the dialogue carry the scene in near-silence.

**TRANSITION**

Cut back to Ward Station Seven.

That's exactly what I told them.

This line should land as the episode's quietest, most dangerous moment — the flaw being actively taught to others as a standard, delivered with total warmth and sincerity.

Slightly more formal framing than Scene 2, reflecting the senior figure's weight.

**SOUND NOTES**

Still no danger cue.
