## 4. Production Notes

**Canon requirements (mandatory):**
- Kael's burden-alone flaw must be dramatized through action, not stated aloud (Phase 2A.2, Section 1) — no line of dialogue may have him articulate the belief that he must act alone.
- The refusal of backup (Scene 4) must read with the intent "I can still do this," never as an explicit statement of his underlying belief — per the seed package's own explicit Dialogue intent field.
- The Ascendant Doctrine's Scene 3 presence is background-only, unpersonified, and not interacted with directly — no named individual, no dialogue, no expansion beyond what the seed package's own World Progression field permits.
- Mira does not appear this episode, consistent with the seed package's own character focus.
- Kael's physical strain must build visibly from Scene 4 onward, not appear suddenly at Scene 5 — a readable through-line.
- The episode must close on vulnerability, not triumph (Scene 6) — no musical or dialogue beat may resolve the cost as a victory.

**Directorial interpretation (non-binding):** exact blocking of the crisis-site geography, specific reaction shots, and pacing within each scene are proposed staging.

**Animation guidance:** one character-state variant (Kael, strain/injury) and one scaled visual effect are the episode's only new production elements relative to Episode 1; both flagged in Section 5.

**Audio guidance:** see Sections 10/11.

**Optional production suggestions:** none flagged.

## 9. Full Screenplay

### SCENE 1 — ALARM

- **Scene Number:** 1
- **Scene Title:** Alarm
- **INT./EXT.:** INT.
- **Location:** Ward Station Seven
- **Time of Day:** Morning
- **Story Purpose:** Open on the crisis beginning — mobilization, not explanation.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Alert, focused, no panic.
- **Characters Present:** Kael; unnamed Ward Division and Light 98th personnel.
- **Required Assets:** Kael; Ward Station Seven.
- **Required Audio:** Urgent but controlled sound design.
- **Required Visual Effects:** Alarm/warning cues per `system-interference-visual-rules.md`.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Opens directly from Episode 1's Scene 5 disturbance, per both packages' Transition fields.

**ACTION**

An alarm — measured, not shrieking. Ward Station Seven shifts from routine to mobilization in seconds. Kael is already moving before most others have processed the alert; not surprise, recognition. Personnel converge, fall into practiced formation.

**DIALOGUE**

COORDINATOR (V.O., over comm)
Breach-adjacent activity, Lower Sector, confirmed escalating. All available Ward and 98th, mobilize.

KAEL
*(already moving, to a colleague falling in beside him)*
Same sector as yesterday's flicker.

COLLEAGUE
Bigger, this time.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

No panic anywhere in this scene — the entire station's competence should read as the payoff of Episode 1's "nothing happened, which is the good kind of shift" now being tested for the first time.

**CAMERA NOTES**

Kinetic but controlled — moving with the mobilization, not chaotic handheld.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Per `lighting-system.md` — the station's warm register from Episode 1 shifts cooler and more urgent, without losing its lived-in quality.

**SOUND NOTES**

Alarm tone, then rising ambient urgency underneath dialogue.

**TRANSITION**

Cut to the response team in transit.

### SCENE 2 — DEPLOYMENT

- **Scene Number:** 2
- **Scene Title:** Deployment
- **INT./EXT.:** EXT.
- **Location:** Transit from Ward Station Seven into the Lower Sector crisis site
- **Time of Day:** Morning
- **Story Purpose:** Establish Kael embedded in the joint response.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Focused, professional.
- **Characters Present:** Kael; unnamed joint response team.
- **Required Assets:** Kael; Lower Sector.
- **Required Audio:** Minimal, coordination-focused dialogue.
- **Required Visual Effects:** None.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Direct continuation from Scene 1.

**ACTION**

Fast, purposeful transit through the Lower Sector. The team moves as a unit; Kael's place within it is unremarkable — one competent person among several, not singled out yet.

**DIALOGUE**

TEAM LEAD
*(brief, over the move)*
Civilians still in the displacement zone. Priority's getting them clear before containment closes it.

KAEL
Understood.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

Anticipation, not dread — this is still a job being done well, not yet a crisis testing anyone's limits.

**CAMERA NOTES**

Moving alongside the team, wide enough to show the group, not isolating Kael yet.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Lower Sector's established flat, functional light, per `lighting-system.md`.

**SOUND NOTES**

Footfalls, brief coordination chatter, rising ambient tension.

**TRANSITION**

Cut to arrival at the crisis site.

### SCENE 3 — THE CRISIS SITE

- **Scene Number:** 3
- **Scene Title:** The Crisis Site
- **INT./EXT.:** EXT.
- **Location:** Lower Sector, crisis site
- **Time of Day:** Late morning
- **Story Purpose:** Establish the crisis's danger and the civilians at risk.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Rising urgency.
- **Characters Present:** Kael, response team, unnamed civilians; a brief, background-only glimpse of an unpersonified Ascendant Doctrine presence.
- **Required Assets:** Kael; Lower Sector (crisis-site dressing); scaled disturbance effect.
- **Required Audio:** Escalating but controlled sound design.
- **Required Visual Effects:** Breach-adjacent disturbance, larger and more visible than Episode 1's cue.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Direct continuation from Scene 2; the disturbance here is the escalated form of Episode 1's Scene 5 cue.

**ACTION**

The crisis site: visible Breach-adjacent disturbance, larger than anything shown in Episode 1, civilians caught in its displacement radius. The team spreads to begin evacuation.

Among the displaced crowd, at the frame's edge, unremarked upon by any character — a small cluster wearing a shared, unfamiliar insignia, watching rather than fleeing. Nothing is said about them. No character interacts with them. The camera does not linger.

**DIALOGUE**

CIVILIAN (off-frame, distressed)
My— I can't get back for—

KAEL
*(already moving toward them)*
I've got you. Move toward the others, I'll clear the rest.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

The Ascendant Doctrine presence must read as barely noticeable — a detail for a second viewing, not a plot beat this episode. If in doubt, underplay it further.

**CAMERA NOTES**

The unfamiliar-insignia cluster appears only in a single wide shot, never isolated in its own coverage.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Disturbance-adjacent lighting per `system-interference-visual-rules.md`, layered over Lower Sector's base lighting.

**SOUND NOTES**

Escalating but controlled; no score sting resolving the danger early.

**TRANSITION**

Cut to Kael continuing the rescue alone.

### SCENE 4 — THE CHOICE

- **Scene Number:** 4
- **Scene Title:** The Choice
- **INT./EXT.:** EXT.
- **Location:** Crisis site
- **Time of Day:** Late morning
- **Story Purpose:** Dramatize Kael's turning-point choice.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Determined, escalating strain.
- **Characters Present:** Kael; at least one unnamed colleague offering backup.
- **Required Assets:** Kael (strain state variant begins here).
- **Required Audio:** One clear dialogue beat carrying the refusal's intent.
- **Required Visual Effects:** None new.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Kael's physical state must begin visibly straining here, not jump suddenly at Scene 5.

**ACTION**

More civilians remain, deeper in the disturbance field than is safe to enter without backup. A COLLEAGUE reaches Kael, offering to go in with him.

**DIALOGUE**

COLLEAGUE
*(urgent, not panicked)*
Wait for two more. You go in alone, that's past protocol.

KAEL
*(already moving, first visible strain in his voice)*
There's no time to wait for two more.

COLLEAGUE
Kael—

KAEL
*(over his shoulder, already gone)*
I've got this.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

This is the flaw's first dramatized instance — the intent is "I can still do this," never a stated belief about needing to act alone. Play it as competence overriding caution, not as visible self-sacrifice.

**CAMERA NOTES**

Hold on the colleague's face a beat after Kael leaves frame — the audience should register the refusal as a choice, not an inevitability.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Same disturbance-adjacent lighting as Scene 3, intensifying slightly.

**SOUND NOTES**

The dialogue is the scene's one clear beat; everything else recedes under it.

**TRANSITION**

Cut to Kael pushing deeper into the disturbance field.

### SCENE 5 — THE COST

- **Scene Number:** 5
- **Scene Title:** The Cost
- **INT./EXT.:** EXT.
- **Location:** Crisis site
- **Time of Day:** Late morning
- **Story Purpose:** Land the episode's climax — success at unsustainable cost.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Peak strain, then collapse.
- **Characters Present:** Kael; civilians being saved.
- **Required Assets:** Kael (strain state variant, peak); scaled disturbance effect.
- **Required Audio:** Sound design peaks, then drops sharply into near-silence.
- **Required Visual Effects:** Disturbance effect at its most visible point this episode.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Strain visible since Scene 4 reaches its physical peak here — not a sudden jump.

**ACTION**

Kael reaches the last civilians. Gets them clear. The disturbance intensifies around him as he does — and the cost registers physically the moment the job is done: he staggers, injury and exhaustion catching up all at once, exactly as the danger resolves for everyone else.

The civilians are visibly safe. Kael is visibly not fine.

**DIALOGUE**

*(None. The scene is carried entirely by physical performance.)*

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

This is not stylized heroism — the strain must read as real cost, per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement. The audience should feel concern here, not triumph.

**CAMERA NOTES**

Sustained, unflinching hold on Kael's collapse — no cutaway that softens it.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Disturbance effect at its peak, then a sudden settling as it resolves.

**SOUND NOTES**

Peak intensity, then a sharp drop into near-silence — contrast, not continuous intensity, per the seed package's own explicit instruction.

**TRANSITION**

Cut to the immediate aftermath.

### SCENE 6 — PULLED BACK

- **Scene Number:** 6
- **Scene Title:** Pulled Back
- **INT./EXT.:** EXT.
- **Location:** Crisis site, aftermath
- **Time of Day:** Midday
- **Story Purpose:** Close the episode on vulnerability, not triumph.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Exhausted, quietly vulnerable.
- **Characters Present:** Kael; an unnamed colleague who physically intervenes.
- **Required Assets:** Kael (strain state variant); Lower Sector (aftermath dressing).
- **Required Audio:** Quiet, minimal.
- **Required Visual Effects:** Settled disturbance effects, aftermath state.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Closes directly into Episode 3's own Beginning State, per both packages' Transition fields.

**ACTION**

The site settling into aftermath. A COLLEAGUE reaches Kael first, physically steadying him — not a rescue, a simple, unspoken act of someone catching a person who's about to go down.

Kael doesn't protest. There isn't enough left in him to.

**DIALOGUE**

COLLEAGUE
*(quiet, not scolding)*
Got you.

KAEL
*(barely, spent)*
...Thanks.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

Minimal — exhaustion, not reflection. Self-awareness of the cost belongs to a later episode, not here. This should read as a person entirely out of reserve, nothing more articulate than that.

**CAMERA NOTES**

Static, quiet framing — let the stillness do the work after five scenes of escalating motion.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Aftermath state — settled, quieter than the crisis's peak.

**SOUND NOTES**

Near-silence; no musical resolution that reads as triumphant, per the seed package's own explicit instruction.

**TRANSITION**

Hold, then cut to black — directly into Episode 3's own Beginning State.
