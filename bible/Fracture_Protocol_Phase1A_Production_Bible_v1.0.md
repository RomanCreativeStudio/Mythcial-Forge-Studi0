## 1. Core Premise

*Compiled synthesis of explicitly Founder-confirmed facts below — not a Founder-authored logline, and should be refined once one exists.* Following humanity's discovery of an ancient artificial intelligence known as **the Guardian**, the interplanetary community entered the historical era known as **The Fracture Protocol** — an ideological fracture between two rival factions, **Light Order** and **Night Haven**, each shaping humanity's institutions, militaries, and public opinion in pursuit of its own vision of the future the Guardian refuses to choose on humanity's behalf. **Kael**, of the **Light 98th Contubernium**, stands at the center of that unfolding conflict.

**Setting date (added 2026-07-11, from the Core Premise Discovery Questionnaires):** the story is set in the year **3022**. No prior date existed anywhere in canon before this; logged here as the first confirmed timeline anchor for the franchise.

## 2. Story Identity

- **Genre:** Science fiction. Confirmed, Locked, non-negotiable absent an explicit future Founder Override: *"The Fracture Protocol remains a sci-fi flagship IP... No genre conversion is authorized."*
- **Creative Register:** storytelling inspired by the scale, emotion, and world-building found in manhwa and manga — explicitly scoped to *storytelling approach only*: cinematic presentation, emotional storytelling, character-driven narratives, progression systems, deep world-building, and memorable characters. This is a presentation/craft influence, not a genre instruction.

## 3. Core Themes

Franchise-level thematic pillars (distinct from, and operating at a broader altitude than, the Series Bible's existing Season One thematic-expression table — Section 3 of `series/01-the-fracture-protocol/Series_Bible_v1.1.md` — which remains unchanged and still governs how these ideas land episode-to-episode):

- Hope
- Restoration
- Peace
- Love
- Humanity
- Leadership
- Sacrifice

## 4. Core Conflict

The flagship conflict is the ideological struggle between **Light Order** and **Night Haven** (Sections 8–9).

## 5. Historical Foundation

Humanity's discovery of an ancient, highly advanced artificial intelligence — the Guardian (Section 7) — set in motion the chain of events historically known as **The Fracture Protocol** (Section 6).

## 6. The Fracture Protocol

**The Fracture Protocol** is the official historical name for the chain of events that followed humanity's discovery of the Guardian.

**Terminology note, flagged rather than silently resolved:** this is a *second, distinct* use of the term "Fracture Protocol" alongside two that already exist in Locked canon — the franchise's universe-level title (Master System Prompt Section 3: *"Universe name: THE FRACTURE PROTOCOL. This is the only valid universe-level identity"*) and the World Bible's use of "the Fracture Protocol" as the name of Authority's containment protocol for the single megacity (World Bible Section 2: *"the protocol that keeps the city stable *is* the Fracture Protocol"*). A title that is also an in-universe historical term is a common and often elegant convention, not inherently a conflict — but with three candidate referents now attached to one name (franchise title / megacity containment protocol / interplanetary historical era), reconciling them is a genuine open question, not something resolved by this compilation. Not altered here per the "resolve wording inconsistencies only, do not introduce new lore" instruction.

## 7. The Guardian

An ancient, highly advanced artificial intelligence discovered by humanity. Its purpose is to guide humanity toward a better future, but it refuses to force humanity's choices.

**Additional confirmed facts (added 2026-07-10, from the Phase 1B civilization material):**
- The Guardian cannot be controlled through force.
- It evaluates individuals according to principles rather than political affiliation.
- Its knowledge exceeds every artificial intelligence humanity has ever created.
- Although immensely powerful, humanity still does not fully understand its true origins or long-term purpose.

**Further confirmed fact (added 2026-07-11, from the Core Premise Discovery Questionnaires):**
- The Guardian cannot be destroyed — it is far more advanced than civilization currently understands.

**Origin theory, presented as legend rather than confirmed history (added 2026-07-11):** asked who built the Guardian, the Founder's own answer was explicitly hedged as theory, not settled fact: *"An ancient human civilization... mysterious, but theory is..."*, with current humanity possibly descending from that same lineage. Recorded here in the same hedged form it was given, rather than promoted to a hard fact — it does not override, and is not asserted to resolve, the still-standing "humanity does not fully understand its true origins" fact immediately above. Whether this theory is confirmed, remains legend in-universe, or is decided some other way is left open.

**Core Guardian Principle:**
> "Humanity cannot be led into a future it has not chosen."

**Relationship to the existing Authority System, flagged rather than silently resolved:** the World Bible (Section 4, Locked) already establishes **the Authority System** as *"the single governing force of the megacity — regulated, emotionless, always active... cannot be removed, destroyed, or ignored."* Authority's described character (controlling, regulated, treats residents as registered digital identity) reads in direct tension with the Guardian's described character (refuses to force choices, evaluates by principle rather than control). Whether these are the same entity under two names, two distinct AIs at different scales (city-level vs. civilization-level), or a deliberate thematic contrast the story is built to explore is not stated in any directive received so far. This document does not invent an answer — "Authority System" is not one of this compilation's requested topics, and connecting the two would be inventing new lore, not compiling given lore. Flagged here as the clearest candidate for "a genuine contradiction," per this task's own instruction to surface one if found.

## 8. Kael

Kael remains the primary protagonist. Full existing characterization (Cadet of the Ward Division, Ward Station Seven, fracture anomaly subject, orphaned by a Breach) is already Locked in World Bible Section 5 and Series Bible Section 1 and is not restated or altered here. **Integration question resolved (updated 2026-07-11):** how Kael's Ward Division story connects to his Light 98th Contubernium affiliation is now settled canon — see `bible/Fracture_Protocol_Phase1B3_Production_Bible_v1.0.md` Section 8 (Locked): the Ward Division and the Light 98th are separate, operationally-partnered organizations, and Kael's Ward Division Cadet status is his Season One starting point.

**Character depth (added 2026-07-11, from the Core Premise Discovery Questionnaires):**
- **Titan** — Kael's best friend and the Light 98th's second-in-command under him, now deceased. Titan's death is Kael's defining past wound and the belief Kael's arc measures itself against ("the version Titan envisioned him to be").
- What he fears losing: everything that makes him who he is — his family, and by extension his sense of self.
- What he must unlearn: that the world needs one person to unify it; what he must learn instead is that people can resolve conflict by working together.
- His stated aspiration: he is described as "a good leader and candidate for possible leader of the solar system," but explicitly does not seek the position for its own sake — what he actually wants is peace for Earth. This is compatible with the already-Locked USCN structure (an elected Chancellor, no monarchy) and supersedes an earlier draft's "king" framing, which was not carried forward into this document for exactly that reason.
- By the end: a charismatic leader who is also proud, humble, and caring, who prioritizes others and humanity over himself.

**Not included here, and why:** a separate draft described Kael as a 19-year-old returning from nearly five years fighting a war on Mars as part of an already-elite Light 98th, with an origin attributed to being "genetically superhuman." Both claims directly conflict with what's Locked above — a Cadet beginning his journey, orphaned by a Breach, raised at Ward Station Seven — and neither has been reconciled by any Founder response received so far, despite being raised across three separate discovery submissions. Per this task's own instruction to flag rather than silently resolve a genuine contradiction, this material is not compiled into Kael's canon here. Full detail and status tracked in `bible/Fracture_Protocol_Proposal_Vault_v1.0.md` ("Phase 1A.2 — Core Premise").

## 9. Light Order

Named as one of the two central ideological factions (Section 4) and as the affiliation of Kael's unit, the Light 98th Contubernium.

**Beliefs (added 2026-07-11, from the Core Premise Discovery Questionnaires):** Light Order holds that technology is the way to secure humanity's future, and believes it is protecting humanity's hope and vision. The conflict with Night Haven is personal as well as political — the two factions trace back to a formerly unified family alliance whose falling-out produced the current rivalry. Political structure and mechanics (influence, funding, government positioning) are Phase 1B.2 material and are not compiled here; only the belief system itself, which is squarely this document's own topic.

## 10. Night Haven

Named as the opposing central ideological faction (Section 4).

**Beliefs (added 2026-07-11, from the Core Premise Discovery Questionnaires):** Night Haven holds that military strength and order are what truly preserve civilization, and believes it is protecting law, order, and legacy. As with Light Order, the rivalry's roots are personal — a once-allied family alliance, not only a political dispute. Political structure and mechanics are left to Phase 1B.2, consistent with Light Order's treatment above.

## 11. Humanity

Not separately defined as a standalone worldbuilding concept in any directive received so far. "Humanity" appears explicitly as one of the seven Core Themes (Section 3); no additional lore-level definition (a species status, a political category, etc.) has been given, so none is invented here.

## 12. Story Philosophy

The Guardian's Core Principle (Section 7) — *"Humanity cannot be led into a future it has not chosen"* — remains the closest single-sentence statement of story philosophy.

**Creative guardrails (added 2026-07-11, from the Core Premise Discovery Questionnaires):** the Founder's own stated principles for what every Fracture Protocol story should and should not do:
- **Should never happen:** humanity's hope portrayed as fully lost, right and wrong blurred together, or the things that keep people going curdling into apathy.
- **Should always happen:** humanity keeps moving forward, stays strong, and the line between good and bad stays legible.
- **Every episode should leave the audience with:** a feeling of hope, restoration, and love, grounded in loyalty within the group.
- **The Founder's stated reason for telling this story:** to remind audiences that no one is too far gone to be saved — logged here as the Founder's own creative intent for the franchise's real-world takeaway, not as in-fiction canon.

## 13. Canon Principles

- The Fracture Protocol remains a sci-fi flagship IP; no genre conversion is authorized.
- Manhwa/manga influence describes storytelling approach only (Section 2), never genre.
- No approved canon is removed by this document — it consolidates and organizes; it does not retire anything in the Master System Prompt, World Bible, or Series Bible.
- Existing canon remains in force unless explicitly superseded by a later, equally explicit Founder directive.
