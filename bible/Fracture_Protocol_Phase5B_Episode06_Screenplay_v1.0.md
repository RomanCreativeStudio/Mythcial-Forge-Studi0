## 4. Production Notes

**Canon requirements (mandatory), PRIMARY VERIFICATION — Titan man/myth duality:**
- Titan does not appear on screen anywhere — no live-action flashback, no clearly-dramatized scene of him acting. Every account is delivered through others' memory, cultural practice, or environmental detail.
- Both layers are structurally required, not optional: Scene 2 (the legend, composed, without visible doubt) and Scenes 3–4 (the man — self-doubt, real humor, real fear, and his one recorded defining mistake) must both appear; the episode would violate Phase 2E's requirement if it stopped at the legend.
- No new fact about Titan beyond the Character Bible's "The Man and the Myth" entry is introduced anywhere.
- No reciprocal Kael/Mira mechanism appears — that beat is Locked to Arc Two or early Arc Three, outside this episode's scope.
- Kael's recognition of his own mirrored flaw (Scene 4) is never stated aloud by any character — it registers on Kael, unexplained by dialogue.
- Kael's flaw is not resolved in Scene 5 — his resolve is weighted and double-edged, not triumphant.

**Directorial interpretation (non-binding):** the specific Mid Zone Light 98th-affiliated space (training ground, memorial marker) is left to production design.

## 9. Full Screenplay

### SCENE 1 — KEPT MEMORY

- **Scene Number:** 1
- **Scene Title:** Kept Memory
- **INT./EXT.:** EXT.
- **Location:** Mid Zone — Light 98th-affiliated space
- **Time of Day:** Afternoon
- **Story Purpose:** Establish the Light 98th's ongoing cultural relationship to Titan's memory.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Reverent, formal, a little distant.
- **Characters Present:** Kael; unnamed Light 98th personnel.
- **Required Assets:** Kael; Mid Zone dressing.
- **Required Audio:** Dialogue establishing the legend's formal shape, not yet his person.
- **Required Visual Effects:** None.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Tonal continuation from Episode 5's close.

**ACTION**

A space within the Mid Zone that carries Titan's memory formally — a training ground still used, a marker still tended. Kael arrives the way someone arrives at a place they've been before but never quite looked at closely.

**DIALOGUE**

LIGHT 98TH PERSONNEL
*(with practiced reverence)*
Still comes up in every new cadet's first week. "Train like Titan did." Nobody quite explains what that means anymore. It's just what you say.

KAEL
*(genuinely curious, not skeptical)*
Did you know him?

LIGHT 98TH PERSONNEL
Knew of him. Different thing.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

Kael's questions come from admiration here, not doubt — he's not yet troubled by anything, just curious in a way the season hasn't shown him being before.

**CAMERA NOTES**

Wider, more formal framing — the legend's register.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Warmer, more reverent than the season's crisis episodes, per `lighting-system.md`.

**SOUND NOTES**

Formal, slightly reverent ambience.

**TRANSITION**

Cut to a more senior figure joining.

### SCENE 2 — THE LEGEND

- **Scene Number:** 2
- **Scene Title:** The Legend
- **INT./EXT.:** EXT.
- **Location:** Same as Scene 1
- **Time of Day:** Continuous
- **Story Purpose:** Present the myth layer fully, before it's complicated.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Admiring, larger-than-life.
- **Characters Present:** Kael; the senior, unnamed Light 98th figure.
- **Required Assets:** Kael; the Scene 2 Light 98th figure.
- **Required Audio:** Dialogue should sound exactly like a legend — composed, without visible doubt.
- **Required Visual Effects:** None.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Direct continuation from Scene 1.

**ACTION**

The senior figure recounts Titan's reputation with the polish of a story told many times — accomplished, composed, second-in-command of an elite formation by the time he was barely older than Kael is now.

**DIALOGUE**

SENIOR LIGHT 98TH FIGURE
Second-in-command of the whole Contubernium, and he earned every bit of it. Never lost his composure in the field. Never asked for anything he wasn't willing to do himself first.

KAEL
*(quiet, absorbing it)*
Sounds like someone worth measuring up to.

SENIOR LIGHT 98TH FIGURE
*(warm, sure of it)*
He is. Was.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

This scene exists to make the pedestal concrete — play it entirely straight, no undercutting yet. The audience should feel exactly what Kael feels here: the pull of an unreachable standard.

**CAMERA NOTES**

Same formal register as Scene 1.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Unchanged.

**SOUND NOTES**

Same reverent ambience.

**TRANSITION**

Cut as the senior figure leaves and someone else approaches.

### SCENE 3 — THE MAN

- **Scene Number:** 3
- **Scene Title:** The Man
- **INT./EXT.:** EXT.
- **Location:** Same as Scenes 1–2, or a more private adjacent space
- **Time of Day:** Continuous
- **Story Purpose:** Introduce the human layer, per Phase 2E's explicit requirement.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Warmer, more candid.
- **Characters Present:** Kael; a different unnamed Light 98th figure, who served closer to Titan personally.
- **Required Assets:** Kael; the Scenes 3–4 Light 98th figure.
- **Required Audio:** Dialogue naming ordinary human qualities, never diminishing him.
- **Required Visual Effects:** None.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Direct continuation from Scene 2; the gap between the two accounts becomes explicit here.

**ACTION**

A different figure — someone who actually served beside Titan, not just inherited his reputation. Their account is looser, warmer, and immediately different in kind from Scene 2's polish.

**DIALOGUE**

LIGHT 98TH FIGURE (PERSONAL)
*(a small, fond laugh)*
"Never lost his composure" — that's the story people tell. He was terrified before every op, same as the rest of us. Just didn't let it stop him.

KAEL
*(surprised, genuinely)*
Nobody's told me that part.

LIGHT 98TH FIGURE (PERSONAL)
Nobody tells the part where he doubted himself. Doesn't fit the story people want. He used to say — if remembering him convinces anyone that only legends can protect people, then we've all missed the point of what he actually stood for.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

This is the gap becoming explicit — play the speaker as fond, not debunking. Both accounts are true; this scene exists to add the second layer, not replace the first.

**CAMERA NOTES**

Closer framing than Scenes 1–2, per `cinematic-mood-guide.md`.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Warmer still, more intimate.

**SOUND NOTES**

Quieter ambience, more personal register.

**TRANSITION**

Cut as the account continues toward its specific point.

### SCENE 4 — WHAT HE CARRIED

- **Scene Number:** 4
- **Scene Title:** What He Carried
- **INT./EXT.:** Same
- **Location:** Same as Scene 3
- **Time of Day:** Continuous
- **Story Purpose:** Land the episode's central beat — Titan's own defining flaw.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Kael quiet, recognizing something in himself; the speaker matter-of-fact.
- **Characters Present:** Kael; the same Light 98th figure from Scene 3.
- **Required Assets:** Kael; the same figure.
- **Required Audio:** The fact stated plainly, without editorializing the connection to Kael.
- **Required Visual Effects:** None.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Direct continuation from Scene 3; Kael's mirrored flaw is never named aloud.

**ACTION**

The figure continues, without knowing what it will land on for Kael specifically.

**DIALOGUE**

LIGHT 98TH FIGURE (PERSONAL)
If he had one real mistake — and he'd have told you this himself — it's that he thought carrying everything alone was the same thing as doing his job well. Never let the rest of us take our share. Not because he didn't trust us. Just... never occurred to him there was another way.

Kael says nothing. Something in his face has gone very still.

**DIALOGUE (CONT'D)**

LIGHT 98TH FIGURE (PERSONAL)
*(not noticing, or choosing not to press)*
Anyway. That's the part they don't put in the stories.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

No line states the parallel to Kael's own flaw — the recognition must register entirely through his stillness and silence. The speaker states the fact plainly, without judgment of Titan and without any awareness of its relevance to Kael.

**CAMERA NOTES**

Held on Kael's stillness — the episode's most restrained, important shot.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Unchanged from Scene 3.

**SOUND NOTES**

Near-silence around Kael's reaction.

**TRANSITION**

Cut to Kael, alone.

### SCENE 5 — WHAT HE DECIDES

- **Scene Number:** 5
- **Scene Title:** What He Decides
- **INT./EXT.:** Same or adjacent
- **Location:** A quieter moment, alone or nearly so
- **Time of Day:** Early evening
- **Story Purpose:** Close the episode on Kael's private, complicated resolve.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Weighted, not inspired.
- **Characters Present:** Kael only, or with minimal unnamed presence.
- **Required Assets:** Kael; Mid Zone dressing.
- **Required Audio:** Minimal or none.
- **Required Visual Effects:** None.
- **Continuity Requirements:** Closes tonally into Episode 7's own Beginning State (Kael's formal advancement bid).

**ACTION**

Kael alone with what he's learned. Not devastated, not inspired in any simple sense — something more complicated settling into him. A private resolve forms, but it is not relief.

**DIALOGUE**

*(None, or a single quiet line.)*

KAEL
*(low, to himself, not triumphant)*
Then I'll do it differently.
*(a beat — the line doesn't land as confident as he means it to)*
...Or I'll try.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

Per Phase 2E's explicit requirement, this must not read as his flaw resolved — it's him choosing to take on more, which should register as double-edged, the same pattern the audience has already learned to watch for since Episode 3.

**CAMERA NOTES**

Quiet, still framing.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Softening toward evening.

**SOUND NOTES**

Near-silence; no resolving musical cue.

**TRANSITION**

Hold on Kael's weighted resolve, then cut — tonally, into Episode 7's own Beginning State (his formal advancement bid).
