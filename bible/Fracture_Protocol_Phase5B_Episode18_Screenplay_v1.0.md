### SCENE 4 — THE QUESTION IT CAN'T DISMISS

- **Scene Number:** 4
- **Scene Title:** The Question It Can't Dismiss
- **INT./EXT.:** INT.
- **Location:** Existing Guardian/Kael partnership environment
- **Time of Day:** Afternoon
- **Story Purpose:** Show the Guardian's own unresolved tension, genuinely open, not answered.
- **Emotional Purpose:** Genuine uncertainty, for once; Kael witnessing a vulnerability he hasn't seen before.
- **Characters Present:** Kael; the Guardian.
- **Required Assets:** Kael; the Guardian; existing partnership environment.
- **Required Audio:** Dialogue leaving the question audibly open.
- **Required Visual Effects:** None.
- **Continuity Requirements:** The Guardian's hidden fear present in tone, not stated as settled; genuinely unresolved.

**ACTION**

For the first time in either man's memory of it, the Guardian's composure carries visible uncertainty.

**DIALOGUE**

GUARDIAN
*(quiet, uncharacteristically uncertain)*
He forces a question I can't fully dismiss. How many times must humanity fail before trust becomes negligence?

KAEL
What's your answer?

GUARDIAN
That trust isn't valuable because humanity always chooses correctly. It's valuable because humanity retains the ability to choose again. That's my answer. I won't tell you it's settled.

**PERFORMANCE NOTES**

Per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement, the Guardian's calm must never read as certainty here — its hidden fear (that Cassian's argument might be correct) is present in tone only, never stated as settled fact. This is its one moment of visible, genuine uncertainty this episode, distinct from its usual steadiness.

**CAMERA NOTES**

Closer, stiller framing on the Guardian — the episode's most intimate camera moment.

**LIGHTING NOTES**

Deepens slightly, per `lighting-system.md`, without becoming symbolic or ominous.

**SOUND NOTES**

Near-silent ambience under the Guardian's uncertainty; no cue resolving the question.

**TRANSITION**

Cut, continuous scene, into Scene 5.
