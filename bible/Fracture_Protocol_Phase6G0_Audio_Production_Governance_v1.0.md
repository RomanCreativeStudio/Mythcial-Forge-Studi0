# THE FRACTURE PROTOCOL — PHASE 6G.0: AUDIO PRODUCTION GOVERNANCE

**Classification:** Internal — Governance Document (**Approved**)
**Status:** Compiled 2026-07-15 per "Founder Directive — Phase 6G.0 Audio Production Governance (v1.0)." Continues directly from Phase 6F.2. This document creates no audio, casts no voice, composes no music, rewrites no dialogue, and modifies no canon. It establishes only the new governance Phase 6G (Audio Production) requires — it does not restate the Environment (Phase 6C), Prop & Technology (Phase 6D.0), Storyboard (Phase 6E.0), or Animation (Phase 6F.0) governance already Locked; those remain in force, unmodified, and are cited by reference only.
**Absolute authority:** subordinate to Master System Prompt → Locked Canon (World Bible, Character Bible, Faction Bible) → Production Bible → Approved Screenplays → Phase 6A Master Production Roadmap (Audio Production Plan) → Phase 6B Character Production line → Phase 6C Environment Production Canon and its full reconciliation lineage → Phase 6D.0/6D.1/6D.1.5 Prop & Technology Production → Phase 6E.0/6E.1/6E.2 Storyboard Production → Phase 6F.0/6F.1/6F.2 Animation Production → **Phase 6G.0 Audio Production Governance**.

---

## 1. Purpose

Phase 6G exists to translate each Approved screenplay's own scene-level Dialogue, Audio Notes, Sound Notes, and Music Direction into produced audio — the production stage after animation, covering dialogue recording, voice performance, music, ambient sound, and sound effects. Phase 6G.0 establishes only the rules that govern this work. It authorizes no audio, casting, composition, or dialogue change itself.

---

## 2. Audio Authority

An audio asset — of any kind — is authorized only by:
1. The specific screenplay scene it accompanies — its own Dialogue, Required Audio, Audio Notes, Sound Notes, and Music Direction fields, cited directly.
2. That scene's governing Environment Production Package (Soundscape field) and Storyboard/Animation Production Packages, where a specific timing or continuity requirement is already on record (e.g., Episode 7's confirmed "comm chime" timing, already cited in FPP-ANIM's own Timing fields).
3. Each present character's own Approved Character Production Package (Phase 6B), where a character-specific audio dependency is already confirmed (e.g., Kael's eye-glow system's own facial/audio synchronization implications).

An audio asset with no citable source in one of these three categories does not exist for production purposes — it must be flagged as missing, never invented, exactly as the same discipline has governed every phase of this production since Phase 6A.

---

## 3. Dialogue Authority

Dialogue audio is governed by the Locked screenplay text alone:
- Every line of recorded dialogue must match its governing screenplay's own DIALOGUE block exactly — word for word, per the Production Bible's own screenplay-authority rule.
- No dialogue may be added, cut, paraphrased, or reordered at the audio production stage. A perceived pacing or performance need does not authorize a text change — it is flagged under the Production Conflict Rule (Section 11, below) for Founder decision, never resolved by silently editing the line.
- Parenthetical performance directions (e.g., "*(strained)*," "*(quiet, observational)*") govern delivery, not content, and must be read as binding direction for Voice Performance Authority (Section 4), not as optional color.

---

## 4. Voice Performance Authority

Voice performance choices are governed by:
- The scene's own Performance Notes and parenthetical delivery directions, cited directly.
- Each character's own Approved Character Production Package — specifically any Locked personal register already on record (e.g., Cassian's measured, deliberate public register fraying only at his five Schedule beats; the Guardian's calm, unremarkable register with its single permitted pause-before-speaking deviation, Episode 8; Kael's economical, controlled baseline).
- The Guardian's own Locked Mystery Protection Rule (Phase 6B.1): voice performance may never introduce vocal expressiveness, tonal coding, or a delivery choice that hints at any of its nine protected narrative facts.

This directive does not authorize casting any voice. Voice Performance Authority governs how an eventual cast performer must deliver a Locked line, once casting occurs under its own separate Founder-directed process (per the Phase 6A Audio Production Plan's own sequencing note) — it does not itself select or approve a performer.

---

## 5. Music Authority

Music is governed by:
- The scene's own Music Direction field, cited directly, wherever one exists (nearly every screenplay carries an explicit Music Direction section already on record — e.g., Episode 5's "the score must never resolve the danger before the visuals do"; Episode 26's own restraint instruction against "score cue recasts Mira's account as tragedy-for-effect").
- The Locked Emotional Lighting Modes' own narrative-beat logic (`lighting-system.md`), applied by analogy: music, like lighting, is chosen by story beat, never by production convenience.
- Where a scene's Music Direction explicitly prohibits a specific effect (a swell, a resolving cue, a triumphant register), that prohibition is binding on any future score, not merely a stylistic suggestion.

This directive does not authorize composing any music. Music Authority governs what an eventual score must and must not do, once composition occurs under its own separate Founder-directed process — it does not itself write or approve a cue.

---

## 6. Ambient Sound Authority

Ambient sound (environmental atmosphere, room tone, background activity) is governed by:
- The scene's own Sound Notes field, cited directly.
- The governing Environment Production Package's own Soundscape field, where confirmed (several are explicitly "Missing — not specified," and must remain flagged, not filled with an invented ambience).
- Where an environment's Soundscape is confirmed only generically (e.g., "focused, competent ambience — tools, quiet coordination," Episode 19), ambient sound may be produced only to that level of specificity — no invented detail beyond the citation.

---

## 7. Sound Effects Authority

Sound effects are governed by:
- The scene's own Required Visual Effects and Sound Notes fields, cross-referenced — nearly every screenplay in this production confirms "None" for Required Visual Effects, and sound effects must not be invented to accompany an effect the screenplay itself does not require.
- The Art Bible's `system-interference-visual-rules.md`, where a Cipher/Fragment/Breach-adjacent effect is confirmed present — any accompanying sound design must respect that effect's own confirmed intensity-scaling and physical-integration rules (no flat, unmotivated sound layered over a system-interference effect, mirroring the visual rule against flat, unshadowed glow).
- FPP-TECH-001 (the comm): its confirmed "chime" is the production's only currently-confirmed discrete, named sound effect, cited directly from Episode 7's own Sound Notes ("the comm chime is the scene's one sharp sound event").

---

## 8. Audio Continuity Rule

Every audio asset must remain consistent with:
- The character's own established vocal register and any confirmed Signal/schedule-based performance state (e.g., Cassian's five-stage escalation must be audible, not only visible, across Episodes 28/29/30/31/35).
- The environment's own confirmed Soundscape and any Permanent Lighting Canon or Production Rule governing its visual register, so that sound and image remain aligned (e.g., Night Haven's confirmed cooler/institutional vs. warmer/resistance register split, Phase 6C.7, should inform its own ambient sound treatment by analogy, though no specific sound rule is itself Locked for this distinction — flagged as an open item, not resolved here).
- The scene's own Transition field, so that audio cuts (hard cuts, fades, sustained-through-transition cues) match the governing Storyboard Production Package's own Transition Rules.

Any apparent inconsistency between an audio choice and its own governing screenplay, character, or environment package must be flagged for Founder decision under the Production Conflict Rule, never silently resolved by the audio team's own judgment.

---

## 9. Audio Revision Rule

Any revision to an Approved audio asset must:
- Reopen the existing audio entry, never overwrite it silently — preserving all prior Founder Approval as historical record, per the reopen-and-append method already established throughout this production.
- Cite the specific new source that justifies the revision (a corrected screenplay, an updated character/environment package, or a new Founder Directive).
- Carry its own new Founder Review, Alignment Audit, and Founder Approval before superseding the prior version.
- Never delete a prior version's record — mark it Superseded, consistent with the standing "nothing disappears without a record" practice.

---

## 10. Version Control

Every audio production document follows the same versioning discipline standing across this entire production: `v1.0` on first compilation, incremented major versions on Founder-directed content changes, changelog entries preserved in full at every version, and git commit/push performed separately for content-compilation versus Founder-approval steps wherever both occur in the same session.

---

## 11. Production Conflict Rule

If any audio work finds:
- A contradiction between two Locked Canon sources,
- A contradiction between a screenplay's own Dialogue/Sound/Music Notes and its governing Environment, Character, or Animation Production Package,
- A dialogue, performance, music, or sound choice with no citable source anywhere in Audio, Dialogue, Voice Performance, Music, Ambient Sound, or Sound Effects Authority (Sections 2–7), or
- Any indication of repository corruption or a missing Locked authority,

production on that specific audio asset stops, and the conflict is flagged for Founder decision — exactly as the Lighting Canon rule and every prior Phase 6 Production Conflict Rule already require in their own domains. No conflict of this kind may be silently resolved by audio-stage inference, convenience, or default. This includes, without exception, any apparent need to alter a Locked dialogue line — such a need is always flagged, never self-authorized.
