## 1. Episode Identity

- **Episode number:** 2
- **Episode title:** "The Crisis"
- **Core purpose:** Dramatize the opening incident (Phase 4A, Episode 2, Purpose).
- **Narrative objective:** Establish the burden-alone flaw through action, not exposition — the specific engine the rest of the season runs on (Phase 2A.2, Section 1).
- **Emotional objective:** Pride and dread at once — Kael succeeds, but the way he succeeds should concern the audience more than reassure it.
- **Audience experience goal:** The first time the audience sees what "protection at unsustainable cost" actually looks like. It should feel like watching someone do something right in a way that's quietly wrong.

---

## 2. Story Structure

*Unlike Episode 1, this episode carries real, Locked conflict — Phase 4A's own seed entry states Kael "refuses backup and overextends past his own safe limits" (Phase 4A, Episode 2, Central Conflict). All eight fields below are populated, tracing directly to that entry and to Phase 2A.2, Section 1.*

- **Opening hook:** The Breach-adjacent containment crisis begins — alarm and mobilization at Ward Station Seven.
- **Setup:** Kael is embedded in a joint Ward Division/Light 98th response, alongside unnamed colleagues (Phase 2A.2, Section 1).
- **Rising conflict:** As the crisis escalates, Kael is offered backup and declines it, pushing past his own safe limits to reach civilians faster (Phase 4A, Episode 2, Central Conflict).
- **Character turning point:** The specific moment Kael chooses to continue alone rather than call for help — the episode's defining choice, and the flaw's first dramatized instance.
- **Major reveal or progression:** A brief, background-only glimpse of Ascendant Doctrine presence near the crisis's displaced/at-risk civilians — first on-screen evidence, unpersonified and not yet a named threat (Phase 4A, Episode 2, World Progression). Not developed further this episode; the fuller recruiting beat belongs to Episode 3 (Phase 4A, Episode 3).
- **Climax:** Kael succeeds — civilians are saved — but only by pushing past his own safe limits; the cost lands physically, not as a plot twist.
- **Closing emotional beat:** Vulnerability replacing triumph — injured and exhausted, Kael is pulled back by someone else (Phase 4A, Episode 2, Ending).
- **Cliffhanger / Transition:** Into the crisis's institutional and personal aftermath — directly into Episode 3 (Phase 4A, Episode 2, Transition).

## 3. Scene Breakdown

*Six scenes proposed, sized for Studio OS's Mainline Episode runtime target (8–14 minutes). No scene introduces content beyond the seed entry; escalation is physical and situational, not a new plot mechanic.*

**Scene 1 — Alarm**
- Purpose: Open on the crisis beginning — mobilization, not explanation.
- Location: Ward Station Seven.
- Characters present: Kael; unnamed Ward Division and Light 98th personnel.
- Emotional state: Alert, focused, no panic — competence under sudden pressure.
- Conflict: The Breach-adjacent crisis itself, still distant.
- Visual requirements: Ward Station Seven interior/mobilization space; alarm/warning cues per Art Bible `system-interference-visual-rules.md`.
- Audio requirements: Urgent but controlled sound design — mobilization, not chaos.
- Production needs: Reuses Ward Station Seven asset (Phase 4A, Section 6).

**Scene 2 — Deployment**
- Purpose: Establish Kael embedded in the joint response.
- Location: Transit from Ward Station Seven into the Lower Sector crisis site.
- Characters present: Kael; unnamed joint Ward Division/Light 98th response team.
- Emotional state: Focused, professional.
- Conflict: None yet direct — anticipation.
- Visual requirements: Lower Sector environment (Phase 2D, Section 2); no new location.
- Audio requirements: Minimal dialogue, coordination-focused, intent only.
- Production needs: Reuses Lower Sector asset.

**Scene 3 — The Crisis Site**
- Purpose: Establish the crisis's danger and the civilians at risk.
- Location: Lower Sector, crisis site.
- Characters present: Kael, response team, unnamed civilians. A brief, background-only glimpse of an Ascendant Doctrine presence among the displaced/at-risk civilians (unpersonified, not interacted with directly this scene).
- Emotional state: Rising urgency.
- Conflict: The crisis itself — danger to civilians.
- Visual requirements: Breach-adjacent disturbance effects, larger and more visible than Episode 1's subtle cue, per Art Bible `system-interference-visual-rules.md`.
- Audio requirements: Escalating but controlled sound design; no score sting that resolves the danger early.
- Production needs: New crisis-site dressing of the existing Lower Sector asset; checked against the Asset Registry before build.

**Scene 4 — The Choice**
- Purpose: Dramatize Kael's turning-point choice.
- Location: Crisis site.
- Characters present: Kael; at least one unnamed colleague offering backup.
- Emotional state: Determined, escalating strain.
- Conflict: Kael is offered backup and declines it, choosing to push forward alone (Phase 4A, Episode 2, Central Conflict).
- Visual requirements: Kael's physical state should begin showing strain here, not only at the collapse (Scene 5) — a readable through-line, not a sudden jump.
- Audio requirements: A single clear dialogue beat carrying the refusal's intent (not the line itself, see Section 4).
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 5 — The Cost**
- Purpose: Land the episode's climax — success at unsustainable cost.
- Location: Crisis site.
- Characters present: Kael; civilians being saved.
- Emotional state: Peak strain, then collapse.
- Conflict: Kael pushes past his own safe limits to complete the rescue.
- Visual requirements: Civilians visibly safe; Kael's overextension shown physically (injury/exhaustion), not narrated.
- Audio requirements: Sound design should peak here, then drop sharply into near-silence as the danger resolves — contrast, not continuous intensity.
- Production needs: No new asset beyond Scene 3's crisis-site dressing.

**Scene 6 — Pulled Back**
- Purpose: Close the episode on vulnerability, not triumph.
- Location: Crisis site, aftermath.
- Characters present: Kael; an unnamed colleague who physically pulls him back/intervenes.
- Emotional state: Exhausted, quietly vulnerable.
- Conflict: None external — the scene is entirely a consequence beat.
- Visual requirements: Same crisis-site asset, aftermath state (settled disturbance effects).
- Audio requirements: Quiet, minimal — the episode should end on near-silence, not a musical resolution that reads as triumphant.
- Production needs: No new asset.

## 4. Character Performance Planning

**Kael.**
- Motivations this episode: Protect the civilians in front of him, regardless of personal cost — consistent with his Locked belief that power is a responsibility (Character Bible, "Leadership Philosophy"; Phase 2C, Kael §1). Not recklessness — a specific, dramatized instance of a philosophy the audience already saw stated calmly in Episode 1.
- Emotional progression: Alert focus (Scene 1) → professional readiness (Scene 2) → rising urgency (Scene 3) → determined refusal (Scene 4) → peak strain and collapse (Scene 5) → quiet vulnerability (Scene 6).
- Dialogue intent, not lines: in Scene 4, the refusal should carry the intent "I can still do this" rather than any explicit statement of his burden-alone belief — the audience should recognize the flaw before he would ever name it himself. In Scene 6, dialogue (if any) should be minimal — exhaustion, not reflection; self-awareness of the cost comes later in the season, not here.
- Body language: controlled and capable through Scenes 1–3; visible physical strain beginning in Scene 4, escalating through Scene 5; genuinely depleted, not performing composure, in Scene 6. Per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement, this episode is exactly where the flaw is *supposed* to cost him something real — the strain must be shown as real cost, not stylized heroism.
- Relationship dynamics: none active this episode — Mira does not appear, consistent with Phase 4A's Episode 2 entry naming only Kael as this episode's character focus.

## 5. Visual Production Planning

- **Required environments:** Ward Station Seven (interior, Scene 1) and a new crisis-site dressing of the existing Lower Sector environment asset (Scenes 2–6) — no new location is introduced; this is set dressing on top of the season's already-established Lower Sector asset (Phase 4A, Section 6).
- **Character appearances:** Kael, per his existing Art Bible reference sheet, with a strain/injury state built as a variant for Scenes 4–6 — not a new character design, a state variant of the existing asset.
- **Props:** Standard Ward Division/Light 98th response equipment, already implied by Locked world-building (Phase 1B.3) — no new prop invented.
- **Effects:** A larger, more visible Breach-adjacent disturbance effect than Episode 1's subtle cue, governed entirely by Art Bible's `system-interference-visual-rules.md` — a scaled application of existing rules, not a new visual rule.
- **Technology:** Governed by Phase 1B.3's existing framework — nothing in this episode requires technology outside what's already Locked.
- **Lighting:** Per Art Bible's `lighting-system.md` for Lower Sector zone identity, adjusted for crisis intensity within existing rules — cited, not redefined.
- **Cinematic requirements:** Per Art Bible's `camera-language.md` and `cinematic-mood-guide.md` — this episode's tone (controlled urgency escalating to strain, then dropping to near-silence) is a mood note for whoever applies those rules, not a shot list.

## 6. Audio Planning

- **Narration needs:** None — the episode is action- and consequence-driven, not narrated.
- **Dialogue priorities:** Scene 4 (the refusal) carries the episode's one dialogue beat with real narrative weight; all other scenes use dialogue sparingly, in service of coordination and urgency, not exposition.
- **Sound design requirements:** Escalating but controlled through Scenes 1–5, peaking at Scene 5's climax, then dropping sharply into near-silence for Scene 6 — per Phase 3A's routing to Production OS's Music Production SOP and Studio OS's Music & Audio Standards.
- **Music tone:** Urgent but not chaotic through the crisis; absent or minimal in the closing scene, letting quiet carry the vulnerability rather than a musical resolution.
- **Emotional timing:** The score should never resolve the danger before the visuals do — Scene 5's success and Scene 6's cost must land as two distinct emotional beats, not one continuous relief.

## 7. Asset Requirements

- **Characters:** Kael — already the season's top asset priority (Phase 4A, Section 6); a strain/injury state variant is needed for Scenes 4–6, checked against the Asset Registry before build as a variant of the existing asset, not a new character.
- **Environments:** Ward Station Seven (interior, reused) and the Lower Sector (reused, with new crisis-site dressing) — both already identified in Phase 4A's Section 6 asset pool.
- **Props:** None beyond standard, already-Locked Ward Division/Light 98th equipment.
- **Effects:** One scaled-up Breach-adjacent disturbance effect (Scenes 3–5) — checked against the Asset Registry first; built only if no equivalent already exists, per Episode 1's own smaller-scale version of the same effect.
- **Reusable assets:** This episode consumes Phase 4A's already-identified season asset pool (Kael, Lower Sector, Ward Station Seven) and adds only state variants and set dressing, not new asset categories.
