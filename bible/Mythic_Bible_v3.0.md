### How to use this document

This is the master design document. It is not a novel and not an episode — it is the law every future story must follow. Another writer, unfamiliar with any single story, should be able to write a new arc from this document alone without breaking continuity.

Sections are tagged:
- **[LOCKED]** — foundational cosmology. Changes require founder/Creative Director sign-off.
- **[LIVING SECTION]** — expected to grow. Writers may extend it within the existing framework via the Lore Consistency process (Section 56).

**World Design Principles (apply to every section below):**
1. The world rewards curiosity — buried detail should always be *findable*, never decorative filler.
2. History matters — nothing resets; consequences persist across arcs and seasons.
3. Magic has rules — every power has a cost, a limit, and a failure mode.
4. Power requires sacrifice — there are no random power-ups and no plot armor.
5. Characters grow realistically — earned, not granted.
6. Every mystery eventually has an explanation — but "eventually" can mean seasons away (see Section 60).

### A Note on This Document's Scope **[LOCKED]**

This Bible documents *The Shattered Realms* exactly as it is experienced by everyone living inside it — including every named character. That account is the full and true one for storytelling purposes, and nothing below should be treated as incomplete or provisional.

A restricted companion document, `bible/Fracture_Protocol_TrueReality_v1.0.md`, exists for Creative Director-level, long-term season planning only. Per World Design Principle 6, some of the open questions in Section 60 have answers reserved there — but "eventually" is not scheduled here, and no writer should treat this note as a hint to go looking for them early.

## 1. Universe Overview **[LOCKED]**

**The Shattered Realms** is the name the present-day world gives itself — a blunt, honest description of what it is: the wreckage of something that used to be whole. Long ago, reality was a single, seamless creation called **Elysia**, shaped by seven primordial beings. A cataclysm called **the Fracture** broke Elysia into **Seven Great Realms**, scattered across a dark, trackless medium called the **Aetherséa**.

The present day is roughly 900 years after the Fracture, an era the Concord calendar calls **the Fourth Age** — popularly, **the Age of Echoes**. The immediate chaos of the cataclysm has settled into fragile, functioning civilizations, but the wounds the Fracture left in reality are stirring again for the first time in generations. That reawakening is the franchise's long-term engine and should escalate across seasons, never resolve early.

This version of the Bible introduces the franchise's flagship protagonist, **Kael** (full profile in Appendix A), a Duskmarch cadet whose personal story is the reader's/viewer's entry point into the wider world — but the world must always feel larger than his story alone.

## 2. History Before Time **[LIVING SECTION — deliberately mysterious]**

What existed before Elysia is not canon-confirmed anywhere in the setting. No character, faction, or text in *The Shattered Realms* knows the answer, and that is intentional — this section exists to be asked about, not answered, until the franchise is ready for a cosmic-scale reveal (see Section 60).

What fragments of pre-Elysian myth persist, told differently in every Realm's oldest oral traditions:
- A recurring image of **"the Silence before the Seven"** — the idea that before Elysia, there was no division between anything, not even concepts, and that the Aurothi were the first act of separation, not creation.
- A recurring number pattern — many pre-Elysian creation fragments reference "the first breath" being plural, hinting the Aurothi may not have been the *only* primordial beings, merely the ones who stayed.
- No source, anywhere, claims to know what (if anything) is outside the Aetherséa beyond the Seven Realms.

**Rule:** Nothing in this section may be contradicted by a confirmed answer without founder-level sign-off. Treat it as sacred ambiguity.

## 3. The Age of Elysia **[LOCKED]**

Before the Fracture, the united cosmos was called **Elysia**, shaped and tended by seven primordial beings known as **the Aurothi** — not worshipped gods, but architects: vast, semi-conscious forces who had each taken responsibility for one aspect of existence.

| Aurothi | Domain | Realm Left Behind |
|---|---|---|
| Ignareth | Flame, ambition, transformation | Emberfall |
| Maelith | Tide, memory, sorrow | The Tidewrought |
| Terrenox | Stone, endurance, permanence | The Ironroot Expanse |
| Zephyrine | Wind, freedom, change | The Windmere Reaches |
| Duskaris | Shadow, silence, death | The Duskmarch |
| Solivane | Light, judgment, order | The Solvane Dominion |
| Virenna | Growth, life, renewal | The Verdant Wilds |

Under the Aurothi, a single civilization — **the Elysian Commonwealth** — flourished for millennia: castes, wars, famines, and golden ages like any real society, but whole, its people able to walk the entire cosmos on foot. Its scholars developed the first systems for working with **Essence** (Section 16), culminating in the founding art of **Sigilcraft** (Section 17), invented by a guild of artisans remembered today only as **the Anvilborn** (Section 10, Section 13).

*Etymological note (Section 31):* In Old Elysian, the word **"solenne"** means "the wholeness that was." Some ancient texts and liturgies still refer to the lost age poetically as **Solenne** — this is a title of reverence, not a rival name; "Elysia" is the formal historical term used in this Bible and by modern scholarship.

The Aurothi did not rule as a unified pantheon. Fragmentary, disputed records suggest a growing disagreement about whether the Commonwealth's Essence-craft was advancing faster than the Aurothi could safely contain. This is widely believed — never confirmed — to be the root cause of the Fracture (see Section 60).

## 4. The Fracture Event **[LOCKED]**

The Fracture is never depicted in full on-page; it is mythologized, half-remembered, reconstructed from ruins and Echoes. Agreed-upon facts across all Seven Realms:

- It struck without warning across **"the Seven Breaths"** — seven cataclysmic pulses, one per Aurothi, in immediate succession.
- Elysia did not merely break geographically — it broke **causally**. Regions once adjacent are not necessarily adjacent now. The Seven Realms drifted into the Aetherséa, and paths between them had to be rediscovered, not simply walked.
- Each Aurothi was gravely wounded, not destroyed. Their consciousness fragmented into **Echoes** (Section 18), which persist in their home Realm as blessings, hauntings, or wounds in reality called **Rifts**.
- Roughly nine in ten Elysians are believed to have died in the immediate aftermath — an era survivors named **the Ashfall**.

**Open narrative question (permanently unresolved by design):** Was the Fracture an accident, a war among the Aurothi, or a deliberate act by one of them? No in-world faction is objectively correct. See Section 60.

## 6. Detailed Realm Profiles **[LIVING SECTION]**

**Emberfall** — Volcanic highlands and forge-cities built into calderas. Culture prizes visible achievement and rank-by-deed; social mobility is real but ruthless. Ruled by the Pyrion Crown (Section 7). Signature conflict: ambition curdling into conquest.

**The Tidewrought** — Archipelagos, sunken ruins, tidal cities on stilts. Obsessed with memory and record-keeping — the Realm most likely to have an accurate written account of any given historical event, and the most likely to argue over whose account is correct. Governed by the Maren Thalassocracy.

**The Ironroot Expanse** — Vast cavern-networks and mountain holds. Change comes slowly and is trusted only after it's proven itself for a generation. Governed by the Ironroot Concord, a council of clan-holds.

**The Windmere Reaches** — Floating landmasses and sky-ship fleets. No fixed capital; identity is carried by fleet and clan, not territory. Governed loosely by the Skyward Free Fleets.

**The Duskmarch** — Perpetual twilight, ash-grey wilds, the highest concentration of active Rifts of any Realm. Home of the **Duskmarch Wardenate**, a militarized monastic order-state whose sole founding purpose is containing Rifts and Voidspawn (Section 24). Kael's home Realm — see Appendix A.

**The Solvane Dominion** — Terraced marble cities, strict border law, centralized theocratic bureaucracy. Controls the Concord mint (Section 32) and much of interrealm banking.

**The Verdant Wilds** — Living cities grown rather than built, overgrown ruins, decentralized council of hereditary groves (the Evergreen Courts).

## 8. Political Systems **[LOCKED framework, LIVING detail]**

- **The Concord of Realms** — the interrealm diplomatic body, seated at **Vaelbridge**, a neutral station built into a stable Crossing-anchor in the Aetherséa (Section 30). Formed after **the War of Ash**, an early Fourth Age interrealm conflict, to prevent Kingdoms from resolving disputes through open war.
- The Concord has no standing army; it enforces cooperation through trade sanctions, Crossing access, and guild cooperation. Powerful but fragile, and entirely dependent on continued buy-in.
- Each Kingdom, plus major Guilds, holds a seat at Vaelbridge — a built-in council-of-factions setting for political storylines.
- **Present-day tension:** rising Rift activity strains Concord authority; the Duskmarch Wardenate, bearing the brunt of incursions, is publicly arguing for a standing interrealm military the Concord was never designed to have. This is a live, unresolved political fault line — do not close it early.

## 10. Ancient Orders **[LIVING SECTION]**

Distinct from modern Guilds — these are organizations whose founding predates or immediately follows the Fracture, often carrying ceremonial or existential weight modern Guilds lack:

- **The Anvilborn Legacy** — descendants and inheritors (by training, not necessarily blood) of the original Sigilcraft inventors; more myth than institution today, but Sigil Houses still trace their authority to this lineage.
- **The Duskmarch Wardenate** — founded in the Ashfall specifically to contain Rift incursions; the oldest continuously operating order in the setting, and the closest thing the world has to a standing anti-catastrophe force. Kael is a cadet here (Appendix A).
- **The Hollow Vigil** — a secretive, semi-sanctioned watch-order (nominally under Choir Faith authority) tasked with monitoring Hollow Creed activity; morally gray, occasionally indistinguishable from the thing it monitors.
- **The Glassnight Remnant** — scattered survivors and ideological heirs of the fallen Glassnight Empire (Section 13); mostly scholars and exiles, occasionally recruited by factions seeking lost Relic-tech.

## 11. Religions **[LIVING SECTION]**

- **The Choir Faith** — mainstream religion across most Realms; venerates the Aurothi as distant, wounded creators, organized into seven regional Orders (one per Aurothi).
- **The Hollow Creed** — minority, sometimes-persecuted faith holding that the Fracture was liberation, not tragedy, and that mortals should finish freeing themselves from Aurothi influence. Deliberately written as a legitimate philosophical position, not a strawman.
- **Ancestral Echo veneration** — widespread folk practice treating local Echoes as ancestor-spirits; theologically incorrect per Choir Faith doctrine, practiced anyway.
- **Essence Purism** — non-theistic worldview, common among Vein-forgers and Ironroot scholars, treating Essence as natural law rather than divine remnant.

**[LIVING SECTION]** — Realm-specific sects, heresies, and reform movements to be developed as stories require religious conflict.

## 12. Gods **[LOCKED framework, LIVING theology]**

The Aurothi are not active, intervening gods in most of the Seven Realms — wounded, scattered, and largely silent.

- Each Aurothi still "exists" in a diffuse, fragmented sense — present in their Realm's Essence and Echoes, but not a conscious, directing will in any recognizable way.
- No Aurothi has been confirmed to communicate directly with a mortal since the Fracture. Any character claiming otherwise should be treated with narrative ambiguity — miracle, delusion, or manipulation are all valid readings, on purpose.
- **Reserved for long-term plot:** the possibility that one Aurothi is beginning to wake. Not to be spent early — finale-tier escalation material (see Studio OS Section 28).

## 15. Magic Philosophy **[LIVING SECTION]**

*The Shattered Realms* treats magic as a natural, if poorly understood, force — not a moral shortcut. Three competing philosophical framings coexist and are never resolved as "the truth":

1. **The Choir view** — Essence is the literal substance of wounded gods; using it is communion, and misuse is a kind of sacrilege.
2. **The Purist view** — Essence is simply a natural law, no different in principle from gravity or heat; framing it as divine is superstition that slows down legitimate study.
3. **The Hollow view** — Essence is a leash the Aurothi left behind, and true freedom means learning to need it less, not more.

**Design rule:** No named character should be "obviously right" about which framing is correct — their belief should say more about their culture and psychology than about objective cosmic truth.

## 16. Essence System **[LOCKED framework, LIVING mechanics]**

Essence is the raw substance the Aurothi are made of and Elysia was built from. It did not vanish in the Fracture — it diffused unevenly across the Seven Realms, taking on the "flavor" of whichever Aurothi's domain it settled near.

**The Seven Essence Aspects** (mapped to the Aurothi/Realms table in Section 3): Ember-Essence, Tide-Essence, Stone-Essence, Wind-Essence, Umbral-Essence, Radiant-Essence, Verdant-Essence.

Every person is born with a natural, usually minor, affinity to one Aspect — revealed in a childhood rite called **the Kindling** (Section 40). Strong affinity is the exception, not the rule, and is the primary gatekeeper for meaningful Path practice (Section 20). Essence can be depleted by overuse, causing **Hollowing** (Section 21), and in trace amounts is refined into currency (Section 32) and fuel (Section 38).

## 17. Sigils **[LOCKED core concept, LIVING catalog]**

Sigils are geometric marks — inked, carved, forged, or scarred — that give Essence a stable shape and function, the way a container shapes water. First developed by the Anvilborn during the Age of Elysia, Sigilcraft is the most widespread and teachable form of magic precisely because it externalizes what would otherwise require rare innate talent.

- Learned in **Sigil Houses**, guild-affiliated schools in every major city.
- Always keyed to one Essence Aspect; a caster's affinity determines which Sigils they can safely use.
- Ranked **Novice → Adept → Master → Sovereign**. Sovereign-tier Sigils are individually named and historically tracked — using one is a plot-level event.
- Can be worn (temporary), tattooed (semi-permanent), or forged into objects/architecture (permanent).

**[LIVING SECTION]** — the visual "alphabet" of Sigil marks is an art-department companion guide, not part of this text-canon document.

## 18. Echoes **[LOCKED core concept, LIVING lore]**

When the Aurothi were wounded, fragments of their consciousness scattered across their home Realms. These fragments are **Echoes**.

- A **clean Echo** is small, usually benevolent or neutral — capable of bonding with a mortal (Echo-communion) or simply lingering in a place, subtly shaping it.
- A **curdled Echo** has been isolated, damaged, or corrupted for too long — usually near an active Rift — and decays into something hostile, the origin of most Voidspawn and corrupted monsters (Section 24).
- Echoes are not ghosts of individual dead people, though folk religion often treats them that way. This is a deliberate, permanent point of cultural disagreement.
- No Echo is a full Aurothi. The theory that enough Echoes could be reassembled into a whole, restored Aurothi is real and dangerous within the fiction — held by certain factions, and directly relevant to Kael's arc (Appendix A) and the Secret Societies of Section 45.

## 19. Relics **[LOCKED core concept, LIVING catalog]**

Objects from the Age of Elysia — pre-Fracture artifacts made by the Anvilborn or, in the rarest cases, allegedly touched directly by an Aurothi. They store stable, concentrated Essence no modern craft can replicate.

- Catalogued (where known) by the Relic Wardens (Section 9), though many remain undiscovered or held privately.
- Using one requires **Relic-binding** (Section 20) — an attunement process that is often irreversible and always personally costly.
- The single most fought-over category of object in the setting; a single named Relic can anchor an entire arc.

**[LIVING SECTION]** — individual named Relics logged in a companion Relic Registry as they're introduced.

## 20. Power Progression **[LOCKED framework, LIVING milestones]**

All power derives from Essence but is accessed through four recognized **Paths**. A character specializes in one; rare "Convergent" individuals train in two — never more, a hard cap for manageable long-term power scaling.

1. **Sigilcraft** — inscribed, learned magic (Section 17). Progression: Novice → Adept → Master → Sovereign, gated by years of formal study, not talent alone.
2. **Vein-forging** — internal cultivation of Essence within the body, pioneered in the Ironroot Expanse. Progression is physical and slow — measured in "Depths" reached within one's own Essence-channels, each Depth requiring sustained discipline, not a single breakthrough.
3. **Echo-communion** — bonding with an Echo to borrow its fragment of Aurothi power. Progression is relational, not mechanical: trust and stability between communer and Echo deepen (or collapse) over time.
4. **Relic-binding** — attuning to a Relic. Progression is defined by the Relic itself, not the wielder; the ceiling is high, but so is the cost, and advancement is rare and dangerous by nature.

**Design rule:** No Path produces a usable power spike inside a single episode/chapter without a visible, proportionate cost paid first or immediately after.

---

## 21. Power Limitations **[LOCKED]**

- **The Two-Path Cap** — no character may meaningfully progress in more than two Paths simultaneously.
- **Hollowing** — overuse of Essence causes a real, feared medical condition of exhaustion; severe Hollowing can be permanent.
- **Sigil-tier gating** — a Sigil above a caster's certified tier cannot be safely used; attempts result in backlash, not just failure.
- **Echo-communion instability** — a communion bond can fray or break under emotional extremes, and a broken bond can curdle the Echo involved.
- **Relic cost** — every Relic-binding carries a specific, permanent price (a debt, a mark, a limitation) defined when the Relic is introduced — never retconned as free later.

## 31. Languages **[LIVING SECTION]**

- **Old Elysian** — the dead, formal language of the Age of Elysia; survives in Relic inscriptions, Sigil terminology, and religious liturgy (a "classical language" role). Its word for "the wholeness that was," *solenne*, is the etymological root of the age's poetic nickname (Section 3).
- **Concord Trade Speech** — the modern interrealm lingua franca.
- **Realm-tongues** — each Realm retains its own vernacular with a distinct phonetic identity (e.g., Emberfall names are hard-consonant and clipped; Tidewrought names are soft and vowel-heavy), so audiences can identify a character's origin by name alone.
- **Sigil-script** — not a spoken language but the symbolic notation underlying Sigilcraft (Section 17).

| Solmark | Gold | Property, major trade, Sigil House tuition |
| Sovereign Aurum | Platinum, Essence-locked | Interrealm banking, state-level transactions only |

Coins carry a trace Essence signature (minted by the Solvane Dominion, fitting its Order/Judgment identity), making them extremely difficult to counterfeit — successfully forging currency is itself a major plot-worthy feat.

**[LIVING SECTION]** — regional/black-market currencies (guild scrip, Windmere barter-credit, Hollow Hand favor-tokens) to be developed as needed.

## 33. Trade **[LIVING SECTION]**

Interrealm trade flows through Crossings (Section 4/30) and is taxed by the Concord, making trade policy a genuine lever of interrealm power. Notable trade goods: Emberfall essence-tempered steel, Tidewrought preserved records and cartography, Ironroot stoneglass, Windmere sky-ship components, Duskmarch Rift-wards, Solvane minted currency and legal services, Verdant Wilds living-wood construction material.

**[LIVING SECTION]** — specific trade routes and disputes to be developed as political stories require.

## 34. Culture **[LIVING SECTION]**

Culture varies sharply by Realm (Section 6) but shares cross-Realm throughlines: the Kindling rite (Section 40) as a universal coming-of-age touchstone, deep respect (varying by Realm) for pre-Fracture relics and ruins, and a shared, unresolved cultural anxiety about the reawakening Rifts. **[LIVING SECTION]** — to be filled out per-Realm as episodic and comic content requires grounded, specific cultural detail.

## 35. Architecture **[LIVING SECTION]**

Architecture should visually encode each Realm's Aspect: Emberfall's caldera-forges (heat, verticality), Tidewrought's stilted tidal cities (impermanence, adaptation), Ironroot's carved cavern-holds (permanence, mass), Windmere's floating platforms (lightness, mobility), Duskmarch's ash-grey fortress-monasteries (defensive, austere), Solvane's terraced marble (order, hierarchy visible in the skyline), Verdant Wilds' grown-not-built living cities (organic, decentralized). **[LIVING SECTION]** — full visual style guide to be developed by production design (see `mythic-forge-art-bible/`, Studio OS Section 19).

## 36. Fashion **[LIVING SECTION]**

Clothing signals Realm, class (Section 47/Studio OS registries), and Path where relevant — Sigil-marked characters often visibly display their Sigils rather than hide them, since concealment reads as suspicious in most Realms. **[LIVING SECTION]** — per-Realm fashion detail to be developed alongside visual style guide.

## 37. Food **[LIVING SECTION]**

Cuisine should reflect biome and Aspect: Emberfall favors intensely spiced, fire-prepared food; the Tidewrought relies on preserved/fermented seafare tied to its memory-culture; the Verdant Wilds' food is grown communally within living cities. **[LIVING SECTION]** — to be expanded per-Realm as needed for grounded daily-life content.

---

## 38. Technology **[LIVING SECTION]**

Baseline technology is late-medieval/early-industrial, universally supplemented by Essence-craft rather than replaced by it: **Sigil-lanterns** (common lighting), **Essence-forges** (smithing beyond fuel-alone precision), **sky-ships** (Windmere-pioneered Aetherséa travel), and an emerging, only-partially-built **Aether-rail** linking a few permanent Crossings. No firearms or industrial mass-production exist yet — a deliberate ceiling, left open for long-term escalation across many seasons.

---

## 39. Transportation **[LIVING SECTION]**

- **Crossings** — fixed or semi-fixed rifts connecting two Realms; some permanent and Concord-taxed, others unpredictable and prized by smugglers.
- **Sky-ships** — the primary vessel for Aetherséa travel and interrealm trade.
- **Aether-rail** — emerging, expensive, unevenly built; a good "the future is arriving unevenly" story element.

## 40. Education **[LIVING SECTION]**

**The Kindling** — a childhood rite (roughly ages 7–10) in which a Sigil House cleric or local priest identifies a child's Essence Aspect affinity; a formative, often loaded life event for characters, and the primary gatekeeper into Sigil House education. Vein-forging and Echo-communion training typically begin later, by choice or circumstance rather than universal rite. **[LIVING SECTION]** — Realm-specific schooling detail to be developed as needed.

## 41. Military **[LIVING SECTION]**

Each Kingdom maintains its own forces; the **Duskmarch Wardenate** is unique in being simultaneously a religious order, a monastic school, and the closest thing the world has to a standing anti-catastrophe force, purpose-built to contain Rifts and Voidspawn. The Concord itself has no standing army (Section 8) — a deliberate structural vulnerability.

## 48. Character Creation Rules **[LOCKED]**

Every new character must be assigned, at minimum:
- [ ] Home Realm and Essence affinity (Section 16)
- [ ] Path, if any (Section 20) — remember the Two-Path Cap (Section 21)
- [ ] A want, a flaw, and a cost they've already paid before the story starts
- [ ] A name following their Realm's phonetic profile (Section 31/53)
- [ ] Passed the Originality Check (Studio OS Section 5) — no resemblance to existing published characters

No character is created "overpowered" by default. Power must be earned on-page.

---

## 49. Villain Design Rules **[LOCKED]**

- Every antagonist has an internally coherent worldview — never evil for its own sake.
- Antagonists should be traceable to one of the setting's real, standing philosophical tensions (Choir vs. Purist vs. Hollow, Section 15; Concord vs. Kingdom sovereignty, Section 43; the Reassembly's theory, Section 45) rather than invented purely to be defeated.
- A villain's power follows the same Power Limitations (Section 21) as any hero — no exemptions.

---

## 50. Hero Design Rules **[LOCKED]**

- Heroes must have a visible weakness that matters mechanically, not just emotionally (see Kael, Appendix A, as the template).
- No plot armor: if a hero survives a lethal situation, the story must show *why*, in-world, before or during the moment — never after, as a retcon.
- Growth must be shown, not stated — a hero's Section 20 progression should be visible across the arc, not summarized in a line of dialogue.

---

## 51. Supporting Character Rules **[LIVING SECTION]**

- Supporting characters should have their own want in any scene they appear in, even a small one — see Studio OS Section 8.
- Avoid designing supporting characters purely as exposition delivery; if a character's only function is to explain lore, either cut the exposition or give the character an independent stake in the answer.

---

## 52. Creature Design Rules **[LIVING SECTION]**

- Every creature category (Sections 23–25) should be traceable to an Essence Aspect, a Rift/Echo origin, or ordinary biome ecology — nothing appears without a "why."
- Legendary Beasts (Section 23) are singular and rare by design; do not multiply them casually.
- Avoid silhouette overlap between creatures introduced in different Realms (see Studio OS Section 19).

## 55. World Rules **[LOCKED]**

6. The world continues existing off-page — factions act, politics shift, and Rifts advance whether or not the current protagonist is present.

## 58. Story Arc Guidelines **[LIVING SECTION]**

- An arc should resolve its own central question while opening at least one new one (never a full reset — see Section 55, Rule 5).
- Every arc should advance at least one of: a character's Power Progression (Section 20), the Concord's political stability (Section 8), or the audience's understanding of the Fracture-era mysteries (Section 60) — ideally more than one at once.
- Arcs centered on a single Realm should still reference the wider Concord/Rift situation, even briefly, to keep the world feeling continuous (Section 55, Rule 6).

## 59. Season Planning Framework **[LIVING SECTION]**

**Season Template:**
1. Define the season's central external threat (usually Rift-activity-adjacent) and its internal character question (usually tied to the season's focal character's Power Progression or Rare Condition, Section 22).
2. Rotate Realm focus deliberately — avoid spending an entire season in one Realm unless the story specifically requires it (risk of the setting feeling smaller than it is).
3. Plant at least one Section 57/60 long-term seed per season, without resolving it.
4. Close the season on both a resolution and a forward hook, consistent with Section 58.

# APPENDIX A — KAEL: FLAGSHIP PROTAGONIST PROFILE **[LOCKED premise, LIVING detail]**

**Realm of Origin:** The Duskmarch
**Institution:** Cadet, the Duskmarch Wardenate, stationed at Duskhollow (Section 29)
**Essence Affinity:** Umbral-Essence — average strength, nothing remarkable on its own
**Path:** Sigilcraft (Novice-to-Adept over the course of the first season, per Section 20's progression rules)
**Rare Condition:** Echo-Sensitivity (Section 22) — the defining, complicating trait of his life

### Childhood
Kael was born in a small Duskmarch border settlement that no longer exists. When he was young, a sudden Rift flare — one of the first in a generation to strike an inhabited area rather than open wilderness — destroyed his home. He survived; his parents did not confirmed dead, but their bodies were never recovered from the Rift-scarred ground, an ambiguity that haunts him specifically because Duskmarch culture treats unresolved deaths as spiritually unsettled. He was taken in as a ward of the Wardenate, which routinely absorbs Rift orphans into its cadet ranks — practical charity, not sentiment, and Kael has never been allowed to forget the distinction.

### Family
No living blood relatives that Kael knows of. His closest thing to family is his cadet cohort at Duskhollow and a Warden-instructor who supervised his early training — a relationship defined by duty more than warmth, at least at the story's start.

### Motivations
To find out what actually happened to his parents — not just "a Rift took them," which is the official, unsatisfying answer, but whether the flare that destroyed his settlement was ordinary or provoked. Underneath that: a need to prove that a Driftborn ward can become a real Warden, not a charity case kept around out of institutional guilt.

### Dreams
To earn a permanent place at Duskhollow — not survival-by-tolerance, but recognition. Long-term, though he wouldn't admit it yet, to matter enough that no one else's family disappears into an unanswered Rift the way his did.

### Weaknesses
- Physically and magically unremarkable by Wardenate standards — his Essence affinity is average, and his early Sigilcraft is competent, not gifted.
- Echo-Sensitivity is, at the start of the story, purely a liability: it draws Echoes (clean and curdled alike) toward him unpredictably, makes him a target of suspicion among cadets who fear it marks him as Hollow-touched, and triggers hard when he's near anything resembling the site of his parents' death.
- Struggles to trust institutions that have only ever measured his worth by usefulness.

### Strengths
- Discipline earned through years of being the outsider who had to work twice as hard to be tolerated.
- Genuine empathy, unusual in a culture that prizes stoicism — he reads people (and, eventually, Echoes) well.
- Once his Echo-Sensitivity is deliberately trained rather than suppressed, it becomes a rare communicative gift — the seed of eventual Echo-communion as a second Path (within the Two-Path Cap, Section 21), earned across seasons, not granted early.

### Personality
Quiet, watchful, dryly understated — the kind of person who deflects sympathy with a flat joke rather than admit he wants it. Not broody for its own sake; his reserve is a coping strategy, not a personality trait to be admired uncritically, and the story should let other characters call it out.

### Speech Pattern
Plain-spoken and economical, shaped by Wardenate monastic training — clipped, functional phrasing in formal settings, loosening into something warmer and more sardonic around people he trusts. Rarely volunteers information about himself unprompted.

### Physical Signature — Eyes **[LOCKED]**

Kael's eyes carry a faint, naturally cool-toned icy blue-cyan cast at all times — subtle enough at rest to read as an unusual but plausible eye color, not overtly supernatural. It intensifies into a visible glow whenever his Echo-Sensitivity activates, and the glow's character is a direct, diegetic readout of his internal state (Art Bible `color-language.md`, `lighting-system.md`):

| State | Glow |
|---|---|
| Calm proximity to an Echo | Soft, stable glow |
| Conflict or danger | Flickering, unstable glow |
| Full communion/activation | Intense, sharp glow |
| A bond fraying or breaking (Section 21) | Fragmented, unstable glow |

This is not decorative — it is the single physical tell that ties directly back to his Rare Condition, and it must never be used in a scene where his Echo-Sensitivity isn't actually active.

### Internal Conflict
Wants belonging, but fears his Echo-Sensitivity marks him as fundamentally "other" — possibly even suspect of Hollow Creed corruption in the eyes of his order. Torn between the Wardenate's institutional answer (his parents' deaths were an ordinary tragedy, full stop) and his own unresolved need to know more.

### External Conflict
- Institutional distrust: some within the Wardenate see his Echo-Sensitivity as a liability or a heresy risk, not an asset, and there is a rival cadet/faction actively arguing he should be reassigned or expelled.
- The wider metaplot: reawakening Rift activity (Section 4) personally endangers him and is intertwined with the mystery of his parents' deaths.
- Long-term (not to be resolved early): his sensitivity may eventually make him relevant to the Reassembly (Section 45) or to whether an Aurothi is waking (Section 12) — he should not be positioned as "the chosen one" who solves this alone; he is one lens on a much larger world.

### Future Character Growth
Across the first season: Novice Sigilcraft cadet who is tolerated, not trusted, learns to stop suppressing his Echo-Sensitivity and instead study it deliberately, gaining a shaky, early foothold in Echo-communion by season's end — still fully Adept-tier at best, nowhere near mastery. Across future seasons: growing Wardenate standing, a slow answer (not a full one) regarding his parents, and increasing entanglement with the setting's central mysteries (Section 60) — always earned, never granted, per Section 50's Hero Design Rules.

**Rule:** Kael must never become the most powerful character in a scene without an explicit, earned reason established on-page. His value to the story is perspective and empathy, not raw power.
