### How to use this document

This is the single source of truth for canon. Every script, comic issue, chapter, and design asset should trace back to something here. Sections marked **[LIVING SECTION]** are intentionally incomplete — they are scaffolding, not walls. Writers may propose additions to living sections through the standard canon-review process; nothing in a living section should be treated as locked until it ships in released material.

Sections marked **[LOCKED]** are foundational cosmology. Changes here ripple through everything else and require creative-director sign-off.

Do not introduce copied names, factions, magic systems, or creatures from existing published IP. If a concept feels too close to something you've seen before, flag it in review before it goes further.

## 1. Universe Overview **[LOCKED]**

*Solenne* is a shattered cosmos. Long ago, reality was whole — a single, seamless creation shaped by seven primordial beings. A cataclysm called **the Fracture** broke that unity into **Seven Great Realms**, each cast adrift in a vast, dark medium called **the Aetherséa**, connected only by rare, unstable crossings.

The series is set roughly 900 years after the Fracture, in an era the Concord calendar calls **the Fourth Age**, or colloquially **the Age of Echoes** — a period where the immediate chaos of the cataclysm has settled into fragile civilizations, but the wounds it left in reality (Rifts, Voidspawn, corrupted Echoes) are becoming active again for the first time in generations. Something that has been dormant since the Fracture is waking up. That slow reawakening is the engine of the franchise's long-term plot and should never be fully resolved — it is meant to escalate across seasons, not seasons-and-done.

**Tone:** Cinematic, emotionally grounded fantasy. Characters are shaped by loss, ambition, and belonging to fractured communities. Magic is beautiful but costly. Political and personal stakes matter as much as spectacle.

**Core Promise to the Audience:** A world that feels older than the story being told in it — every character, faction, and place has history the audience can sense even before it's explained.

## 2. History Before the Fracture **[LOCKED, with room for flashback expansion]**

Before the Fracture, the cosmos was called **the Unity of Solenne**, or simply **the Whole**. It was shaped and tended by seven primordial beings known as **the Aurothi** — not gods in the worshipped sense, but architects: vast, semi-conscious forces that had each taken responsibility for one aspect of existence.

| Aurothi | Domain | Later Realm |
|---|---|---|
| Ignareth | Flame, ambition, transformation | Emberfall |
| Maelith | Tide, memory, sorrow | The Tidewrought |
| Terrenox | Stone, endurance, permanence | The Ironroot Expanse |
| Zephyrine | Wind, freedom, change | The Windmere Reaches |
| Duskaris | Shadow, silence, death | The Duskmarch |
| Solivane | Light, judgment, order | The Solvane Dominion |
| Virenna | Growth, life, renewal | The Verdant Wilds |

Under the Aurothi, a single civilization — **the Solenne Commonwealth** — flourished for millennia. It was not utopian; the Commonwealth had castes, wars, and famines like any society, but it was whole, and its people could travel the entire cosmos on foot. Its scholars developed the first systems for working with the raw substance the Aurothi were made of — what is now called **Essence** (see Section 9) — culminating in the founding art of **Sigilcraft** (Section 10) by a guild of artisans remembered today only as **the Anvilborn**.

The Aurothi did not rule as a unified pantheon. Records (fragmentary, and disputed by modern scholars) suggest a growing disagreement among them about whether the Commonwealth should be allowed to keep advancing its Essence-craft, or whether mortals were beginning to reshape reality faster than the Aurothi could safely contain. This disagreement is widely believed to be the root cause of the Fracture, though no surviving text states this outright — it is inference, not fact, and should remain contested in-universe.

## 3. The Fracture Event **[LOCKED]**

The Fracture is not depicted on-page in full; it is mythologized, half-remembered, reconstructed from ruins and Echoes. What is agreed upon across all seven Realms:

- It happened without warning, over the course of what survivor-accounts call **"the Seven Breaths"** — seven cataclysmic pulses, one per Aurothi, in immediate succession.
- The Whole did not merely break geographically — it broke **causally**. Regions that were adjacent before the Fracture are not necessarily adjacent after it. The Seven Realms drifted apart into the Aetherséa, and the paths between them had to be rediscovered, not simply walked.
- Each Aurothi was gravely wounded, not destroyed. Their consciousness fragmented; pieces of them — **Echoes** (Section 11) — persist in the Realm that bears their name, sometimes as faint blessings, sometimes as monstrous wounds in reality called **Rifts**.
- Roughly nine in ten people living in the Commonwealth at the time of the Fracture are believed to have died in the immediate aftermath — starvation, realm-quakes, and Voidspawn incursions in the chaotic first years afterward, an era survivors named **the Ashfall**.

**Open narrative question (intentionally unresolved):** Was the Fracture an accident, a war between the Aurothi, or a deliberate act by one of them? Different cultures across the Seven Realms have different answers, and no faction in the setting should be treated as objectively correct. This ambiguity is a permanent narrative asset — do not close it out in early seasons.

## 4. Current World **[LIVING SECTION]**

The present day (Fourth Age, ~900 AF — "After Fracture") is defined by fragile stability:

- The Seven Realms are each sovereign, each home to at least one major Kingdom (Section 17) and usually several smaller states, guilds, and unclaimed territories.
- Travel between Realms is possible only via **Crossings** — naturally occurring or artificially stabilized rifts across the Aetherséa — and is expensive, regulated, and occasionally fatal.
- A loose diplomatic body, **the Concord of Realms** (Section 18), prevents the Seven Realms from lapsing into open interrealm war, but tensions are rising.
- Rifts — wounds from the original Fracture — are reawakening in numbers not seen in three generations. Voidspawn incursions, previously considered a solved problem, are increasing. This is the inciting anomaly the franchise's A-plot should track across seasons.
- Relic-hunting, once purely academic, has become a lucrative and dangerous profession as old Commonwealth ruins are being newly exposed by realm-quakes.

This section should be updated by showrunners each season to reflect ongoing status-quo shifts (new wars, fallen kingdoms, discovered Crossings). Treat it as a rolling "state of the world" memo, not fixed lore.

## 5. Seven Great Realms **[LOCKED names/identities, LIVING internal detail]**

| Realm | Aspect | Character | Signature Environment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Emberfall** | Flame / Ambition | Industrious, hierarchical, forge-cultures; the most militarily aggressive Realm | Volcanic highlands, forge-cities built into calderas |
| **The Tidewrought** | Tide / Memory | Seafaring confederation culture; obsessed with preserving the past | Archipelagos, sunken ruins, tidal cities on stilts |
| **The Ironroot Expanse** | Stone / Endurance | Deep-delving, tradition-bound, slow to change but nearly unbreakable | Vast cavern-networks and mountain holds |
| **The Windmere Reaches** | Wind / Freedom | Nomadic sky-faring clans; the least centralized Realm | Floating landmasses, sky-ship fleets |
| **The Duskmarch** | Shadow / Death | Militarized monastic culture, sworn to contain Rifts and Voidspawn | Perpetual twilight, ash-grey wilds, the highest concentration of Rifts |
| **The Solvane Dominion** | Light / Judgment | Rigid, lawful, theocratic-bureaucratic empire | Terraced marble cities, strict border law |
| **The Verdant Wilds** | Growth / Life | Decentralized courts and groves; deep-rooted oral tradition | Overgrown ruins, living cities grown rather than built |

Each Realm should support its own visual identity, faction politics, and regional monster/creature ecology (Section 15) distinct enough to sustain its own multi-season arc. **[LIVING SECTION]** — sub-regions, rival city-states, and minor Realms-within-Realms are intentionally left open for writers to develop per-season.

## 6. Geography **[LIVING SECTION]**

- **The Aetherséa:** The dark medium between Realms. Not simply "space" — it is trackless, non-Euclidean, and hostile to unprotected life. Only sky-ships, Crossing-anchors, and a handful of Relic-born vessels can traverse it safely.
- **Crossings:** Fixed or semi-fixed rifts connecting two Realms. Some are permanent and heavily trafficked (taxed by the Concord); others appear briefly and unpredictably, prized by smugglers and explorers.
- **The Scarlands:** Regions within any Realm too close to an active Rift to safely inhabit; common setting for horror/adventure content.
- **The Drift:** Free-floating fragments of land — pieces of the original Whole that broke off during the Fracture and belong to no Realm. Contested, lawless, valuable for Relic salvage.

Each Realm's specific city maps, trade routes, and regional landmarks are to be developed per-production as needed rather than exhaustively pre-mapped — this keeps geography a tool for story rather than a constraint on it.

## 8. Power System **[LOCKED framework, LIVING techniques]**

All power in *Solenne* derives from **Essence** (Section 9), but mortals access it through four recognized **Paths**. A character can specialize in one, and rare individuals ("Convergent") train in two — never more; this is a hard cap to keep power scaling manageable across a long series.

1. **Sigilcraft** — inscribed, learned magic using geometric marks (Section 10). Reliable, teachable, the most common Path. Roughly equivalent narrative role: "trained mages."
2. **Vein-forging** — internal cultivation of Essence within one's own body, developed by the Ironroot Expanse and later spread everywhere. Slower to master, produces raw physical/martial power rather than external effects. Roughly equivalent narrative role: "martial-arts cultivators."
3. **Echo-communion** — bonding with an Echo (Section 11) to borrow its fragment of an Aurothi's power. Rare, spiritually risky, and the only Path that produces effects no living Sigil-crafter could replicate. Roughly equivalent narrative role: "spirit-bound characters."
4. **Relic-binding** — attuning to a pre-Fracture Relic (Section 12) and channeling its stored Essence. Vanishingly rare simply because Relics are rare; the highest ceiling, the highest cost.

**Design rule:** Power should always cost something visible on-screen — physical toll, emotional toll, or social/political cost. No Path is "free" power. This is a permanent tone rule, not just a mechanic.

## 9. Essence System **[LOCKED framework, LIVING mechanics]**

Essence is the raw substance the Aurothi are made of and the Whole was built from. After the Fracture, it did not vanish — it diffused unevenly across the Seven Realms, taking on the "flavor" of whichever Aurothi's domain it settled near.

**The Seven Essence Aspects** map directly to the Aurothi/Realms table in Section 2:

- Ember-Essence (Emberfall)
- Tide-Essence (The Tidewrought)
- Stone-Essence (The Ironroot Expanse)
- Wind-Essence (The Windmere Reaches)
- Umbral-Essence (The Duskmarch)
- Radiant-Essence (The Solvane Dominion)
- Verdant-Essence (The Verdant Wilds)

Every person is born with a natural, usually minor, affinity to one Aspect — revealed in a childhood rite called **the Kindling** (Section 22). Strong affinity is the exception, not the rule, and is the primary gatekeeper for who can practice a Path at a meaningful level. Essence can be depleted by overuse (causing "Hollowing" — a real, feared medical condition of exhaustion) and, in trace amounts, can be refined into currency (Section 20) and fuel (Section 21).

**[LIVING SECTION]** — exact numerical/game-mechanical rules for Essence (if the franchise ever needs them for a game adaptation) should be built as a separate technical design document, not folded into this narrative bible.

## 10. Sigils **[LOCKED core concept, LIVING catalog]**

Sigils are geometric marks — inked, carved, forged, or scarred — that give Essence a stable shape and function, the way a container gives shape to water. First developed by the Anvilborn during the Age of Unity, Sigilcraft is the most widespread and teachable Path specifically because it externalizes what would otherwise require rare innate talent.

- Sigils are learned in **Sigil Houses**, guild-affiliated schools found in every major city.
- A Sigil is always keyed to one Essence Aspect; a caster's personal affinity determines which Sigils they can safely use.
- Sigils are ranked **Novice → Adept → Master → Sovereign**. Sovereign-tier Sigils are rare enough that each one in circulation is individually named and historically tracked — using one is a plot-level event, not a routine occurrence.
- Sigils can be worn (temporary chalk/ink), tattooed (semi-permanent), or forged into objects and architecture (permanent) — a Sigil-marked building might have wards, lighting, or structural reinforcement baked into its walls.

**[LIVING SECTION]** — the specific visual "alphabet" of Sigil marks should be developed by the art department as a companion visual-language guide; this bible defines what Sigils *mean*, not their exact glyph designs.

## 11. Echoes **[LOCKED core concept, LIVING lore]**

When the Aurothi were wounded in the Fracture, fragments of their consciousness scattered across their home Realms. These fragments are called **Echoes**.

- A "clean" Echo is a small, usually benevolent or neutral fragment — capable of bonding with a mortal (Echo-communion, Section 8) or simply lingering in a place, subtly shaping it (a Tidewrought harbor town that never floods, an Ironroot mine that never collapses).
- A "curdled" Echo has been isolated, damaged, or corrupted for too long — usually near an active Rift — and decays into something hostile. Curdled Echoes are the origin of most **Voidspawn** and corrupted monsters (Section 15).
- Echoes are not ghosts of individual dead people, though folk religion often treats them that way (Section 19). This distinction — Echoes as divine fragments vs. popular belief that they're ancestral spirits — is a deliberate, permanent point of in-world cultural disagreement, useful for character-level philosophical conflict.
- No Echo is a full Aurothi. The idea that enough Echoes could be reassembled into a whole, restored Aurothi is a real (and dangerous) theory held by certain factions — a strong long-term franchise hook, not to be confirmed or denied in early material.

---

## 12. Relics **[LOCKED core concept, LIVING catalog]**

Relics are objects from the Age of Unity — pre-Fracture artifacts made by the Anvilborn or, in the rarest cases, allegedly touched directly by an Aurothi. They store stable, concentrated Essence in a way no modern craft can replicate.

- Relics are catalogued (where known) by the Concord's **Relic Warden** guild-office, though many remain undiscovered or held privately.
- Using a Relic requires **Relic-binding** (Section 8) — an attunement process that is often irreversible and always personally costly (a price, a debt, a permanent physical or spiritual mark).
- Relics are the single most valuable and most fought-over category of object in the setting — worth building entire episode/issue arcs around a single Relic's recovery, ownership dispute, or awakening.

**[LIVING SECTION]** — individual named Relics should be introduced deliberately, one story arc at a time, and logged in a companion Relic Registry as they're created, rather than exhaustively listed here.

## 13. Ancient Civilizations **[LOCKED anchors, LIVING detail]**

- **The Solenne Commonwealth** — the single pre-Fracture civilization; source of nearly all "ancient ruin" content across every Realm, since its remains are now scattered across all seven.
- **The Anvilborn** — not a nation but a guild-civilization of artisans within the Commonwealth who invented Sigilcraft and crafted the first Relics. Revered/mythologized differently in each Realm today.
- **The Glassnight Empire** (~600–690 AF) — a post-Fracture empire that rose in the Solvane Dominion and briefly conquered footholds in three other Realms, attempting forced reunification of the Seven Realms using looted Relic-tech. Its collapse (a self-inflicted Rift catastrophe called **the Glassnight Ruin**) is the single most referenced cautionary historical event in the present day, frequently invoked in political rhetoric across the Concord.

**[LIVING SECTION]** — additional regional pre-Fracture and early post-Fracture cultures (one or two per Realm) should be developed as individual Realms get deeper episodic focus.

## 14. Gods **[LOCKED framework, LIVING theology]**

The Aurothi are not worshipped as active, intervening gods in most of the Seven Realms — they are wounded, scattered, and largely silent. What people actually worship varies (see Section 19), but the underlying cosmological fact (known to scholars, disputed by the faithful) is:

- Each Aurothi still "exists" in a diffuse, fragmented sense — present in their Realm's Essence and Echoes, but not a conscious, directing will in any way the faithful would recognize.
- No Aurothi has been confirmed to communicate directly with a mortal since the Fracture. Any character who claims otherwise should be treated by the narrative with ambiguity — miracle, delusion, or manipulation are all valid readings, on purpose.
- A single, dangerous exception is reserved for long-term plot: the possibility that one Aurothi is beginning to wake. This should not be spent early — it is franchise-level escalation material.

## 15. Monsters **[LIVING SECTION]**

Broad creature categories, meant to be extended per-Realm:

- **Voidspawn** — creatures born directly from Rifts; alien, hostile, without exception dangerous. The default "outside threat."
- **Curdled Echoes** — corrupted divine fragments (Section 11); often tragic in design — something that was once beautiful or benevolent, gone wrong.
- **Realm-beasts** — natural wildlife saturated with an Aspect of Essence, scaled up and altered by it; each Realm should have its own distinct bestiary (e.g., Emberfall's magma-scaled predators vs. the Tidewrought's storm-singing leviathans).
- **Sigil-constructs** — man-made guardians/golems, common in Solvane and Ironroot; a "monster" category that's really a technology/security category, useful for morally gray conflicts (is destroying one murder?).

**[LIVING SECTION]** — full bestiary to be developed per-Realm as episodic/issue needs arise.

## 16. Guilds **[LIVING SECTION]**

Cross-Realm organizations that operate independently of any single Kingdom, giving characters a reason to travel and interact across factions:

- **The Sigil Wrights' Guild** — regulates Sigil House curricula and certification.
- **The Relic Wardens** — catalog, recover, and (in theory) safeguard Relics; equal parts scholars, soldiers, and treasure hunters.
- **The Cartographers' Concord** — maps Crossings and the Aetherséa; holds outsized political power because safe travel depends on their charts.
- **The Hollow Hand** — an information-broker/smuggler network operating in the gaps between Kingdoms' laws; not purely villainous, but not trustworthy either.
- **Echo Circles** — loose, informal fellowships of Echo-communers, more spiritual order than guild, viewed with suspicion by orthodox Choir Faith clergy (Section 19).

**[LIVING SECTION]** — additional trade, mercenary, and academic guilds to be added as stories require them.

## 18. Political Structure **[LOCKED framework, LIVING politics]**

- **The Concord of Realms** — the interrealm diplomatic body, seated at **Vaelbridge**, a neutral station built into a stable Crossing-anchor in the Aetherséa. Formed after **the War of Ash** (an early Fourth Age interrealm conflict, pre-dating the present story and available for flashback/prequel use) to prevent Kingdoms from resolving disputes through open war.
- The Concord has no army of its own; it enforces cooperation through trade sanctions, Crossing access, and guild cooperation — making it powerful but fragile, and dependent on the Kingdoms and Guilds actually wanting to comply.
- Each Kingdom, plus the major Guilds, holds a seat at Vaelbridge. This gives the writers a built-in "council of factions" setting for political-thriller storylines.
- Present-day tension (see Section 4): rising Rift activity is straining the Concord's authority, as some Kingdoms (notably the Duskmarch Wardenate, who bear the brunt of Rift incursions) argue for a standing interrealm military the Concord was never designed to have.

## 19. Religions **[LIVING SECTION]**

- **The Choir Faith** — the mainstream religion across most Realms; venerates the Aurothi as distant, wounded creators, organized into seven regional Orders (one per Aurothi). Clergy often serve civic/ceremonial roles (Kindling rites, funerals).
- **The Hollow Creed** — a minority, sometimes-persecuted faith holding that the Fracture was a liberation, not a tragedy, and that mortals should finish what it started by fully freeing themselves from Aurothi influence. Morally ambiguous rather than purely villainous — a legitimate philosophical position some sympathetic characters can hold.
- **Ancestral Echo veneration** — widespread folk practice (not a formal church) of treating local Echoes as ancestor-spirits; theologically incorrect per Choir Faith doctrine, practiced anyway.
- **Essence Purism** — a non-theistic, almost scientific worldview, common among Vein-forgers and Ironroot scholars, treating Essence as natural law rather than divine remnant.

**[LIVING SECTION]** — Realm-specific sects, heresies, and reform movements to be developed as stories require religious conflict.

## 20. Currency **[LIVING SECTION]**

Interrealm trade uses a Concord-regulated currency system, since raw Essence itself is too volatile and locally-flavored to serve as universal money.

| Denomination | Metal | Approx. Value |
|---|---|---|
| Threll | Copper | Daily wages, street goods |
| Vaen | Silver | Skilled labor, trade goods |
| Solmark | Gold | Property, major trade, Sigil House tuition |
| Sovereign Aurum | Platinum, Essence-locked | Interrealm banking, state-level transactions only |

Coins are minted with a trace Essence signature (administered by Solvane-based Concord mints, fitting that Realm's Order/Judgment identity) that makes them extremely difficult to counterfeit — a nice worldbuilding detail for heist/crime storylines, since successfully forging currency is itself a major plot-worthy feat.

**[LIVING SECTION]** — regional/black-market currencies (guild scrip, Windmere barter-credit, Hollow Hand favor-tokens) to be developed as needed.

## 21. Technology **[LIVING SECTION]**

Baseline technology is late-medieval/early-industrial, universally supplemented by Essence-craft rather than replaced by it:

- **Sigil-lanterns** — common lighting, replacing open flame in most cities.
- **Essence-forges** — smithing and crafting tools that use refined Essence for heat/precision beyond what fuel alone could achieve.
- **Sky-ships** — Windmere-pioneered vessels that cross the Aetherséa using Wind-Essence and Crossing charts; the primary form of interrealm travel besides fixed Crossings.
- **Aether-rail** — an emerging, expensive, only-partially-built transit system linking a few permanent Crossings; a good "the future is arriving unevenly" story element.
- No firearms or industrial mass-production yet exist. This is a deliberate ceiling for Version 1.0 — **[LIVING SECTION]**: technological escalation (early essence-engines, printing, etc.) is intentionally left open as a long-term series development, potentially mirroring the audience's sense of "the world moving on" across many seasons.

## 22. Daily Life **[LIVING SECTION]**

- **The Kindling** — a childhood rite (roughly ages 7–10) in which a Sigil House cleric or local priest identifies a child's Essence Aspect affinity, a formative and often loaded life event for characters.
- Diet, dress, architecture, and social customs vary strongly by Realm and should be developed by production design per-Realm rather than standardized — the Seven Realms should never look or feel visually interchangeable.
- Family and community structures are generally tighter than modern audience norms — displacement (losing one's home Realm or Kingdom) is one of the setting's deepest cultural traumas, tying directly back to the Fracture and to the **Driftborn** underclass (Section 24).

**[LIVING SECTION]** — to be filled out per-Realm as episodic and comic content requires grounded, specific daily-life detail.

---

## 23. Languages **[LIVING SECTION]**

- **Old Solenne** — the dead, formal language of the Age of Unity; survives in Relic inscriptions, Sigil terminology, and religious liturgy — functions like a classical/liturgical language (a "Latin" role) for flavor text and naming conventions.
- **Concord Trade Speech** — the modern interrealm lingua franca, spoken as a second language nearly everywhere.
- **Realm-tongues** — each of the Seven Realms retains its own vernacular language(s) and naming conventions, to be developed with distinct phonetic identities per Realm (e.g., Emberfall names harder and consonant-heavy; Tidewrought names softer and vowel-heavy) so audiences can identify a character's origin by name alone.
- **Sigil-script** — not a spoken language but the symbolic notation system underlying Sigilcraft (Section 10); a visual-design responsibility more than a writing one.

**[LIVING SECTION]** — full naming-convention guides per Realm to be produced as a companion style-guide for writers.

---

## 24. Social Classes **[LIVING SECTION]**

A cross-Realm pattern (with local variation per Kingdom):

- **Sovereign-Born** — ruling bloodlines, typically strong hereditary Essence affinity.
- **Sigil-Marked** — recognized magic-practitioners; a professional class more than a bloodline, socially mobile.
- **Concord Citizens** — registered free citizens of a Kingdom, the broad middle of society.
- **Unmarked Common-born** — the majority population; no notable Essence affinity, ordinary trades and labor.
- **The Driftborn** — stateless people displaced from a home Realm or Kingdom by Rift disasters, war, or the original Fracture's long tail; a sympathetic, often-marginalized underclass with no fixed Concord citizenship, a strong source of grounded, human-stakes stories.

**[LIVING SECTION]** — should be developed with care and specificity per Kingdom; avoid treating any class as monolithic or purely decorative.

## 25. Future Expansion Ideas **[LIVING SECTION — brainstorm, not canon]**

Flagged explicitly as non-canonical seeds for later seasons/writers to develop or discard:

- An **eighth, lost Realm** — rumored, never confirmed, possibly the source of the next major cataclysm.
- Confirmation (eventually) of what actually caused the Fracture — to be revealed only after long-term audience investment, not early.
- A second, independent cosmos making first contact with Solenne through the Aetherséa — a potential universe-scale escalation for a much later season.
- One Aurothi fully reawakening — the single largest possible narrative event in the franchise; treat as a finale-tier reveal, not a mid-season twist.
- A playable/interactive adaptation (game) built on the Essence/Sigil/Path system, requiring a separate technical mechanics document distinct from this narrative bible.
- Deeper exploration of the Glassnight Empire's fall as a prequel miniseries or comic arc.
- A "second Concord" splinter movement led by Kingdoms who want a standing interrealm army — direct escalation of the Section 18 political tension.
