# THE FRACTURE PROTOCOL — PHASE 6F.0: ANIMATION PRODUCTION GOVERNANCE

**Classification:** Internal — Governance Document (**Approved**)
**Status:** Compiled 2026-07-15 per "Founder Directive — Phase 6F.0 Animation Production Governance (v1.0)." Continues directly from Phase 6E.2. This document creates no animation, modifies no canon, and rewrites no screenplay. It establishes only the new governance Phase 6F (Animation Production) requires — it does not restate the Environment (Phase 6C), Prop & Technology (Phase 6D.0), or Storyboard (Phase 6E.0) governance already Locked; those remain in force, unmodified, and are cited by reference only.
**Absolute authority:** subordinate to Master System Prompt → Locked Canon (World Bible, Character Bible, Faction Bible) → Production Bible → Approved Screenplays → Art Bible → Phase 6B Character Production line → Phase 6C Environment Production Canon and its full reconciliation lineage → Phase 6D.0/6D.1/6D.1.5 Prop & Technology Production → Phase 6E.0/6E.1/6E.2 Storyboard Production → **Phase 6F.0 Animation Production Governance**.

## 1. Purpose

Phase 6F exists to translate each Approved Storyboard Production Package's own confirmed camera, composition, and blocking rules into animated motion — the production stage after storyboarding and before final render. Phase 6F.0 establishes only the rules that govern this translation. It authorizes no animation itself.

---

## 2. Animation Authority

An animated performance is authorized only by:
1. The governing screenplay scene's own Action, Dialogue, and Performance Notes — cited directly, exactly as Phase 6E.0's Storyboard Authority already requires for storyboards.
2. That scene's Storyboard Production Package (Phase 6E.2) — its Composition Rules, Blocking Rules, and Continuity Requirements, which animation must execute, not reinterpret.
3. Each present character's own Approved Character Production Package (Phase 6B) — Expression/Pose Libraries, Animation Notes, and any character-specific Locked motion rule (e.g., Kael's eye-glow behavior, the Guardian's stillness).

Animation depicting a beat, gesture, or emotional state not present in one of these three sources is not an animation of the screenplay — it is new performance content, and is prohibited under Section 10, below. Where a screenplay's own Performance Notes are genuinely restrained or silent (several already on record explicitly requiring restraint — e.g., the Guardian's Mystery Protection Rule, Phase 6B.1; Kael's "processing, not resolution" pattern, Episode 19), animation must preserve that restraint, never fill the silence with invented business.

---

## 3. Motion Authority

Camera-relative motion (character movement within frame, entrances, exits, blocking changes across a shot) is authorized only by:
- The scene's own Action text describing physical movement.
- The governing Storyboard Production Package's own Camera Language and Blocking Rules fields.
- The Art Bible's general Lens Philosophy and camera-movement defaults (`camera-language.md`), where a storyboard's own Camera Language field is itself flagged as unconfirmed (as several Phase 6E.2 packages already disclose, e.g., FPP-SB-001's lack of a general Ward Station Seven template).

No new blocking, staging, or spatial choice may be introduced at the animation stage that the storyboard did not already establish — animation is the storyboard set into motion, not a second design pass.

---

## 4. Acting Authority

Character acting choices (posture, gesture, micro-expression, physical habit) are authorized only by:
- The scene's own Performance Notes, cited directly.
- Each character's own Approved Character Production Package (Phase 6B) — specifically the Expression/Pose Library and any Locked personal performance signature already on record (e.g., Cassian's exact five-stage schedule, Episodes 28/29/30/31/35; Mira's grounded, hands-on physicality; the Guardian's "slightly longer pause before speaking," its only permitted weight-signal per Phase 6B.1).
- Where a scene's Performance Notes describe an emotional state without describing the specific physical action that conveys it, the animator may apply ordinary, unremarkable staging consistent with the character's own established performance register — this is interpretation within confirmed bounds, not invention, exactly as Phase 6E.0's own Composition Rule already permits for storyboard composition.

No acting choice may contradict a character's own Locked physical signature, and no acting choice may reveal, hint at, or gesture toward any of the nine protected narrative facts the Guardian's Mystery Protection Rule (Phase 6B.1) enumerates.

---

## 5. Timing Rule

Shot duration, cut timing, and performance pacing must derive from:
- The scene's own Sound Notes and Music Direction, where these describe a specific timing event (e.g., Episode 7's confirmed "comm chimes... a hard cut in coverage at the comm's arrival," Episode 8's "hard cut, not a swell").
- The governing Storyboard Production Package's own Transition Rules field.
- Where no specific timing is confirmed, the Art Bible's general pacing conventions and the scene's own confirmed Emotional Lighting Mode (Grounded/Charged/Wounded, `lighting-system.md`) — a Charged-mode scene implies faster cutting than a Grounded-mode scene, as a reasonable, Locked-grounded default, not an invented rule.

Timing choices may never manufacture tension, comedy, or emphasis the screenplay's own Performance Notes do not support — several screenplays explicitly instruct against exactly this (e.g., Episode 5's "no score cue recasts Mira's account as tragedy-for-effect"; Episode 27's "nothing resolves any faction's position").

---

## 6. Facial Performance Rule

Facial animation is governed by:
- Each character's own Approved Expression/Pose Library (Phase 6B).
- The scene's own Performance Notes, where a specific facial beat is described (e.g., Episode 27's "the ease drops from his face instantly").
- The Guardian's own Locked constraint (Phase 6B.1): no facial performance may introduce expressiveness beyond its established ordinary-humanoid register, and its only permitted deviation is the confirmed pause-before-speaking weight-signal — never a new facial cue.

Facial animation may not be used to signal information the screenplay withholds — most notably, nothing in any character's facial performance may imply knowledge of, or reaction to, any of the Guardian's nine protected narrative facts, or Mira's deliberately unspecified Episode 33 mechanism (Phase 5A.2).

---

## 7. Body Mechanics Rule

Physical movement mechanics (gait, posture under strain, physical labor, combat-adjacent movement) must derive from:
- The scene's own Action text and Animation Direction section, where confirmed (e.g., Episode 19's "active manual-labor animation," Episode 2's "one character-state variant... strain/injury").
- The governing Environment Production Package's own Animation Requirements field (e.g., ENV-011's confirmed range from minimal patrol movement to significant crowd/evacuation staging).
- Each character's own Locked physical description (Phase 6B) as the bounding reference for proportion, scale, and silhouette during motion.

Where a character-state variant is confirmed (e.g., Kael's Episode 2 strain/injury state, his Episode 7 rank-marker update), body mechanics must reflect that specific, cited state — never a generic or invented physical condition.

---

## 8. Continuity Rule

Every animated sequence must remain consistent with:
- The character's own established silhouette, costume, and any confirmed Signal/schedule-based physical state, exactly as Phase 6E.0's own Continuity Rule already requires for storyboards.
- The environment's own confirmed architecture, materials, lighting register, and any Permanent Production Rule or Permanent Lighting Canon governing it.
- The immediately preceding and following shots' own established motion and pacing, so that animated sequences read as continuous within their scene and across scene transitions, per the governing Storyboard Production Package's own Transition Rules.

Any apparent inconsistency between an animated sequence and its own governing storyboard, screenplay, or character/environment package must be flagged for Founder decision under the Production Conflict Rule (Section 10, below), never silently resolved by the animator's own judgment.

---

## 9. Animation Revision Rule

Any revision to an Approved animated sequence must:
- Reopen the existing animation entry, never overwrite it silently — preserving all prior Founder Approval as historical record, per the reopen-and-append method already established throughout this production.
- Cite the specific new source that justifies the revision (a corrected screenplay, storyboard, or character/environment package, or a new Founder Directive).
- Carry its own new Founder Review, Alignment Audit, and Founder Approval before superseding the prior version.
- Never delete a prior version's record — mark it Superseded, consistent with the standing "nothing disappears without a record" practice.

---

## 10. Version Control

Every animation production document follows the same versioning discipline standing across this entire production: `v1.0` on first compilation, incremented major versions on Founder-directed content changes, changelog entries preserved in full at every version, and git commit/push performed separately for content-compilation versus Founder-approval steps wherever both occur in the same session.

---

## 11. Production Conflict Rule

If any animation work finds:
- A contradiction between two Locked Canon sources,
- A contradiction between a storyboard's own Camera/Blocking rules and the screenplay scene it depicts,
- A performance, motion, or timing choice with no citable source anywhere in Animation, Motion, or Acting Authority (Sections 2–4), or
- Any indication of repository corruption or a missing Locked authority,

production on that specific sequence stops, and the conflict is flagged for Founder decision — exactly as the Lighting Canon rule, the Phase 6D.0 Production Conflict Rule, and the Phase 6E.0 Production Conflict Rule already require in their own domains. No conflict of this kind may be silently resolved by animation-stage inference, convenience, or default.
