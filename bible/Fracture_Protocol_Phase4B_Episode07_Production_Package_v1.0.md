## 1. Episode Identity

- **Episode number:** 7
- **Episode title:** "Earned, Not Given"
- **Core purpose:** Advance Kael within the Locked Ward Division ladder; introduce Night Haven's first background presence (Phase 4A, Episode 7, Purpose).
- **Narrative objective:** Deliver the season's proposed mid-season turning point — a genuine, earned moment of confidence, positioned deliberately before the season's stakes rise (Phase 4A, Section 2, "Movement II → III").
- **Emotional objective:** Real pride, without false resolution — Kael's advancement is legitimately earned on its own institutional terms, but the audience (per the season's established pattern) should sense it tests only competence, not the flaw the season is actually about.
- **Audience experience goal:** This episode should feel like a genuine win — the season's first real one — while planting, quietly, that the ground is about to shift underneath it.

---

## 2. Story Structure

- **Opening hook:** Kael petitions for, or is notified of, an assessment for advancement within the Ward Division ladder (Cadet → Ward, Phase 1B.3, Section 8).
- **Setup:** The assessment's institutional stakes are established — a real, earned process, not a formality.
- **Rising conflict:** Institutional — Kael must prove readiness for greater responsibility through competence and judgment, in a process that does not examine whether he has learned to share the burden of it (Phase 4A, Episode 7, Central Conflict).
- **Character turning point:** The season's proposed mid-season turning point — Kael's advancement is granted, precisely at the point where the season pivots from Movement II's relationship-building into Movement III's rising pressure (Phase 4A, Section 2).
- **Major reveal or progression:** Night Haven registers, for the first time this season, as a background political presence — ambient, institutional, still non-hostile to Kael personally (Phase 4A, Episode 7, World Progression; Phase 2A.2, Section 2, item 8).
- **Climax:** Not a confrontation — the moment Kael's advancement is confirmed, a genuinely earned personal win.
- **Closing emotional beat:** A quiet character moment between Kael and Mira, trust continuing to deepen (Phase 2A.2, Section 7) — immediately undercut by word of a new, larger operation forming (Phase 4A, Episode 7, Ending).
- **Cliffhanger / Transition:** Into Movement III, the season's rising-pressure block (Phase 4A, Episode 7, Transition).

## 3. Scene Breakdown

*Five scenes proposed, sized for Studio OS's Mainline Episode runtime target. Night Haven's introduction is deliberately staged as ambient institutional texture, not a direct scene with a named representative — consistent with its Locked "background presence" scope this episode.*

**Scene 1 — The Assessment Begins**
- Purpose: Open the episode on the advancement process itself.
- Location: Ward Station Seven.
- Characters present: Kael; unnamed Ward Division evaluators.
- Emotional state: Focused, a little nervous — this matters to him.
- Conflict: Institutional — the assessment's stakes are real.
- Visual requirements: Ward Station Seven interior, reused asset.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent establishing the assessment's formal stakes — intent only.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 2 — Proving It**
- Purpose: Dramatize the assessment's actual content.
- Location: Ward Station Seven.
- Characters present: Kael; unnamed evaluators.
- Emotional state: Concentrated competence — Kael performing at his best.
- Conflict: The assessment tests leadership judgment, tactical competence, and responsibility-handling — explicitly not his willingness to share a burden, which the process has no way to measure (Section 1 above, Narrative Objective).
- Visual requirements: Same environment; no new location.
- Audio requirements: Minimal dialogue; the scene should read primarily through competent action, per Phase 2E's requirement that his flaw remain untouched by this success.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 3 — Word from the Moon**
- Purpose: Introduce Night Haven's background presence.
- Location: Ward Station Seven or an adjacent institutional space — a place where political/administrative information would ordinarily circulate.
- Characters present: Unnamed Ward Division/Light 98th personnel discussing ambient political news; Kael may be present peripherally, not as the scene's focus.
- Emotional state: Neutral, procedural — this is not a threat, just a fact entering the world's texture.
- Conflict: None direct — Night Haven is registered, not encountered.
- Visual requirements: Same environment; no new location, no Night Haven character design of any kind.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should convey Night Haven as an existing, known political entity, referenced the way real institutions reference each other — not introduced with exposition explaining what it is.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 4 — Earned**
- Purpose: Land the episode's advancement beat.
- Location: Ward Station Seven.
- Characters present: Kael; unnamed evaluators/superiors.
- Emotional state: Genuine pride — Kael's, and the people around him.
- Conflict: None — this is the episode's earned payoff.
- Visual requirements: A visible marker of advancement per Locked Ward Division convention (Phase 1B.3, Section 8) — no new rank or title is invented.
- Audio requirements: Dialogue intent should carry real warmth, distinct from Episode 3's misdirected institutional praise — this approval is for something Kael actually did well, not a reward for his flaw.
- Production needs: No new asset.

**Scene 5 — Before It Turns**
- Purpose: Close the episode on the quiet-then-undercut beat.
- Location: Ward Station Seven or Mid Zone, an informal space.
- Characters present: Kael, Mira.
- Emotional state: Warm and settled, then unsettled by the final beat.
- Conflict: None until the very end — word arrives of a new, larger operation forming, breaking the moment's calm.
- Visual requirements: Same assets as prior Kael/Mira scenes.
- Audio requirements: The scene should end on an abrupt tonal shift — quiet warmth interrupted, not escalated gradually.
- Production needs: No new asset.

## 4. Character Performance Planning

**Kael.**
- Motivations this episode: To prove himself capable of more responsibility — a genuine, earned desire, not performance for others (Character Bible, "Leadership Philosophy").
- Emotional progression: Focused nerves (Scene 1) → concentrated competence (Scene 2) → peripheral awareness (Scene 3) → genuine pride (Scene 4) → warmth, then sudden unease (Scene 5).
- Dialogue intent, not lines: in Scene 4, his response to the advancement should carry real, earned satisfaction — this is not written as a moment to undercut ironically the way Episode 3's praise was. Per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement, this success must not be written as him having learned to share his burden — it tests something real but narrower than his actual flaw.
- Body language: increasingly settled and confident through Scenes 1–4; in Scene 5, a visible shift from ease to alertness at the episode's final beat, not gradual.
- Relationship dynamics: with Mira, continued trust-deepening (Scene 5) consistent with Phase 2A.2's own pacing note calling for a quiet moment here; no other core-cast relationship is active this episode.

**Mira.**
- Motivations this episode: Presence and support for Kael's genuine achievement — an equal, established relationship beat, not a device to comment on his flaw.
- Emotional progression: Warm, present, glad for him (Scene 5 only — she does not appear in Scenes 1–4).
- Dialogue intent, not lines: her response to his advancement should be genuine and unguarded, consistent with her Locked relationship trajectory (trust deepening toward friendship this season, Phase 2C, Mira §4).
- Body language: at ease, per Phase 2E's Emotional Continuity requirement for Mira — nothing in this scene may define her by rescue or victimhood; here that means she is simply present as an equal, sharing in a good moment.
- Relationship dynamics: with Kael only, this episode.

- **Dialogue priorities:** Scenes 2 and 4 carry the assessment/advancement thread; Scene 3 is ambient/background dialogue; Scene 5 carries the episode's emotional close.
