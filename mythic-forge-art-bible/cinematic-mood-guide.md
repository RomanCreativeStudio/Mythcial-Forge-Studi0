# Cinematic Mood Guide
### Mythic Forge Art Bible v1.0

**Governs:** the emotional tone of the world, per-Realm narrative atmosphere, and storytelling-tone constraints.

---

## Brand Tone, Visualized **[LOCKED]**

The studio's brand voice (Studio OS Section 4 — earnest, cinematic, quietly confident, never ironic) translates visually as:

- **Sincerity in performance/acting:** no exaggerated cartoon takes or self-aware mugging at the camera.
- **Patient pacing in camera holds:** let a shot breathe rather than cutting away from an earned emotional beat.
- **Warmth in color even during dark beats:** avoid a fully nihilistic, desaturated-grey horror aesthetic — see the Wounded lighting mode's warm-source requirement (`lighting-system.md`).

## Per-Realm Emotional Key **[LIVING SECTION]**

Each Realm carries a distinct emotional register alongside its visual signature (Bible Section 6), to be reinforced by every department, not just color/lighting:

| Realm | Emotional Key |
|---|---|
| Emberfall | Urgency, ambition |
| The Tidewrought | Melancholy, nostalgia |
| The Ironroot Expanse | Steadiness, patience |
| The Windmere Reaches | Freedom, restlessness |
| The Duskmarch | Vigilance, quiet dread |
| The Solvane Dominion | Order, tension |
| The Verdant Wilds | Growth, wonder |

## Storytelling Tone Constraints **[LOCKED]**

- Hope must remain visually present even in the season's darkest beat — this is non-negotiable and is enforced mechanically via the Wounded lighting mode's warm-source rule.
- No sequence may tip into nihilism for its own sake; darkness in this world is always in service of an eventual, earned answer (Bible Section 55, "every mystery eventually has an explanation"), and the visual mood should never foreclose that possibility even at its bleakest.
- Villains (Series Bible Sections 7, 27) are visually treated with the same care and realism as heroes — no cheap "evil" visual shorthand (exaggerated harsh lighting used *only* on antagonists, cheap desaturation reserved *only* for their scenes). Ossic and Yselde Cray, in particular, should be lit and composed with the same sincerity as Kael.
