# Lighting System
### Mythic Forge Art Bible v2.0

**Governs:** natural light, night lighting, system-effect lighting, and the emotional lighting-mode system used across all production.

---

## Natural / Practical Light **[LOCKED]**

Ambient megacity light (sky-glow, sodium/white Authority lighting, interior fixtures) is motivated by a visible or clearly implied source. Flat, directionless ambient fill is forbidden — every lit surface should be able to point back to where its light came from.

## Night Lighting **[LOCKED]**

Default night language is a **cool ambient base** (city sky-glow, white Authority signage) contrasted against **warm practical sources** (interior lighting, worn equipment, personal devices). This warm/cool contrast is the default grammar for every night scene, across every layer of the megacity — layer identity is carried by *which* warm sources appear and how dense they are, not by breaking the underlying contrast rule.

## System-Effect Lighting **[LOCKED framework, LIVING detail]**

Cipher-work and Fragment phenomena cast light in the cyan/icy-blue register (`color-language.md`):

- System-effect light obeys physical falloff, occlusion, and shadow-casting exactly like practical light — it is never a flat, unshadowed glow laid over a scene.
- Intensity and steadiness scale with the user's skill and the cost being paid (World Bible Section 7): a strained user's glow should visibly flicker, dim, or waver. A glow at full, steady brightness reads as *effortless* — reserve that for high-tier, well-earned displays only.
- Fragment apparitions are never lit as solid character models — they are translucent, light-distorting presences that bend and dim practical light passing through them rather than emitting flat glow.

## Emotional Lighting Modes **[LOCKED]**

Three lighting modes are chosen by story beat, never by scene convenience:

| Mode | Use | Visual Language |
|---|---|---|
| **Grounded** | Everyday scenes, establishing shots | Naturalistic, motivated, low contrast |
| **Charged** | Confrontation, revelation, rising tension | Heightened contrast, strong directional key light |
| **Wounded** | Breach/Fracture-adjacent horror, loss, trauma beats | Desaturated, harsh single-source or top-down light |

**Rule:** even in a Wounded-lit scene, at least one warm light source should remain visible somewhere in frame — this is the visual expression of the brand's "hopeful" tone pillar (Studio OS Section 4) and must never be sacrificed for a fully nihilistic look.
