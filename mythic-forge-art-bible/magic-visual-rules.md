# Magic Visual Rules
### Mythic Forge Art Bible v1.0

**Governs:** allowed vs. forbidden magic visuals, effect intensity scaling, and integration with the realism baseline.

---

## Allowed **[LOCKED]**

- Sigil-glow effects following the per-Aspect color language (`color-language.md`).
- Visible strain and cost cues: caster fatigue, light dimming or wavering under exertion, the pallor and unsteadiness of Hollowing (Bible Section 21).
- Echo apparitions rendered as translucent, light-distorting presences (`lighting-system.md`) rather than solid character models.
- Environmental Essence residue (faint, localized color-tinted haze or texture) at sites of recent magical activity — a visual clue system, not a plot-explaining one.

## Forbidden **[LOCKED]**

- Cyberpunk or sci-fi visual intrusions of any kind: neon signage, holographic interfaces, LED-style light, futuristic HUD overlays. This is a hard boundary — no exceptions, regardless of how a specific Sigil or Relic effect is described narratively.
- Screen-filling, "video-game ability" particle-spam flashes.
- Any magic effect with no visible point of origin or caster.
- Any effect that reads as costless or infinite in scale.

## Intensity Scaling **[LOCKED]**

Effect scale must be proportionate to the Sigil tier or Path level being used (Bible Sections 17, 20):

| Tier/Level | Visual Scale |
|---|---|
| Novice Sigilcraft | Small, humble, easily missed if not framed deliberately |
| Adept Sigilcraft | Clearly visible, still contained and controlled-looking |
| Master Sigilcraft | Commands the frame, but still shows visible strain on the caster |
| Sovereign-tier / Relic-bound | Screen-dominating scale permitted — and even here, cost must be visible in the same shot (a caster who looks untouched by a Sovereign-tier effect is a continuity error, not a flex) |

## Integration with Realism **[LOCKED]**

Magic light and effects interact physically with their environment exactly as practical light does (`lighting-system.md`): they cast shadows, reflect off wet or metallic surfaces, and obey falloff. Magic is never a flat overlay effect divorced from the shot's actual lighting setup.
