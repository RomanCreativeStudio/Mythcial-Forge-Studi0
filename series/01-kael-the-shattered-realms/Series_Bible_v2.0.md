## 1. Series Premise **[LOCKED]**

Kael, a Driftborn cadet of the Duskmarch Wardenate, has spent his life as a tolerated outsider — taken in after a Rift flare destroyed his home settlement and, officially, killed his parents. When a new wave of Rift activity begins matching the exact signature of the incident that orphaned him, Kael's long-suppressed **Echo-Sensitivity** (Mythic Bible Section 22) starts reacting to things no one else at Duskhollow can perceive. Season One follows Kael's investigation into what actually happened the night he lost his family — and the discovery that the truth was buried, deliberately, by people he was trained to trust.

## 2. Why This Story Matters **[LOCKED]**

This is a story about earning belonging in an institution that only ever valued you for your use — and about what happens when the truth you're chasing turns out to implicate the very people who raised you. It matters because Kael's personal wound (an unresolved death, an incomplete answer) is the same shape as the world's largest wound (the Fracture, an unresolved catastrophe with no confirmed cause) — the series argues, without ever stating it outright, that healing and history work the same way: slowly, and never completely.

## 3. Themes **[LOCKED]**

| Theme | Expression in Season One |
|---|---|
| Belonging vs. usefulness | Kael must be more than a tolerated charity case to the Wardenate |
| Truth has a cost | Every step closer to the truth about his parents costs someone something real |
| Institutions protect themselves | Ossic's cover-up and Yselde Cray's manipulation both come from people who believed they were serving the greater good |
| Power is earned, not given | Kael's Sigilcraft and Echo-communion progress is slow, incomplete, and costly all season |
| Mysteries compound, they don't just resolve | Every answer this season opens a bigger question (Section 15) |
| Identity and control | What it means to become who you are when your circumstances were shaped by forces you didn't choose — the same question underlies Kael's Wardenate identity and, at a level no Season One character can see, something deeper (Section 20, "Reveal Discipline") |

## 4. Audience Experience **[LIVING SECTION]**

The audience should feel like an investigator alongside Kael — never ahead of him, rarely far behind him. Every episode should land on at least one small "click" of understanding, paired with a new open question, so viewers leave each episode with something solved and something worse to worry about. Emotional beats should land before plot beats — a viewer should feel Sella's injury (Episode 14) before they process its plot implications.

## 7. Villains **[LOCKED premises, LIVING detail]**

**Instructor Halvard Ossic** — Season One's first-layer antagonist. Not evil: a man who, as a young Warden, was manipulated into running an unauthorized Relic experiment that killed Kael's settlement, and has spent years since covering it up rather than confessing. His arc ends in Episode 16–17 with a confession and an apparent death, silencing him before he can testify — removing an easy resolution rather than providing one.

**Former High Warden Yselde Cray** — Season One's true antagonist, revealed in Episode 18. A retired, beloved institutional elder who orchestrated Ossic's original experiment and has spent decades quietly advancing the theory that reassembling enough Echoes could permanently seal the Rifts. She is not driven by malice but by a coherent (if monstrous in practice) utilitarian conviction — matching Villain Design Rules (Section 27). Her fate at the end of Season One is deliberately left as "detained, not defeated" — she remains a long-term recurring threat/ideology, not a one-season disposable.

**[LOCKED]** Neither villain may be simplified into "evil for its own sake" in any future material — see Section 27.

## 10. Season One Overview **[LOCKED]**

Season One resolves **one specific mystery** — what really happened the night Kael's settlement was destroyed, and who covered it up — while deliberately leaving open the **larger mystery** it uncovers: whether the Reassembly's theory (reassembling Echoes into a restored Aurothi) is actually possible, now partially and frighteningly demonstrated in the finale. The season is structured in four movements:

| Movement | Episodes | Function |
|---|---|---|
| I — Status Quo & First Thread | 1–5 | Establish Kael's world, allies, rival, and the first evidence of a cover-up |
| II — Investigation Deepens | 6–12 | Real costs paid, alliances tested, midpoint reveal: Concord-level involvement |
| III — Consequences Escalate | 13–17 | External danger, sympathetic villain revealed, cost paid by someone Kael loves |
| IV — Climax & New Mystery | 18–20 | True antagonist revealed, season conflict resolved, larger mystery ignited for Season Two |

**Episode 1 — "The Kindling That Wasn't"**
- **Objective:** Establish Kael's daily status as a tolerated Driftborn cadet at Duskhollow.
- **Major Conflict:** During a routine Wardenate ceremony, Kael's Echo-Sensitivity flares unexpectedly in front of the assembled cadets.
- **Character Growth:** Kael chooses silence and suppression over explaining himself — establishing his baseline flaw.
- **Big Twist:** The officiating Warden recognizes the flare's exact pattern from "before" — a reaction that shouldn't be possible for an ordinary Rift echo.
- **Cliffhanger:** Digging through routine record-filing duty, Kael finds a ledger page referencing a "Flare Incident" on the date of his parents' death — filed under a location that doesn't match the official record.

**Episode 2 — "Border Ash"**
- **Objective:** Kael's cadet team is sent on a routine Rift-ward patrol.
- **Major Conflict:** A minor, off-pattern Rift flare occurs; Kael senses an Echo no one else can perceive.
- **Character Growth:** Kael acts against orders to investigate — his first real act of initiative.
- **Big Twist:** The Echo is "clean," but terrified, and flees, leaving behind a Umbral-Essence residue stronger than anything natural.
- **Cliffhanger:** Instructor Halvard Ossic personally intercepts and redacts Kael's patrol report.

**Episode 3 — "What the Ledger Hides"**
- **Objective:** Kael and Sella investigate the redacted ledger entry.
- **Major Conflict:** Archive access is restricted above their rank; they sneak in.
- **Character Growth:** Kael extends real trust to an ally for the first time.
- **Big Twist:** The ledger shows his parents' settlement flare was reclassified from "Rift incident" to "unrelated fire" a decade ago.
- **Cliffhanger:** Reyth catches them in the archive and threatens exposure — unless Kael forfeits an upcoming trial bout in Reyth's favor.

**Episode 4 — "The Bout"**
- **Objective:** Kael decides whether to throw the trial bout.
- **Major Conflict:** Pride and duty vs. protecting the investigation.
- **Character Growth:** Kael chooses integrity, fights honestly, and loses anyway — no plot armor — but earns Reyth's grudging respect instead of leverage.
- **Big Twist:** Mid-bout, Kael's sensitivity spikes: an Echo is watching from the crowd.
- **Cliffhanger:** After the match, the Echo approaches him directly for the first time, whispering an unfamiliar name.

**Episode 5 — "The Name in the Whisper"**
- **Objective:** Kael tries to identify the whispered name — "Aris."
- **Major Conflict:** A Choir Faith clergy member warns him that unsupervised contact with a wild Echo borders on heresy.
- **Character Growth:** Kael quietly defies doctrine, driven by need rather than rebellion.
- **Big Twist:** "Aris" is the name of a Warden killed in the same incident that took Kael's parents — the Echo carries that Warden's fragmentary memory.
- **Cliffhanger:** Aris shows Kael a fractured memory-image: two figures arguing beside a Rift — one of them a much younger Halvard Ossic.

**Episode 6 — "Adept's Trial"**
- **Objective:** Kael petitions for an early Adept trial to gain Archive-level access.
- **Major Conflict:** High Warden Serath is openly skeptical of a Driftborn cadet advancing early.
- **Character Growth:** Kael must prove competence through Sigilcraft skill alone, not sensitivity.
- **Big Twist:** He passes, barely — and Serath privately logs his Echo-Sensitivity in a private file, revealing she already knows more than she's said.
- **Cliffhanger:** With his new access, Kael finds the incident files already removed — by Ossic.

**Episode 7 — "The Bond Fraying"**
- **Objective:** Kael attempts renewed contact with Aris for more answers.
- **Major Conflict:** Repeated, extractive contact begins fraying the fragile new Echo-bond, per the setting's Echo-communion instability rule.
- **Character Growth:** Kael learns communion requires patience and reciprocity, not extraction.
- **Big Twist:** Aris's memory-fragment is tied to a specific Relic used the night of the incident.
- **Cliffhanger:** Sella discovers Reyth has secretly been reporting Kael's movements to Ossic the entire time.

**Episode 8 — "Reyth's Reasons"**
- **Objective:** Kael confronts Reyth over the reports.
- **Major Conflict:** Reyth reveals his own motive: his older sibling died under Ossic's command years ago, and he's been building leverage against Ossic independently.
- **Character Growth:** Rivalry reframes into an uneasy alliance built on shared grievance.
- **Big Twist:** Ossic isn't the top of the chain — he answers to someone off-record.
- **Cliffhanger:** Sella goes missing after being seen near the restricted Relic vault.

**Episode 9 — "The Vault"**
- **Objective:** Kael and Reyth search for Sella.
- **Major Conflict:** The vault's wards nearly Hollow Kael when he trespasses.
- **Character Growth:** Kael pushes his Sigilcraft past safe limits for the first time — and pays the cost immediately (Hollowing symptoms).
- **Big Twist:** Sella is found safe, having investigated independently — she's found proof the reclassification order came from Vaelbridge itself, not just Duskhollow.
- **Cliffhanger:** A masked figure retrieves something from the vault and escapes through a hidden Crossing-shard.

**Episode 10 — "Ash and Echo"** *(Mid-season episode)*
- **Objective:** Recover from Hollowing; regroup and lay out everything learned so far.
- **Major Conflict:** The team clashes over how far to push an investigation that now implicates the Concord itself.
- **Character Growth:** Kael commits fully — the season's point of no return.
- **Big Twist:** Aris's fully recovered memory reveals the incident wasn't a natural Rift flare at all — it was a controlled Relic experiment gone wrong, and the masked figure from Episode 9 was present that night too, years younger.
- **Cliffhanger:** High Warden Serath summons Kael directly — not to punish him, but to recruit him into a sanctioned, secret inquiry.

**Episode 11 — "Sanctioned Shadows"**
- **Objective:** Kael is partially brought into the Wardenate's own internal investigation.
- **Major Conflict:** Kael must decide whether to trust an institution that has lied to him his whole life.
- **Character Growth:** Cautious, conditional cooperation rather than full trust.
- **Big Twist:** Serath suspects the Reassembly (world canon, Bible Section 45) infiltrated the original experiment — connecting Kael's personal tragedy to the setting's largest ongoing secret-society mystery.
- **Cliffhanger:** Ossic vanishes from Duskhollow overnight.

**Episode 12 — "The Archivist's Ghost"**
- **Objective:** Track Ossic's last movements through Archive records.
- **Major Conflict:** Someone deliberately laid false trails; Reyth and Kael clash over methods — force versus patience.
- **Character Growth:** Kael deliberately uses Echo-communion as an investigative tool for the first time, not just out of personal desperation.
- **Big Twist:** Aris independently locates a residue-trail no living tracker could find — proof the bond has real tactical value.
- **Cliffhanger:** The trail leads outside Duskhollow entirely, toward the lawless Drift.

**Episode 13 — "Into the Drift"**
- **Objective:** Kael, Reyth, and Sella get limited authorization to pursue Ossic into the Drift.
- **Major Conflict:** The Drift's lawlessness is the first real danger Kael has faced without Duskhollow's institutional safety net.
- **Character Growth:** Kael operates independently for the first time.
- **Big Twist:** They find an abandoned but recently used Reassembly waystation — physical proof the secret society is real.
- **Cliffhanger:** An unusually large, coordinated curdled Echo ambushes them.

**Episode 14 — "The Hollow King's Shadow"**
- **Objective:** Survive the coordinated ambush.
- **Major Conflict:** The Echo behaves like a scout for something far larger (foreshadowing the Legendary Beast known as the Hollow King, world canon Section 23).
- **Character Growth:** Kael asks Aris to calm the curdled Echo rather than simply fight it — a philosophically distinct victory: empathy over force.
- **Big Twist:** It works, partially — but the effort badly frays Aris, who nearly breaks apart entirely.
- **Cliffhanger:** Sella is wounded protecting Kael during the ambush.

**Episode 15 — "What Sella Paid"**
- **Objective:** Get Sella to safety and treatment.
- **Major Conflict:** Reyth blames Kael for pushing the investigation too far; the alliance fractures.
- **Character Growth:** Kael confronts his own pattern of pulling people he cares about into danger for his personal mystery.
- **Big Twist:** Sella, recovering, reveals she volunteered for this knowingly — she has her own undisclosed grievance against the Wardenate.
- **Cliffhanger:** They find Ossic — alive, alone, and clearly hiding from something other than the Wardenate.

**Episode 16 — "Ossic's Confession"**
- **Objective:** Confront Ossic directly.
- **Major Conflict:** Ossic is not a clean villain — he confesses he was young and manipulated by a senior Reassembly-linked Warden into running the fatal experiment, and has spent years covering it up out of guilt, not malice.
- **Character Growth:** Kael must reckon with an antagonist who is genuinely sympathetic — no easy justice.
- **Big Twist:** Ossic names his manipulator: a retired, still politically connected former High Warden.
- **Cliffhanger:** Before they can extract Ossic for testimony, the masked figure from Episode 9 reappears and apparently kills him to keep him silent.

**Episode 17 — "The Cost of Knowing"**
- **Objective:** Deal with the loss of the season's key witness.
- **Major Conflict:** Without Ossic's testimony, proving the truth becomes far harder — a real structural setback, not a convenient reveal.
- **Character Growth:** Kael and Reyth's alliance solidifies through shared loss.
- **Big Twist:** Aris, now more stable through practiced communion, retains a memory-fragment of the masked figure's face — identifying them as a currently serving, trusted figure at Duskhollow, not a random assassin.
- **Cliffhanger:** The identified figure is **Yselde Cray** — the beloved, "harmless" retired elder seen quietly in the background since Episode 1.

**Episode 18 — "The Elder's Hand"**
- **Objective:** Confirm Yselde Cray's involvement and true motive.
- **Major Conflict:** She is a beloved institutional figure — accusing her wrongly would destroy Kael's and Serath's credibility entirely.
- **Character Growth:** Kael acts on incomplete but sufficient evidence, accepting the real risk of being wrong — direct contrast to Episode 1's silence and avoidance.
- **Big Twist:** Yselde's motive isn't simple ideology — she genuinely believes reassembling enough Echoes could permanently close the Rifts forever, and considers the human cost, including Kael's parents, justified collateral.
- **Cliffhanger:** Exposed, Yselde doesn't flee — she activates something already in motion at Duskhollow itself.

**Episode 19 — "What She Started"**
- **Objective:** Stop a controlled Rift-flare experiment Yselde has set in motion at Duskhollow, using accumulated Echo-fragments.
- **Major Conflict:** Full-scale action climax; Duskhollow itself is at risk.
- **Character Growth:** Kael uses Sigilcraft and Echo-communion together for the first coordinated use of both his abilities — the season's clearest earned payoff.
- **Big Twist:** The experiment doesn't reassemble an Aurothi — but it does cause a fragment of Duskaris's Echo-network to visibly, genuinely react across the entire Realm, confirming (without resolving) that Yselde's theory might actually be possible.
- **Cliffhanger:** In the chaos, Aris is pulled into the reaction and disappears — fate unknown.

**Episode 20 — "The Shattered Answer"** *(Season Finale)*
- **Objective:** Resolve the season's central mystery — Yselde is detained, and the truth about the original incident is at least partially, officially acknowledged within the Wardenate.
- **Major Conflict:** Kael must accept that some answers — whether the Reassembly is right, what full reassembly could do, where Aris went — remain open.
- **Character Growth:** Kael ends the season with real, earned standing at Duskhollow (Adept-tier) and a fragile second-Path foothold in Echo-communion — explicitly not mastery.
- **Big Twist:** Serath reveals the Duskaris-network reaction was noticed beyond Duskhollow — a Concord envoy from Vaelbridge has already been dispatched to investigate personally.
- **Cliffhanger:** As the envoy arrives, Kael faintly senses something that feels like Aris — not gone, just elsewhere, changed — with a final suggestion that the reaction may have been felt in more than one Realm simultaneously.

## 19. Rules for Future Writers **[LOCKED]**

- Never contradict the world canon (`bible/Mythic_Bible_v3.0.md`) without a logged Canon Change (Bible Section 56).
- Never resolve a Running Mystery (Section 15) without founder/Creative Director sign-off.
- Every episode must answer one question while creating at least one new one (ideally two).
- No episode may exist purely as filler — if an episode doesn't advance plot, character, or world understanding, cut or merge it.
- Do not consult or reference the restricted True Reality Codex when writing Season One or Season Two material — see Section 20, "Reveal Discipline."

## 20. Things Never To Change **[LOCKED]**

- Kael's Two-Path Cap (Sigilcraft + Echo-communion only) — world canon Section 21.
- The requirement that all power costs something visible on-page.
- Yselde Cray's and Ossic's core motivations, once established (Section 7) — they may act, but should not retroactively become simpler or more evil than written here.
- The unresolved status of the Reassembly's theory (world canon Section 60).
- Kael's eye-glow signature (Appendix A) — cyan/icy-blue, intensity tied to Echo-Sensitivity state — is a fixed physical trait, not a costume choice, and must render identically whether he's calm, in an early Path lesson, or in the Season One finale.

**Reveal Discipline:** the restricted `bible/Fracture_Protocol_TrueReality_v1.0.md` exists for founder/Creative Director long-term planning only. No detail from it may appear in any script, storyboard, or published material before it has been earned through story and explicitly approved via the Canon Change process (Bible Section 56). This is a hard boundary, not a style preference — premature disclosure would collapse the mystery structure the entire franchise is built on.

## 21. Things Allowed To Evolve **[LIVING SECTION]**

- Specific supporting cast beyond the core six listed in Section 6.

- The order in which Running Mysteries (Section 15) are resolved, so long as none are abandoned entirely.

## 22. Storytelling Standards **[LOCKED]**

- Character choices drive plot — events do not happen "to" Kael without his own decision being the hinge.
- Every mystery must feel rewarding when solved, never arbitrary; plant clues at least one episode ahead of any reveal (Section 14).
- No episode should end in a full emotional reset — carry consequence forward (world canon Section 55, Rule 5).

## 23. Dialogue Standards **[LIVING SECTION]**

- Kael's dialogue stays plain-spoken and economical (Appendix A, Speech Style) even under stress — his emotional state should show in what he doesn't say.
- Avoid exposition-dump dialogue; if a character must explain lore, give them a personal stake in the explanation (world canon Section 51).

## 24. Pacing Standards **[LIVING SECTION]**

- 8–15 minutes per episode; every episode should contain exactly one major revelation, not more, to avoid diluting impact.
- Mid-season (Episode 10) and finale (Episode 20) episodes may run toward the upper end of the runtime window; early episodes (1–3) should run toward the lower end to establish rhythm quickly.

## 25. Cliffhanger Standards **[LOCKED]**

- Every episode must end on an unresolved question or unresolved danger — never a clean button.
- Cliffhangers should escalate either the mystery or the personal stakes, never both simultaneously more than twice per season (to avoid audience fatigue) — see the Quick-Reference Table for the season's escalation rhythm.

## 26. Flashback Rules **[LIVING SECTION]**

- Flashbacks (e.g., Aris's memory-fragments) must be sourced diegetically — Kael only sees what Aris shows him, not omniscient backstory.
- No flashback may reveal more than the audience needs for the immediate episode; save full-context flashbacks for reveal episodes (10, 16, 18).

## 27. Villain Design Rules **[LOCKED, series-specific refinement of world canon Section 49]**

- Both of this season's villains must remain internally coherent and explicable, never simplified into pure malice, per Section 7.
- A villain's power follows the same Power Limitations as any hero (world canon Section 21) — Yselde's climactic experiment costs her control, not just opposition.

## 28. Hero Design Rules **[LOCKED, series-specific refinement of world canon Section 50]**

- Kael's growth must be shown on-page across specific episodes (Sigilcraft: Episode 6; Echo-communion: Episodes 7, 12, 19) — never summarized in a line of dialogue.
- No plot armor: Episode 4's loss and Episode 9's Hollowing are required beats precisely to establish this early.

## 29. Character Consistency Rules **[LOCKED]**

- Kael must never become the most powerful person in a scene without an explicit, earned, on-page reason (world canon Appendix A).
- Reyth's shift from antagonist to ally must remain traceable to Episode 8's shared-grievance reveal in all future material — do not retroactively soften his Episode 1–7 hostility.

## 30. Emotional Storytelling Rules **[LOCKED]**

- Every major twist (Section 18) must land through a character relationship, not exposition alone — e.g., Ossic's confession (Episode 16) is delivered as a personal reckoning, not a plot briefing.
- Loss must be felt before it is explained — Sella's injury (Episode 14) and Aris's disappearance (Episode 19) should be shot/written for emotional impact first, plot consequence second.

# APPENDIX A — KAEL: FULL PROTAGONIST PROFILE

*(Expands on `bible/Mythic_Bible_v3.0.md` Appendix A with series-specific detail and the five-season growth roadmap.)*

### Biography
Kael was born in a small Duskmarch border settlement destroyed by a Rift flare in his early childhood — officially a natural catastrophe, actually (as Season One reveals) a covered-up Relic experiment. Taken in as a ward of the Duskmarch Wardenate, he grew up at Duskhollow among cadets who tolerated him without ever fully trusting him.

### Personality
Quiet, watchful, dryly self-deprecating. Reserved not because he lacks feeling but because he's learned that visible need gets you managed, not helped.

### Strengths
Discipline earned through years of having to work twice as hard to be tolerated; genuine empathy, unusual in a culture that prizes stoicism; growing skill at reading people — and, over the season, Echoes.

### Weaknesses
Physically and magically unremarkable by Wardenate standards; his Echo-Sensitivity is, for most of the season, a liability that draws suspicion and unwanted attention; struggles to trust institutions that have only ever measured his worth by usefulness.

### Core Values
Truth over comfort, even when the truth implicates people he needs to trust; earned belonging over granted charity; protecting the people who choose to stand with him, even when it costs him control of his own mystery.

### Greatest Fear
That his Echo-Sensitivity marks him as fundamentally "other" — and that pursuing the truth about his parents will cost him the one home he has left.

### Greatest Dream
To earn a permanent, respected place at Duskhollow — not survival-by-tolerance, but recognition that he belongs there on his own merits.

### Speech Style
Plain-spoken, economical, shaped by monastic Wardenate training; loosens into dry humor and warmth only around people he trusts (Sella, eventually Reyth).

### Humor
Dry, understated, often self-deflecting — a coping mechanism more than a natural lightness, and the show should let other characters gently call this out rather than treat it as purely charming.

### Habits
Tends to go quiet rather than argue when overwhelmed; obsessively re-checks Archive records once he's found a thread worth pulling; avoids physical contact with unfamiliar Echoes out of old trauma, even clean ones.

### Physical Signature — Eyes **[LOCKED, see Mythic Bible Appendix A]**

Kael's eyes carry a subtle, naturally icy blue-cyan cast, intensifying into a visible glow whenever his Echo-Sensitivity activates:

| State | Glow |
|---|---|
| Calm | Soft, stable glow |
| Conflict | Flickering, unstable glow |
| Activation (active communion or a strong Sigilcraft cast) | Intense, sharp glow |
| Breakdown (a bond fraying, Hollowing onset) | Fragmented, unstable glow |

This must appear consistently from Episode 1 onward — it is not a Season One "unlock," it has always been true of him, and its intensity simply becomes more legible to the audience (and to Kael himself) as the season progresses.

### Relationships
See Section 8 for the full relationship map — Reyth (rival to ally), Sella (closest trust), Aris (fragile communion bond), Warden Vray (earned respect), High Warden Serath (cautious alliance), Ossic (devastating sympathy).

### Internal Conflict
Wants belonging but fears his Echo-Sensitivity marks him as fundamentally other; torn between the Wardenate's official, tidy answer about his parents and his own unresolved need to know more, even at personal cost.

### External Conflict
Institutional distrust and active suppression of his investigation (Ossic, then Yselde); the wider metaplot of reawakening Rift activity; the emerging, unresolved question of whether the Reassembly's theory is true.

### Growth Across Five Seasons

| Season | Kael's State |
|---|---|
| **One** | Novice-to-Adept Sigilcraft cadet; gains a fragile, early Echo-communion foothold; earns real (if incomplete) standing at Duskhollow |
| **Two** | Investigates Aris's fate and the wider, cross-Realm implications of the Duskaris-network reaction; first travel beyond the Duskmarch |
| **Three** | Confronts the Reassembly as an ecosystem of factions, not a single enemy; Adept-to-Master Sigilcraft progress, continued Echo-communion growth |
| **Four** | Approaches Convergence-adjacent competence; the cost of real power becomes personal and immediate, testing the Two-Path Cap under narrative pressure |
| **Five** | Directly confronts the reality (or falsehood) of an Aurothi reawakening — the franchise's largest reserved escalation; Kael remains a perspective character within a larger world, never the sole chosen-one solution |

**Rule (unchanged from world canon):** Kael must never become the most powerful character in a scene without an explicit, earned reason established on-page.
